Task 2 — Learning history (discuss-both-views)

Some people say that history provides little or nothing to us, while others argue that we must learn history to understand the present. Discuss both views and give your opinion. (2023.3.11)

立场: 学习历史对理解当下不可或缺; 理由1: 历史揭示当下制度、冲突与价值观的成因; 理由2: 反对者强调当下技能更实用，但历史锻炼判断力，无法被技术替代。

Some people argue that history offers little practical value to modern citizens, while others insist that studying the past is essential if we are to understand the present. Both views contain a measure of truth, but on balance I side with those who treat history as indispensable.

Those who dismiss the subject point out that most graduates will never use historical knowledge in their daily jobs. Engineers, software developers and marketing executives solve problems with technical skills, scientific reasoning and current data, not with recollections of past treaties or revolutions. From this perspective, the time spent on history could be invested in more immediately useful disciplines such as coding or applied economics, where the financial returns on training are easier to measure and where national economies clearly reward those who master them.

Nevertheless, advocates of historical study offer a more compelling argument. History explains why today's borders, institutions and social conflicts look the way they do, and a citizen who cannot trace those origins is poorly equipped to interpret current events. Understanding the Great Depression, for instance, helps voters grasp how monetary policy affects their savings, just as knowledge of colonial rule makes contemporary debates about race and identity more intelligible. Without this perspective, the present becomes a confusing stream of headlines rather than a coherent story.

In my view, the dismissive position overlooks the deeper function of historical study, which is not to memorise names and dates but to sharpen judgement about where society is heading.

In conclusion, although technical subjects clearly have their place, history remains indispensable for anyone who wishes to understand the forces shaping the present.
