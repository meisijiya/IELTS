Task 2 — Global fashion influence (positive-negative)

Many aspects of the way people dress today are influenced by global fashion trends. How has global fashion become such a strong influence on people's lives? Do you think this is a positive or negative development? (C20T4)

立场: 解释数字媒体与名人文化扩散全球时尚，并判断整体为利弊并存; 理由1: 社交媒体与名人代言放大全球潮流影响; 理由2: 利弊并存需理性消费与文化自觉加以平衡

Global fashion trends today exert influence on almost every wardrobe, from the sneakers teenagers wear in Seoul to the dresses sold in small markets in West Africa. While this worldwide spread of style brings genuine excitement and choice, I would argue that, on the whole, the trend is a mixed one, because it both empowers ordinary consumers and quietly pressures them to keep up with cycles they did not design.

Two factors explain the strength of global fashion today. The first is the rise of social media and fast-moving trend cycles. Influencers post new outfits on Instagram and TikTok within hours of a runway show, and algorithms push these images into the feeds of millions of teenagers who then rush to imitate the look. The second factor is the global celebrity culture that surrounds music, sport and film. When a football star wears a particular jacket during a televised match, demand for that jacket soars across continents within days, a cycle that local designers struggle to match.

The benefits are genuine. Global fashion gives ordinary people access to styles that were once limited to wealthy cosmopolitans, encourages cross-cultural appreciation, and offers small designers a global platform on which to launch creative work. The drawbacks, however, are real. Constant pressure to buy the latest look fuels impulsive spending, generates enormous textile waste and pushes young people towards anxiety about their appearance. Were fashion companies and influencers to adopt more sustainable practices, the negative side of the trend could be significantly reduced.

In conclusion, global fashion has become powerful through social media and celebrity culture, and although it enriches personal style and supports creative industries, its consumer and environmental costs mean the development is mixed, calling for greater responsibility rather than rejection.
