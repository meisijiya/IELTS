Task 2 — Studying the past vs present issues (agree-disagree)

Some people think that researching the past is a waste of time and money. We should instead focus on the issues of today's world. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2026.4.11)

立场: 不同意抛弃历史; 理由1: 历史研究提供应对当代问题的经验; 理由2: 历史塑造身份认同与道德判断

Some commentators argue that academic research into the past consumes funds and effort that ought to be redirected towards the urgent problems of the present. I strongly disagree with this view, because careful study of history offers indispensable guidance for solving modern challenges and for keeping societies morally grounded.

The first reason is that history provides a vast laboratory of human experience that no contemporary dataset can replace. When policymakers debate how to respond to a financial crisis, a pandemic or a climate emergency, the most useful resources are often the documented responses to similar crises in earlier centuries, because they reveal which interventions actually worked and which made matters worse. A clear example is the way modern epidemiologists revisited the handling of the 1918 influenza when designing quarantine policies for Covid-19, saving countless lives. Without this historical grounding, decision-makers would have to experiment on living populations, repeating every costly mistake their predecessors already paid for.

The second reason is that history shapes the shared identity and moral compass that a healthy community needs. Citizens who know how their institutions were built, and at what human cost, are far more likely to defend press freedom, minority rights and the rule of law in their working life. Conversely, societies that erase or neglect their past often grow vulnerable to propaganda, because citizens lack the factual anchor that allows them to recognise manipulation. Were governments to slash historical research to fund short-term programmes, they would be mortgaging the long-term civic health of the nation for modest immediate savings.

In conclusion, far from being a waste, historical research is a wise investment in better policy and wiser citizens, and societies that treat the past as an irrelevance risk repeating its worst errors.
