Task 2 — Old buildings vs modern (agree-disagree)

Some people believe that too much money is spent on looking after and repairing old buildings. We should knock them down and build modern buildings instead. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2022.10.8)

立场: 不同意拆除老建筑; 理由1: 老建筑承载历史文化身份; 理由2: 修缮比新建更环保且更具经济效益

Some commentators argue that governments waste public funds when they preserve and repair old buildings, and that these structures should simply be demolished to make way for modern construction. I disagree with this position, because old buildings carry an intrinsic value that no freshly built tower can replicate, and keeping them in use is often both wiser and more sustainable.

The first reason is that heritage buildings anchor a city's cultural identity and provide tangible evidence of how earlier generations lived and worked. When families walk past a restored guild hall, a centuries-old temple or an art-deco cinema, they encounter a shared history that strengthens community pride and attracts tourists whose spending supports local jobs. A clear example is the restored old town of Warsaw, which has become one of Poland's most visited districts precisely because its post-war reconstruction preserved the original street pattern and façades.

The second reason is that well-planned renovation is often far cheaper and greener than demolishing and rebuilding from scratch. Reusing existing bricks, timber and foundations avoids the substantial carbon emissions of producing new concrete and steel, while skilled repair work can extend a building's life for another century at a fraction of the price. Were planners to level every historic structure in the name of modernity, they would not only destroy irreplaceable craftsmanship but also contribute directly to climate change, an outcome that no rational cost-benefit analysis can justify.

In conclusion, I still believe that spending on the upkeep of old buildings is money well invested, because it preserves cultural memory and represents a far more sustainable choice than demolition followed by modern reconstruction.
