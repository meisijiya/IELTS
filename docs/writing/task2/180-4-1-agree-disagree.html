Task 2 — Standard of living vs wildlife (agree-disagree)

Some people think that improving people's standard of living is always more important than protecting wild birds and animals. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2025.7.19)

立场: 不同意生活水平永远优先; 理由1: 生态破坏最终反噬人类福祉，二者并非零和; 理由2: 野生物种在医学、农业上具有难以预估的价值，且人类负有代际与跨物种的道德责任。

Some people argue that improving the standard of living should always take priority over the protection of wild birds and animals. While economic prosperity is undeniably important to any modern society, I firmly disagree with this absolute ranking, because the two goals are deeply interconnected and a healthy natural world is one of the foundations of human wellbeing over the long term.

The first reason is that environmental destruction eventually undermines the very standard of living it was meant to raise. Forests that regulate rainfall, wetlands that filter drinking water and pollinators that sustain agricultural yields all provide services that no amount of industrial growth can substitute cheaply. Were governments to ignore these systems in pursuit of short-term income, the long-term costs of soil erosion, contaminated water and falling harvests would fall heavily on the very communities whose welfare was supposed to improve. Protecting wildlife therefore preserves the resources on which rising living standards depend, rather than competing with them.

The second reason is that wild species carry scientific, medical and ethical value that cannot be captured by economic calculations alone. Research on sharks, for instance, has revealed immune mechanisms that are now inspiring new treatments for cancer, and countless modern crop varieties owe their resilience to wild relatives preserved in protected habitats. Beyond such practical contributions, humans hold a moral responsibility, both to future generations and to other species, to treat the natural world with restraint, and to dismiss wildlife protection as a luxury reflects an uncomfortably narrow view of what constitutes progress.

In conclusion, I still believe that improving living standards and protecting wildlife must be pursued as complementary goals, because each one ultimately strengthens the other in ways that no short-term calculation can fully capture.
