Task 2 — Parenting skills (agree-disagree)

The only way to solve the decreasing crime rate of young offenders is to teach parents better parenting skills. Do you agree or disagree? (2026.3.7)

立场: 不同意父母培训是唯一途径; 理由1: 同伴、学校、社区与社交媒体对青少年的影响同样关键; 理由2: 把全部责任推给父母不公平,且会忽略最需要帮助的家庭

Some people believe that the only way to address the problem of young offenders is to teach parents better parenting skills. I strongly disagree with this view, because adolescence is shaped by far more than the home environment, and placing the entire burden on parental training would be both unfair to families and largely ineffective in practice.

The first reason is that peer groups, schools, and the wider community exert an influence on young people that no parenting course can possibly match. A teenager who returns from a violent or drug-ridden school each afternoon will absorb attitudes and habits that contradict anything taught at home, regardless of how competent his or her parents may be. Online communities and social media now shape adolescent behaviour just as powerfully as face-to-face friendships, exposing young people to ideas and pressures that caring parents may never even see.

The second reason is that making parents solely responsible for juvenile crime risks deepening the very inequalities that drive offending in the first place. Many troubled teenagers already come from households where parents are overworked, unwell, or simply absent, and these families are the least able to attend evening classes or absorb new techniques. Were the state to focus exclusively on parenting programmes, it would quietly abandon those children most in need of additional support, while sparing schools, social services, and the police from their own share of responsibility. A more honest answer combines parenting guidance with better-funded schools, accessible youth clubs, and early intervention by mental-health professionals.

In conclusion, I still believe that parenting skills matter, but they are one piece of a much larger puzzle, and treating them as the only solution would leave too many young offenders behind.
