Task 2 — Salary-driven job choice (advantage-disadvantage)

Many people's choice of future job is based on salaries. Do the advantages outweigh disadvantages? (2025.1.4 / 2025.3.15)

立场: 利大于弊, 经济安全是生活规划根基; 理由1: 高薪带来财务安全与长期稳定; 理由2: 仅看薪资易致倦怠与错配

The question of how people should choose their future career has both supporters and critics. While many argue that salaries should be the primary factor, there are also notable drawbacks that deserve consideration before any final judgement is reached.

The advantages of prioritising salary are clear. A high income provides financial security, which in turn enables workers to support their families, pay off debts and plan for the future without constant anxiety. Furthermore, well-paid jobs often signal that a profession is in high demand, increasing job stability over time. For example, graduates who enter fields such as software engineering or finance frequently enjoy generous starting packages and long-term career prospects, allowing them to invest in housing or education much earlier than their peers in lower-paid sectors, a fact that shapes their entire working life.

Nevertheless, choosing a job solely on the basis of salary carries real risks. When pay becomes the only criterion, workers may ignore the intrinsic value of their daily tasks and end up in roles that feel empty or stressful. This mismatch can quickly erode motivation, leading to burnout, mental health problems and, in the worst cases, premature resignation. A clear example is the well-documented trend of junior bankers quitting high-paying desks after only a few years, having discovered that the long hours outweigh the financial rewards they once hoped would justify the effort.

On balance, I believe the advantages of a salary-driven choice outweigh the disadvantages, because financial security remains the foundation on which most other life plans are built. Nevertheless, the drawbacks should not be entirely dismissed, and ideally graduates should weigh income against personal fulfilment before committing to a particular path.
