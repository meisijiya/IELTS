Task 2 — Economic development vs social values (advantage-disadvantage)

Nowadays, most countries improve the standard of living through economic development. But some social values are lost as a result. Do you think the advantages of the phenomenon outweigh the disadvantages? (2023.4.22)

立场: 利大于弊，但需制度保护传统价值; 理由1: 经济发展提高物质生活水平与个人选择; 理由2: 传统社区关系受损但可由政策修复

Whether the social values that are quietly lost in the pursuit of higher living standards are a price worth paying for economic development is a difficult question that every modernising country must sooner or later face. This phenomenon brings genuine benefits but also carries meaningful costs that deserve careful consideration.

The advantages are substantial. Rising incomes allow ordinary families to afford better housing, healthcare and education, while new industries create employment that did not exist a generation ago. Economic growth also broadens personal freedom considerably, giving people the resources to travel, to study abroad and to choose lifestyles that their parents could scarcely imagine in the past. A clear example is the rapid spread of mobile phones in developing nations, which has connected millions of villagers to banking services, public health advice and distant relatives in just a few years.

The disadvantages, however, should not be dismissed. Traditional practices such as extended family meals, communal festivals and informal neighbourhood support tend to weaken as people move to cities and work longer hours for wages. This erosion of social ties can quietly increase loneliness and reduce the sense of belonging that older generations took for granted. Fortunately, these drawbacks are largely reversible: public investment in community centres, flexible working policies and cultural heritage programmes can mitigate the damage without slowing economic progress.

On balance, I believe the advantages of economic development outweigh the disadvantages, because the material gains lift millions out of poverty, and the cultural losses can be addressed through deliberate policy choices.

In conclusion, economic progress is firmly worth pursuing, but governments should pair it with deliberate measures that actively protect the social values that bind local communities together over the long term.
