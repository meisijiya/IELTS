Task 2 — Teenagers breadth vs depth (problem-solution)

Some people believe that teenagers should concentrate on all school subjects. Others believe that teenagers should focus on the subject that they are best at or that they find the most interesting. Discuss both these views and give your own opinion. (2024.9.7)

Many students today face a difficult choice between studying a wide range of school subjects and concentrating on the one they enjoy most or perform best in. This essay will discuss the main reasons behind this problem and suggest practical ways schools and parents can help teenagers make a balanced decision.

The problem has two main causes. First, external pressure is intense. Teenagers are told by parents, teachers and university admissions officers that top grades in a narrow set of subjects will guarantee a place at a good university, so many students abandon subjects they enjoy in order to focus on the few that are seen as useful. Second, students themselves often lack the self-awareness needed to judge their true strengths. At fifteen or sixteen, most teenagers have only experienced a small slice of the curriculum and therefore have little evidence on which to base such an important decision.

There are several practical solutions. Schools can introduce regular one-to-one counselling sessions in which a tutor helps each student review his or her grades, interests and longer-term goals, then maps out a personal subject combination that balances core requirements with chosen strengths. Parents can be encouraged to listen to their child's own preferences rather than imposing their own career ambitions, and universities can signal that breadth is valued by giving credit for a wider range of A-level or equivalent subjects when making offers. Together, these three measures can ease the pressure on individual teenagers without lowering academic standards.

In conclusion, the difficulty teenagers face in choosing between breadth and depth comes mainly from external pressure and limited self-knowledge. With better school guidance and more open parental support, this problem can be managed effectively.
