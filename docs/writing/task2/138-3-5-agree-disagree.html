Task 2 — Health as a duty to society (agree-disagree)

People should look after their health as a duty to the society where they live, rather than their own benefits. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2021.5.15)

立场: 不同意将健康定义为对社会的义务; 理由1: 健康本质上是个人自主权; 理由2: 将健康义务化会滑向过度干预与污名化

Some people argue that looking after one's health should be regarded primarily as a duty owed to society, not as a matter of personal benefit. I firmly disagree with this view, because framing health as a civic obligation misrepresents its nature and risks turning medical care into a tool of social control.

The first reason is that health is essentially a dimension of personal autonomy. Each individual has a unique body, a unique set of beliefs and a unique tolerance for risk, and forcing everyone to follow the same diet, exercise routine or sleep schedule would erode the freedom on which liberal societies are built. A clear example is the case of smokers or extreme sports enthusiasts: society may rightly warn them of the dangers, yet it cannot legitimately dictate their private choices without sliding into paternalism. Treating health as a personal project, by contrast, encourages informed self-management rather than passive obedience to official guidelines, and it allows people to weigh their own trade-offs openly.

The second reason is that redefining health as a social duty can easily deteriorate into stigma and over-intervention. Once governments begin to penalise unhealthy lifestyles through higher insurance premiums or restricted employment opportunities, citizens with chronic conditions or disabilities would face quiet discrimination, even though their conditions may be largely outside their control. Were a so-called health duty to be enforced too rigidly, the line between public welfare and intrusive surveillance would become dangerously thin.

In conclusion, I still believe that health is a deeply personal responsibility, and society is better served by encouraging healthy habits than by trying to convert them into a legal obligation.
