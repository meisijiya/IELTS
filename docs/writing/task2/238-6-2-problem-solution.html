Task 2 — Consumers less influenced by advertising (problem-solution)

Research indicates that nowadays some consumers are much less influenced by advertising than in the past. What do you think are the reasons for this? Do you think this is a positive or negative development? (2026.1.10)

立场: 倾向积极（消费者更理性）; 理由1: 信息透明与社交评论削弱广告权威; 理由2: 平台与品牌通过透明沟通与媒体素养教育建立新信任

The aim of this essay is to examine why today's consumers appear less influenced by advertising than earlier generations, and to consider whether this shift is a positive or negative development for markets and society.

There are two main reasons for the change. First, information has become far more transparent. Online reviews, comparison websites and independent blogs allow shoppers to evaluate a product's real quality within minutes, often before they ever see the brand's own advert. Second, social media has turned ordinary customers into trusted commentators whose negative experiences can reach thousands of followers in a single afternoon. A clear example is the way a single viral post exposing misleading claims can destroy a campaign that cost millions to produce, something that would have been unimaginable in the era of one-way broadcast television, when viewers had little opportunity to challenge the slogans they were shown.

There are several practical solutions that brands, platforms and educators can adopt to keep the relationship healthy. Companies can invest in transparent communication, publish independent test results and respond openly to criticism rather than hiding behind polished slogans. Independent platforms can strengthen verification systems so that reviewers' identities and purchase histories are easier to confirm, while schools can incorporate media literacy into curricula that teach young people how advertising tries to shape their everyday choices. Together, these measures protect sceptical consumers without silencing legitimate commercial speech and reward brands that invest in lasting quality.

In conclusion, the declining influence of advertising reflects a more informed public, and this trend is largely positive because it pushes brands to compete on quality and honesty rather than on psychological tricks.
