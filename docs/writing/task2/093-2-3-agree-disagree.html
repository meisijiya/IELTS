Task 2 — Working from home (agree-disagree)

Many employees can now do their work from home using modern technology. However, this change may only benefit workers, not the employers. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2024.7.27)

立场: 不同意; 理由1: 雇主可降低办公成本并提升产出; 理由2: 远程推动人才储备与组织韧性

It is sometimes argued that the rise of remote work, made possible by modern communication technology, advantages employees alone while leaving employers with little to gain. I disagree with this view, because companies that allow their staff to work from home can also reduce their costs, widen their talent pools and build a more resilient organisation over the long term.

The first reason is that employers can cut significant overheads when portions of their workforce operate from home. By reducing the size of office premises, companies spend far less on rent, utilities and on-site facilities, and those savings can be redirected into research, training or product development that would otherwise be hard to fund. A clear example can be seen in several large international firms that have shrunk their headquarters and reinvested the savings into digital infrastructure, which has measurably improved their productivity. The second reason is that remote work allows employers to recruit the best candidates regardless of where they live, a benefit that a strict office-based policy would deny them entirely. When companies restrict themselves to a single city, they limit their options to a small pool of local talent, often driving up salaries at the same time and missing out on specialists who could transform their products. Were senior managers to abandon remote arrangements entirely, they would lose both the cost savings and the broader talent base that flexible work has given them in recent years.

In conclusion, I am convinced that remote work benefits employers as much as it does employees, because it lowers costs, expands talent pools and strengthens the long-term resilience of the company.
