Task 2 — Youth spending less time outdoors (problem-solution)

Young people do not spend their holiday and weekend doing outdoor activities like hiking and climbing in the natural environment. Why? And how to encourage them to go out? (2021.4.17)

立场: 揭示屏幕娱乐与户外设施不足导致青少年远离自然，并提出家庭与社区组织方案; 理由1: 数字娱乐与社交媒体占据空闲时间; 理由2: 户外俱乐部与学校项目可重新吸引青少年

The aim of this essay is to examine why so many young people today prefer to spend their holidays and weekends indoors rather than hiking in natural surroundings, and to consider what families, schools and governments can do to draw them back outside.

Several forces push young people away from nature. The most powerful is the lure of digital entertainment: smartphones, online games and short-video platforms offer a stream of stimulation that demands no physical effort and certainly no mosquito bites. Social media has compounded the problem, since young users now measure leisure by likes rather than by the view from a hilltop. Beyond screens, outdoor logistics deter them: hiking routes are usually far from home, transport is inconvenient, and parents worry about safety in remote areas. School schedules crammed with tutoring leave little genuine free time for a child to organise an outing.

Encouraging a return to nature calls for a mix of family, school and community action. Schools could organise regular weekend trips to nearby national parks, framing hiking as an enjoyable social activity rather than an exhausting chore. Local councils might subsidise transport to popular trails and equip them with safe, well-marked routes suitable for beginners. Families could set a simple rule that one weekend outing a month involves walking in a green space, and could invite friends along so the trip feels less like an isolated ritual. Outdoor clubs that pair novices with experienced guides would also help anxious beginners gain confidence quickly.

In conclusion, young people stay indoors because screens are easier than trails, and convincing them to go outside will require organised school trips, safer local routes and family habits that make the natural world feel both accessible and genuinely fun.
