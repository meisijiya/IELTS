Task 2 — University study harder than school (problem-solution)

Many students find it harder to study when they are at the university or college than when they were at school. Why is this? What can be done to solve the problem? (2024.10.19)

Many students today report that studying at university feels harder than learning at school, even though they did well in earlier studies. This essay will discuss the main reasons behind this problem and suggest practical ways in which universities and students themselves can solve it.

There are two main reasons why the transition feels so difficult. First, the way knowledge is organised changes sharply. At school, subjects are broken into short lessons with frequent tests, so students always know exactly what to revise. At university, a single course may cover the work of an entire school term in a few weeks, and students are expected to read widely and form their own conclusions. Second, the social environment is much less structured. Teenagers often move to a new city, live with strangers and manage their own time for the first time, and the absence of a fixed timetable makes it easy for study to slip behind sleep, part-time work or social life.

There are several practical solutions. Universities can run short bridge courses in the first semester that teach not only subject content but also how to take notes from lectures, plan weekly reading and write essays at university level. They can also assign each new student a personal tutor who checks progress every two weeks. Students themselves can help by forming small study groups, keeping a simple weekly timetable, and treating their degree as a full-time job rather than a few contact hours a week.

In conclusion, the difficulty students feel at university comes mainly from a sudden change in teaching style and daily structure. With better university support and more disciplined personal habits, this problem can be managed effectively.
