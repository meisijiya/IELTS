Task 2 — New buildings in traditional style (agree-disagree)

The government should protect culture. Therefore, some people believe that new buildings should be built in traditional styles. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2026.1.24)

立场: 有限度同意; 理由1: 历史街区新建筑宜延续传统风格; 理由2: 但需允许城市新区发展现代建筑

It is sometimes argued that, in order to protect national culture, governments should require all new buildings to be designed in traditional architectural styles. I only partially agree with this view, because traditional design is appropriate in some contexts but unnecessarily restrictive in others.

The first reason I lean towards agreement is that traditional styles play an essential role in preserving the visual character of historic districts. When new apartment blocks or shops in a centuries-old town centre are forced to mimic local roof lines, materials and colour palettes, the area keeps its charm and the cultural memory embedded in its skyline is passed on to the next generation. A clear example is the strict design code enforced in the old town of Dubrovnik, where new structures must follow traditional stone-and-tile patterns so that the UNESCO-listed skyline remains intact for visitors and residents alike.

The second reason I hesitate to endorse a blanket rule is that contemporary architecture is itself a form of cultural expression, and cities need room to grow in new directions. Were every new hospital, school or railway station forced to imitate the past, they might become less functional, more expensive and unable to express the values of the present era. A growing city such as Rotterdam, for instance, deliberately blends restored historic buildings with bold modern landmarks like the Markthal, showing that tradition and innovation can strengthen each other rather than conflict.

In conclusion, I still believe that new buildings should follow traditional styles only in historically sensitive areas, while the wider city should be free to develop contemporary designs that reflect today's needs and aspirations.
