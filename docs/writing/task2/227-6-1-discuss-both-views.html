Task 2 — Printed books vs digital (discuss-both-views)

Some people think that printed books are no longer needed in the digital era, because all writing can be stored electronically. Others think printed books will still play an important role. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (2023.10.28)

立场: 折中（数字存储主导但印刷仍重要）; 理由1: 数字存储便宜、高效、便于检索与分享; 理由2: 印刷书提供沉浸式阅读与可靠的长期保存

The question of whether printed books remain necessary in an age of universal electronic storage continues to divide readers and publishers. Some argue that digital formats are cheaper and more flexible than ever, while others maintain that paper editions still perform essential roles that screens cannot fully replicate.

Those who consider printed books obsolete point out that e-books and online articles have dramatically reduced the cost of distributing text. A single library database now provides access to millions of titles that previously required an entire warehouse to store, and readers can search across thousands of pages in seconds and carry a personal library on a device that weighs less than a single hardback. For publishers, eliminating print runs also reduces paper waste and the carbon footprint of transportation, which is an environmental benefit that printed books struggle to match.

Advocates of printed books, however, emphasise that paper reading remains uniquely immersive. Many readers find that long passages on a screen invite distraction, while a printed volume encourages deeper concentration. Paper editions also survive changes in software, whereas digital archives can become inaccessible when a distributor goes out of business. A clear example is the way many prestigious literary prizes still consider only printed submissions, signalling that the physical book retains cultural weight no algorithm can confer.

In my view, neither format is sufficient on its own. The most thoughtful approach treats digital storage as the backbone of routine reading while preserving printed editions for works that deserve to be treasured over a lifetime.

In conclusion, digital storage has rightly taken over much of the everyday work of reading, yet printed books continue to provide immersive reading experiences and dependable preservation that screens cannot fully replace.
