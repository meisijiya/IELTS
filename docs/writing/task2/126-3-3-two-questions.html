Task 2 — Mobile and internet for elderly (two-questions)

Mobile phones and the internet could have many benefits for old people. However, this age group uses technology the least. What are the benefits for old people of using mobile phones and the internet? How can we encourage them to use this new technology? (2024.5.18)

立场: 价值显著，需要针对性推广; 理由1: 数字工具帮助老人保持联系、便捷办事并辅助健康管理; 理由2: 简化设计、代际互助与社区课程可以显著提升使用率

The aim of this essay is to outline the main benefits that mobile phones and the internet can bring to older people, and to suggest practical ways to encourage greater use of these technologies among them.

The benefits for older adults fall into three broad categories. First, mobile phones and social apps help them stay in close touch with distant family members, reducing the loneliness that frequently accompanies retirement or the loss of a spouse. Second, online services allow them to handle banking, shopping and government appointments without travelling long distances or standing in queues, which is a real advantage for those with limited mobility. Third, wearable devices and health-monitoring apps can track heart rate, blood pressure and medication schedules, providing early warnings of medical problems and supporting independent living at home.

To encourage wider adoption, governments and families must tackle the practical barriers that hold older users back. Device manufacturers can design phones with larger icons, simpler menus and clear privacy defaults, while telecoms companies can offer discounted data plans tailored to light users. Equally important is human support: community centres can run free, patient classes where younger volunteers teach basic digital skills one-on-one, and adult children can set aside time during family visits to walk their parents through new apps at a comfortable pace. Where these efforts are coordinated, older people quickly gain confidence and continue using technology on their own for many years.

In conclusion, mobile phones and the internet offer older people genuine gains in connection, convenience and health, and a combination of age-friendly design and patient instruction can bring these benefits within reach of far more seniors than today.
