Task 2 — Visual images accuracy (discuss-both-views)

Some people think that visual images (like photographs and videos) can tell information more accurately in a news story. Others think they are not reliable sources. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (2022.8.6)

立场: 折中（视觉证据有强说服力但易被操控）; 理由1: 图像与视频可记录瞬间现场，比纯文字更直观; 理由2: 裁剪、滤镜与深度伪造使图像易失真，需要专业核查

Whether photographs and video clips can communicate information more accurately than words in a news story is a question that divides editors and readers alike. Some argue that a single picture carries a truth no paragraph can match, while others insist that visual material is easily manipulated and therefore unreliable.

Those who value visual evidence point out that a sharp photograph or an unedited clip can freeze a moment in time with extraordinary precision. Witness footage of a natural disaster, for example, allows audiences thousands of miles away to gauge the scale of damage more convincingly than any written description. Visual evidence also crosses language barriers, which makes it indispensable in breaking news where speed matters, and it can preserve details that an eyewitness might forget to mention.

Those who doubt the reliability of images counter that a photograph is only as honest as the person who frames it. Cropping, selective angles and lighting tricks have been used for more than a century to put a misleading spin on otherwise ordinary scenes, and modern editing software now makes such manipulation almost effortless. A clear example is the rise of deepfake videos, in which artificial intelligence produces footage of events that never actually happened. Without professional verification, viewers may accept fabricated images as genuine and form judgements that have no basis in fact.

In my view, neither side is wholly correct. Photographs and videos are powerful evidence, but only when they have been authenticated by trained journalists who can confirm their origin, date and context.

In conclusion, visual material can tell a news story more vividly than words alone, yet its accuracy depends on professional verification, so the most trustworthy reporting combines both visual and written evidence under strict editorial standards.
