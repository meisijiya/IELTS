Task 2 — Employment skills vs academic study

Some people believe that university education should focus on skills of employment for the future. Others think they should focus on academic study only. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (2021.6.12)

立场: 折中（学术核心 + 实用元素）; 理由1: 实用课程缩短课堂与职场的距离; 理由2: 学术通识塑造批判思维与终身学习力

The question of whether universities should concentrate on employment-related skills or remain devoted to pure academic study continues to provoke debate. Some argue that higher education must prepare graduates for the labour market, while others believe that universities should preserve their traditional role as centres of intellectual exploration.

Those who favour an employment-focused curriculum point out that most students enrol in university with the practical goal of securing a stable career. Graduates who have only studied abstract theory often struggle in the workplace, where employers usually expect candidates to demonstrate teamwork, communication and basic technical literacy. By integrating internships and case studies, universities can mitigate the gap between campus learning and real working life. A business student who has managed a mock marketing campaign during college adapts to a junior marketing role far faster than a peer who has only memorised textbook models.

Advocates of pure academic study, however, emphasise that universities are first and foremost places of intellectual exploration. Subjects such as philosophy, literature and theoretical physics may not lead directly to a specific job, yet they cultivate critical thinking, independent reasoning and a broad understanding of human knowledge, qualities that any profession ultimately relies on. Were higher education to become too vocational, students would lose the chance to discover interests outside the obvious career path, which can actually limit their long-term personal growth.

In my view, neither approach is sufficient on its own. Universities should keep a strong academic core while introducing employment-related components, so that students graduate with both the ability to think independently and the practical competence required by the modern labour market.

In conclusion, a balanced model that respects academic depth without ignoring practical needs offers the most compelling path forward for students and society.
