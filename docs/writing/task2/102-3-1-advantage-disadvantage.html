Task 2 — Out-of-town supermarkets (advantage-disadvantage)

In some countries, some small town-center shops are going out of business because customers are shopping at large out-of-town stores. As a result, some people without cars have limited access to shops, and there may be an increase in the use of cars. Do the disadvantages outweigh the advantages? (2022.9.17)

立场: 缺点大于优点; 理由1: 大型店价低选择多; 理由2: 但无车群体被排除且加剧汽车依赖

The shift of consumer spending from small town-centre shops towards large out-of-town stores is a familiar pattern in many developed economies around the world. While the rise of these big-box retailers brings clear savings for shoppers, I believe the disadvantages for society as a whole ultimately outweigh the advantages.

The main advantage is that out-of-town stores can offer lower prices and a much wider selection of goods than traditional corner shops ever could. Their bulk purchasing power and efficient logistics cut costs that are passed on to customers, and many families genuinely appreciate being able to buy a full week's groceries under one roof in a single trip. In countries such as Germany and the United States, large discount retailers have become an everyday part of household life, and millions of consumers rely on them for affordable basics and predictable quality.

The disadvantages, however, are both social and environmental, and they tend to affect the most vulnerable groups in society first. Once small town-centre shops close, elderly residents, low-income families and people without cars lose their convenient access to fresh food and daily necessities, while those who do drive steadily increase their reliance on private vehicles, adding to congestion and carbon emissions across the region. A clear example is the gradual disappearance of neighbourhood grocers in British market towns, where surveys show that non-drivers now travel further and spend significantly more to reach the nearest large supermarket.

On balance, I believe the disadvantages of out-of-town retail expansion outweigh the advantages, because the harms fall disproportionately on people without cars and on the wider environment, while the benefits are largely captured by shoppers who already own vehicles and can easily reach the new stores.
