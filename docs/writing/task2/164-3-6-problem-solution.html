Task 2 — High-schoolers lack money skills (problem-solution)

In many countries, students who leave high school have no understanding of how to manage their money. Why is this case? What can be done to improve students' understanding of how to manage personal finance? (2024.3.25)

立场: 揭示学校课程缺失与家庭回避导致理财空白，并提出必修课与生活实践方案; 理由1: 学校无系统理财课且家长刻意回避金钱话题; 理由2: 必修理财课与模拟账户可补齐短板

The aim of this essay is to examine why so many young people reach adulthood without a basic grasp of personal finance, and to suggest practical measures that could give every graduate a confident start with money.

Two related factors explain this widespread shortfall. The first is the curriculum itself: most secondary schools still treat mathematics as the study of algebra and geometry, with little attention to budgeting, credit, tax or saving. Pupils can solve abstract equations yet cannot prepare a simple monthly budget or compare bank accounts. The second factor is the culture at home. Many parents consider money a sensitive topic and deliberately avoid discussing household finances with their children, leaving young people to absorb consumer habits from advertising and social media rather than from clear family guidance. The result is a generation that can navigate an online shop without understanding how an overdraft or a compound interest rate actually works.

The remedy should combine classroom teaching with real-life practice. Education ministries should require a dedicated personal-finance module at upper-secondary level, covering budgeting, debt, insurance, digital payments and long-term saving, with regular assessments that include real-world case studies. Banks and regulators could partner with schools to offer supervised student bank accounts and simulation apps that let teenagers try investing small sums and immediately see the consequences of risky or cautious choices. Parents, for their part, can include older children in family discussions about bills, mortgages and savings goals, normalising open conversation about money at the kitchen table.

In conclusion, students leave school financially illiterate because formal curricula ignore the topic and families rarely discuss it, and the solution lies in mandatory finance lessons at school, supervised practice with real tools and franker conversations at home.
