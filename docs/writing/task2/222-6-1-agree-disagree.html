Task 2 — Libraries no longer necessary (agree-disagree)

Students easily access information online, so libraries are no longer necessary. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2022.2.26)

立场: 不同意取消图书馆; 理由1: 图书馆提供可靠的馆藏、安静学习空间与专业馆员指导; 理由2: 数字鸿沟使低收入家庭仍依赖实体图书馆

Some commentators argue that, because students can now retrieve almost any piece of information through search engines, public and university libraries have lost their purpose. I firmly disagree with this view: although the internet has transformed how research is conducted, libraries continue to perform essential functions that no website can fully replace.

The first reason is that libraries offer curated collections, quiet study environments and professional guidance that online resources seldom match. A good academic librarian helps students distinguish peer-reviewed scholarship from predatory journals, a judgement that even experienced researchers struggle to make unaided. Library reading rooms also provide a focused atmosphere, free from distracting notifications, that many students find indispensable when preparing for examinations or working on lengthy dissertations. A clear example is the way revision periods at major universities see packed reading rooms even though the same texts are technically available with a click at home.

The second reason is that physical libraries remain crucial for communities that the digital revolution has left behind. Households without reliable broadband, students who cannot afford expensive textbooks and elderly readers unfamiliar with tablet devices still rely on their local branch as a primary gateway to knowledge. Were governments to close libraries on the assumption that the internet is enough, they would quietly widen the existing gap between rich and poor students, undermining the social mobility that public education is meant to deliver and putting a generation of disadvantaged readers at risk of falling further behind.

In conclusion, I still believe that libraries deserve continued public investment, because they offer expert guidance, focused study space and equitable access that online information alone cannot provide for everyone.
