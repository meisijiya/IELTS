Task 2 — Unpaid community work (advantage-disadvantage)

Some people suggest that young adults should be encouraged to do some unpaid work for a period of time for the community to help people. Does it bring more benefits or drawbacks to the community and the young people? (2023.9.9)

立场: 利大于弊; 理由1: 培养年轻人的公民责任感与同理心，社区获得额外劳动力; 理由2: 但低收入家庭负担重、可能挤压有偿岗位，需有保障机制。

It is widely suggested that young adults should devote a period of their time to unpaid community work to help those in need. While there are clearly some drawbacks, on balance I believe the benefits to both society and the young people themselves outweigh them.

The principal advantage of such a scheme is that it nurtures a sense of civic responsibility among young adults who might otherwise enter the workforce with little awareness of the world beyond their own interests. By serving in homeless shelters, hospitals or environmental projects, volunteers gradually develop empathy and an understanding of social problems that no classroom lecture can really provide. The community, for its part, gains access to additional labour for tasks that government funding often cannot cover, ranging from neighbourhood clean-ups to elderly companionship programmes. Were this practice to become widespread, social cohesion would almost certainly strengthen over time.

Nevertheless, the drawbacks should not be entirely dismissed. The most serious concern is that compulsory unpaid labour places an unfair financial burden on young people from low-income families, who cannot easily forgo several months of paid employment. There is also a real risk that organisations may exploit the availability of free volunteers to displace paid positions, thereby depressing wages in the social sector. Were such displacement to occur on a large scale, the policy would harm precisely those workers it claims to help.

In conclusion, although unpaid community work carries genuine risks, its long-term benefits to both young volunteers and society at large are more substantial and more widely shared. Were governments to introduce this scheme with proper safeguards for low-income participants, the advantages would clearly outweigh the disadvantages.
