Task 2 — Arts funding (discuss-both-views)

Some people think the government should support artists of all kinds, such as painters, musicians, and poets, while others believe this is a waste of money. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (2023.5.6)

立场: 适度支持艺术家; 理由1: 公共艺术塑造文化身份; 理由2: 资金应优先保障基本公共服务

It is frequently debated whether public funds should be directed towards supporting painters, musicians, poets and other artists, with critics dismissing this as a waste of taxpayers' money. After considering both perspectives, I believe that moderate state support is justified, although it should not crowd out more urgent social spending.

Those in favour of public arts funding highlight the cultural and educational value that creative work brings to a society. State-backed galleries, concert halls and poetry workshops give ordinary citizens free access to experiences otherwise reserved for the wealthy, helping to level the cultural playing field. A compelling argument can also be made in terms of national identity, because folk songs and local paintings preserve stories textbooks often ignore. When the government funds young artists, it also nurtures a creative workforce that may later boost tourism and fashion.

Opponents, however, argue that taxpayers' money is better spent on hospitals, schools and infrastructure, where benefits are more tangible. In their eyes, supporting struggling artists is a luxury that poorer citizens cannot afford, when waiting lists for surgery lengthen. Critics also worry that state grants may end up rewarding a small artistic elite rather than ordinary creators, raising questions of fairness.

In my view, a sensible compromise is to ring-fence a modest share of the cultural budget for grants, scholarships and public exhibitions, while ensuring that core services such as health and education remain the priority. Such a balance lets society enjoy the cultural enrichment artists provide without neglecting the basic needs of citizens.

In conclusion, I still believe that the state has a legitimate, if limited, role in supporting artistic work, because culture and welfare are both pillars of a civilised country.
