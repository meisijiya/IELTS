Task 2 — Local history vs world history (agree-disagree)

It's more important for school children to learn about their local history than world history. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2022.6.4)

立场: 不同意以本地史取代世界史; 理由1: 世界史培养跨文化理解与多元视角; 理由2: 世界史帮助学生应对跨国挑战

Some people argue that school children should concentrate on the history of their own country rather than the history of the wider world. I firmly disagree with this view, because a balanced curriculum that places world history at the centre is far better suited to the realities of modern life.

The first reason is that world history cultivates the kind of cross-cultural understanding that local study alone cannot provide. When students examine how different civilisations have solved shared problems such as poverty, disease and political conflict, they begin to recognise patterns that transcend any single nation. A clear example is the way comparative study of the industrial revolution in Britain, Germany and Japan helps young people grasp how technology, capital and education interact across cultures. Without such wider perspective, students may grow up with a narrow sense of national identity that finds it hard to engage with neighbours, trading partners or immigrants.

The second reason is that world history equips future citizens to deal with challenges that, by definition, do not respect national borders. Issues such as climate change, pandemics and global trade require young people who can read international news critically, weigh evidence from multiple sources and cooperate with people whose upbringing differs from their own. Schools whose curricula focus only on local history risk producing graduates who feel bewildered by the wider forces shaping their working life. Were world history to be crowded out of the timetable, the next generation would inherit a brittle patriotism instead of the resilient cosmopolitanism that an interconnected century demands.

In conclusion, I still believe that world history deserves a central role in school education, because it broadens minds and prepares young people to live responsibly in a shared global community.
