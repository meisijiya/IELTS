Task 2 — Technology and reading and writing skills (agree-disagree)

Some people think that the increasing use of computers and mobile phones for communication has a negative effect on young people's reading and writing skills. Do you agree or disagree? (2024.7.20)

立场: 部分同意; 理由1: 碎片化沟通削弱深度阅读与正式写作; 理由2: 但技术也提供新的读写实践与表达机会

It is often claimed that the growing reliance on computers and smartphones for everyday communication is harming the reading and writing abilities of young people. While I partly agree with this concern, I would argue that the picture is more complicated, because digital tools have simultaneously opened up fresh ways to read and write that did not exist a generation ago.

My first reason for concern is that instant messaging has trained teenagers to favour short, hurried sentences over the careful, paragraph-by-paragraph writing once expected in school essays. Spelling is reduced to a quick tap on predictive text, and long articles are skimmed in seconds rather than read attentively. A clear example is the way many pupils now struggle to draft a coherent letter of complaint, even though they can fire off dozens of casual messages in a single evening. Without deliberate practice, their formal literacy is unlikely to keep pace with their conversational fluency.

My second reason for a balanced view is that digital platforms actively encourage young writers and readers in ways that print culture rarely managed. Teenagers run fan blogs, compose video scripts, publish short stories on writing apps and receive instant feedback from global audiences. Were teachers to ignore these new literacy practices, they would miss the most powerful motivation their students currently have for putting pen to paper or fingers to keyboard.

In conclusion, I partly agree that constant messaging can weaken traditional reading and writing skills, but I still believe that a balanced curriculum which blends classic texts with modern digital composition can turn the technology into an ally rather than a long-term threat.
