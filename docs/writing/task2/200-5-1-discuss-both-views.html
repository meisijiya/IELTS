Task 2 — Technology and crime (discuss-both-views)

Some people think technology development decreases crime, while others believe it actually encourages crimes. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (2021.9.26)

视角1: 技术降低传统街头犯罪并协助破案; 视角2: 技术催生网络与高科技犯罪; 立场: 净收益仍偏正面但需监管

The rapid progress of digital technology has prompted fierce debate about its impact on crime, with optimists arguing that innovation makes society safer and pessimists warning that new tools hand offenders fresh opportunities. This essay will examine both sides before explaining why the overall effect is positive, provided that laws keep pace with technology.

Those who claim that technology reduces crime point to the steady fall in many traditional offences since surveillance cameras and biometric payment became widespread. Automatic number-plate recognition helps police trace stolen vehicles, while CCTV in city centres deters opportunistic robbery. A clear example is the visible decline in street muggings near busy stations in cities that have invested heavily in public cameras, where thieves know their faces will be shared within minutes.

On the other hand, critics observe that the same technology has created disturbing new categories of crime that did not exist a generation ago. Cyber-fraud, identity theft, ransomware attacks and the online grooming of minors have surged as daily life has migrated online. Criminals can now steal millions from a laptop on another continent, and victims often feel that police lack the expertise to respond. Were such offences to escalate further, public trust in digital commerce could collapse, harming ordinary shoppers and small businesses alike.

My own view is that, weighing these arguments, the net effect of technology on crime remains favourable, because most traditional victims benefit from improved detection while only a minority suffer from new fraud. The key, however, is continuous investment in cyber-police units, international cooperation and digital literacy so that risks do not outpace protections.

In conclusion, technology has both curbed familiar crimes and invented unfamiliar ones, but with thoughtful regulation it can keep making our communities safer.
