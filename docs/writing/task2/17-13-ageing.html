Task 2 — Ageing (two-questions)

A recent survey shows that many countries across the world people are generally living longer. But an increased life expectancy has many implications for the aging individuals and for the society as a whole. What are the possible effects of a longer life expectancy on individual and the society? (2021.1.21)

In recent decades, people in many parts of the world have been living significantly longer than previous generations. Longer life expectancy, however, brings a range of consequences for both the elderly themselves and the wider society. This essay will discuss the main effects on individuals and on the community.

For individuals, an extended lifespan can bring both opportunities and difficulties. On the positive side, older adults have more time to enjoy retirement, to travel, and to develop hobbies they were too busy for during their working years. They can also spend more time with their grandchildren and pass on family knowledge. On the negative side, longer life does not always mean healthier life. Many elderly people suffer from chronic illnesses such as diabetes or heart disease, and some experience loneliness after their friends and spouses have passed away. Financial pressure is also a concern, since pensions and savings may not cover decades of retirement.

For society as a whole, a longer-living population creates several important effects. The most obvious one is the ageing of the workforce. As more employees retire, there may not be enough young workers to fill the positions, which can slow economic growth and increase the burden on workers. Healthcare and pension systems also come under greater strain, since governments spend more on hospitals, long-term care, and benefits. At the same time, however, older citizens can still contribute to society through volunteering, part-time work, and mentoring younger generations.

In conclusion, longer life expectancy has clear effects on both individuals and society. While individuals may enjoy more free time but face health and financial challenges, society must adapt to an ageing workforce and rising public spending. All parties need to plan ahead so longer lives can be rewarding.
