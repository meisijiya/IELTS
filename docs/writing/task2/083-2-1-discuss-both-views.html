Task 2 — Job and social life (discuss-both-views)

Some people think a job not only provides income but also a social life. Others think it is better to develop a social life with people you do not work with. Discuss both views and give your opinion. (2024.1.27)

立场: 倾向后者, 工作与社交分离更健康; 理由1: 同事友谊易把工作矛盾带入私人关系; 理由2: 外部社交保护业余时间不被工作侵蚀

The role that work plays in a person's social life has become a contentious issue. Some people argue that a job should provide both income and a close circle of friends, while others believe that it is healthier to keep professional and personal lives separate. Both perspectives carry weight and merit closer examination.

Those who see the workplace as a natural source of friendship point out that colleagues share experiences that outsiders rarely do. Long projects, tight deadlines and shared challenges create strong bonds, and many employees confess that their closest companions are people they first met in the office. Such friendships also bring practical benefits: a teammate who knows your strengths is more likely to offer help in a crisis, and informal conversations by the coffee machine can relieve the pressure of an otherwise stressful day. These workplace ties are also easier to maintain, since colleagues already meet regularly without the need for additional scheduling.

Those who prefer to develop friendships outside work, however, warn that mixing the two can blur essential boundaries. When colleagues are also close friends, disagreements over personal matters may spill into professional decisions, and a failure at work can easily poison one's free time. Having considered this, many workers deliberately seek social circles through hobbies, sports or volunteering, where relationships are not complicated by hierarchies, performance reviews or shared employers. For such people, an external social network provides a refreshing contrast to the demands of office life and protects relationships from the inevitable disappointments of any career.

After considering both perspectives, I am more inclined to support the second view, because keeping professional and personal circles separate tends to reduce conflicts and protect the leisure time that modern workers so badly need.
