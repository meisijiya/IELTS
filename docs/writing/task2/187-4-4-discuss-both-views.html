Task 2 — Fresh water use rights (discuss-both-views)

Some people think they have right to use as much fresh water as they want, but others believe government should control the use of fresh water as it is limited resource. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (2023.2.4)

立场: 倾向政府监管, 同时保留基本使用自由; 理由1: 自由使用容易导致浪费与生态恶化, 加剧供需失衡; 理由2: 政府监管能够保障公平分配、提高使用效率; 但完全剥夺个人权利违背基本民生, 应以阶梯监管 + 最低保障配额替代。

Whether individuals should be free to use as much fresh water as they wish, or whether the state should strictly control water consumption, remains a controversial question in regions where scarcity is becoming the norm. Both views carry genuine arguments, and any sensible policy must reconcile household freedom with the realities of a finite resource.

Those who defend unrestricted household access argue that water is a basic human need and that imposing limits would intrude on personal freedom. Generous use supports hygiene and a reasonable quality of daily life, and forcing families to ration every shower or laundry cycle would disrupt routines, particularly where young children or elderly relatives are present. Treating water as a freely usable commodity is, in their view, the most dignified response to an essential need.

Advocates of government regulation counter that freshwater supplies are finite and unevenly distributed, so leaving consumption to individual preference invites serious harm. Wealthier households would quietly consume far more than their share, leaving poorer neighbours to face shortages and rising prices. A clear example is the way unregulated groundwater pumping has drained village wells in many arid regions, forcing residents to migrate. Mandatory limits, smart metering and tiered pricing can preserve water for everyone while penalising conspicuous waste.

In my view, neither extreme is satisfactory. Governments should regulate extraction and charge higher rates for excessive use, while guaranteeing every household a free lifeline supply. Such a model respects the basic right to water while curbing the waste that pure freedom would allow.

In conclusion, unrestricted consumption is no longer sustainable in water-stressed regions, but heavy-handed controls would also be unjust. A balanced system combining regulation with a guaranteed minimum offers the most convincing way forward.
