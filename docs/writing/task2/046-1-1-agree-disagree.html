Task 2 — Specialists vs wider subjects (agree-disagree)

Students in university education should develop specialists in one subject area rather than develop a wider range of different subjects. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2023.3.18)

立场: 不同意过窄的专业化; 理由1: 跨学科训练培养复合型思维能力; 理由2: 现实难题需要跨领域协作

Some people believe that university students should specialise in a single subject rather than study a wider range of disciplines. I disagree with this view, because a broad education across several fields gives graduates advantages that narrow specialisation cannot match.

The first reason is that interdisciplinary study develops the kind of versatile thinking that employers and society increasingly need. When students combine, for instance, statistics with environmental science, or philosophy with computer engineering, they learn to translate ideas between domains and to question assumptions that look obvious inside any one field. A clear example is a graduate who combines public-health training with data analysis and can therefore design community vaccination campaigns more effectively than a pure biologist or a pure statistician. Without exposure to several disciplines, even talented specialists may find their judgement too narrow for the messy problems of real workplaces.

The second reason is that the challenges facing modern societies rarely fall within the boundaries of a single academic department. Climate policy, for instance, demands insight from economics, engineering, ethics and international relations at once. Were universities to push every student into early specialisation, graduates would gain deep but isolated knowledge, leaving them poorly equipped to collaborate with experts from other backgrounds. A broader curriculum, by contrast, allows students to build bridges between fields and tackle complex issues through genuinely integrated thinking. Those who study only one subject may excel inside their own field, yet they often struggle to communicate across disciplines or to recognise when an outsider's perspective is essential to a solution.

In conclusion, I still believe that universities should encourage students to study a wider range of subjects, because versatile minds serve both individual careers and the public good better than narrow expertise alone.
