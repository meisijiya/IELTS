Task 2 — Free public transport 24/7 (agree-disagree)

Some people say the best way for the government to solve traffic congestion is to provide free public transport 24 hours a day and 7 days a week. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2026.2.7)

立场: 不同意，免费全天候并非最佳方案; 理由1: 财政成本高而边际效用低，深夜需求有限; 理由2: 提高班次、整合票价与需求管理是更高效的投资方向

Some people argue that the most effective way to relieve traffic congestion is for the government to make all public transport completely free, twenty-four hours a day and seven days a week. I firmly disagree with this view, because the policy would consume enormous public funds while offering only marginal gains for congestion, and the money could be far better spent on more targeted improvements.

The first reason is that operating buses, trains and metro services around the clock is extraordinarily expensive while demand outside rush hours is generally quite low. Maintaining night-time services requires extra drivers, security staff, fuel and ongoing maintenance, and the resulting subsidy would divert resources from hospitals, schools and other essential public services without producing a meaningful drop in congestion, since most drivers are on the road during the day rather than at three in the morning.

The second reason is that targeted improvements to public transport deliver greater benefits at a fraction of the cost. Increasing peak-hour frequency, integrating ticketing across different modes and protecting dedicated bus lanes would cut journey times for the majority of commuters and convince many motorists to leave their cars at home. Adding congestion charges for city centres and improving cycling infrastructure would reinforce the effect, encouraging genuine long-term shifts away from private cars in a way that free off-peak rides simply cannot match.

In conclusion, I still believe that free twenty-four-hour public transport is not the best answer to traffic congestion, because it addresses the wrong time of day and consumes funds that could otherwise transform the system for the many people who actually need it most urgently.
