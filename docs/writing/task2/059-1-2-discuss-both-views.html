Task 2 — Organised activities for children (discuss-both-views)

Some people believe children should have organised activities in their free time. Others believe children should decide what to do in their free time on their own. Discuss both sides and give your opinion. (2024.1.6)

立场: 视年龄而定,低龄以组织活动为主,高龄给予自主权; 理由1: 组织活动培养社交与时间管理; 理由2: 自由安排促进创造力与独立性

How children should spend their leisure hours is a question that divides many parents and educators. Some argue that free time should be filled with structured activities such as music lessons, sports clubs or art classes, while others believe that children themselves are best placed to choose how to use their hours away from school. Both views present reasonable arguments and merit careful consideration.

Supporters of organised activities point out that structured programmes teach children skills they are unlikely to acquire on their own, from teamwork in a football squad to discipline in a music practice room. A clear example is the noticeable advantage enjoyed by children who attend weekly drama or coding workshops; such sessions expose them to peers from different backgrounds and help them learn how to cooperate and follow instructions. Parents who work long hours also value these activities because they provide a safe and stimulating environment when the family is otherwise unavailable.

Those who favour unstructured time, by contrast, argue that free play is essential for the development of creativity, independence and emotional resilience. When children are left to organise their own afternoons, they must negotiate, invent games and resolve minor conflicts without adult intervention, capabilities that no scheduled class can fully instil. Furthermore, an overscheduled childhood can leave young people unable to pursue their genuine interests, simply because every slot has been booked by well-meaning adults.

Having considered both perspectives, I am more inclined to support a balanced approach that depends on the child's age. Younger children, in my view, benefit most from the routine and social exposure of organised activities, whereas teenagers should be granted increasing freedom to design their own leisure so they can develop the autonomy they will need as adults.
