Task 2 — Rural to urban migration (advantage-disadvantage)

More and more people move from the countryside to big cities. Does this development bring more advantages or disadvantages to the environment? (2022.7.23)

立场: 缺点大于优点; 理由1: 城市扩张占用农田林地，损害生态; 理由2: 城市人口密度上升加剧污染和能耗

The accelerating movement of people from rural areas into large cities has become a defining trend of the twenty-first century. While this migration brings a few modest environmental benefits, on balance I believe it creates more disadvantages than advantages for the natural world around us.

One modest advantage is that concentrating more residents in dense urban centres can, in theory, limit the spread of buildings across the surrounding countryside. Apartment blocks use much less land per family than scattered rural housing, and shorter travel distances within a city may encourage walking, cycling and efficient public transport instead of long car journeys. A clear example is the Netherlands, where strict containment of urban growth has preserved large stretches of farmland and meadow for decades, even though the national population has continued to increase steadily over that period.

The disadvantages, however, are far more serious and considerably harder to reverse for policymakers. Each new arrival in a megacity adds to traffic volumes, household energy use, sewage output and waste production, while the city itself expands outwards to accommodate them, swallowing fertile soil, wetlands and fragments of nearby forest. Air quality in fast-growing capitals such as Delhi and Lagos has deteriorated to the point that routine outdoor activity becomes a genuine health hazard, and the carbon footprint of a single new resident in such places can easily outweigh the environmental savings of leaving the countryside empty behind them.

On balance, I believe the disadvantages of rural-to-urban migration outweigh the advantages for the environment, because the harms are long-lasting, affect millions of people simultaneously and are difficult to mitigate once a megacity has already crossed a critical size threshold.
