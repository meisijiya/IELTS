Task 2 — Spending on national festivals (agree-disagree)

In some countries, too much money is spent by both the government and individuals on national festivals, such as New Year. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2022.4.21)

立场: 不同意过度担忧; 理由1: 节日承载文化认同与情感联结; 理由2: 节日消费拉动经济与就业

Some critics argue that governments and ordinary citizens spend too much money on national festivals such as New Year, Lunar New Year or Independence Day. I disagree with this criticism, because the cultural and economic returns from these celebrations generally justify the expense and often reach far beyond the festival itself.

The first reason is that national festivals play a vital role in sustaining cultural identity and shared emotional life across generations. When families gather, decorate streets, prepare traditional meals and pass stories to younger generations, they reinforce the social bonds that hold communities together in an age of constant mobility and digital distraction. A clear example is Lunar New Year in Vietnam, where the extended holiday allows migrant workers to return home, repair family ties and transmit customs such as bánh chưng wrapping, practices that would otherwise fade under the pressure of modern urban life.

The second reason is that festival spending stimulates significant economic activity in sectors such as hospitality, transport, retail and the performing arts. Hotels fill up, restaurants hire extra seasonal staff, florists and gift shops see their annual sales peak, and performers find paid work that sustains their craft for the rest of the year. Were governments to slash festival budgets out of austerity concerns, they would forfeit this reliable seasonal boost and damage small businesses that depend on the celebrations in order to survive the leaner months that follow.

In conclusion, I still believe that spending on national festivals is broadly money well spent, because it preserves cultural memory and delivers tangible economic benefits that extend well beyond the days on which the celebrations actually take place.
