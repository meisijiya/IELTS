Task 2 — Free housing duty (discuss-both-views)

Some people think that the government should provide free housing, while others think that it is not the government's responsibility. Discuss both views and give your opinion. (2023.8.26)

立场: 面向弱势群体提供; 理由1: 免费住房保障基本生存; 理由2: 全民免费难以负担且易滥用

The question of whether the state should guarantee free housing for its citizens has sparked intense debate, with some arguing that this is a fundamental duty and others insisting that it is not. After considering both views, I believe that governments should provide housing for the most vulnerable, but should not make it universally free.

Supporters of free public housing emphasise the intrinsic value of having a secure roof and the social dangers of leaving people on the streets. When families cannot afford rent, children move between shelters, schoolwork suffers and illness rises. State-funded housing, advocates claim, also reduces crime, because stable addresses help former offenders find work and rebuild their lives. In countries such as Finland, sustained public investment in social housing has cut homelessness dramatically, which suggests that generous provision can be both humane and effective.

Those who oppose the idea argue that free housing for all is financially unrealistic and would discourage personal effort. Building enough flats to cover an entire population would require decades of construction and enormous debt, while the taxes needed to maintain them might drive businesses overseas. Critics add that universal provision could be exploited, since wealthy households would simply claim a free flat they do not need, leaving the poor on long waiting lists.

In my view, a targeted approach is more sensible. The state should guarantee decent accommodation for low-income families, the elderly and people with disabilities, while encouraging working adults to buy or rent through regulated markets. Such a policy protects the vulnerable without overburdening the public purse.

In conclusion, I still believe that governments must play a strong role in housing, but the policy should focus on those in genuine need rather than the population as a whole.
