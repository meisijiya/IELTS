Task 2 — Physical activities over study (advantage-disadvantage)

Parent should encourage children to spend less time on studying and more time on doing physical activities. To what extent do you agree? (2021.2.20)

Many parents today are encouraged to push their children to spend less time studying and more time doing physical activities. While this advice has clear benefits for children's health, it also brings genuine drawbacks that families should not overlook.

The main advantage of reducing study time in favour of sport is better physical health. Regular exercise strengthens children's muscles and bones, improves cardiovascular fitness and helps them maintain a healthy weight at a stage when childhood obesity is rising in many countries. Beyond the body, sport teaches lessons that classrooms often cannot: how to handle winning and losing, how to cooperate with teammates and how to persevere when results disappoint. These social and emotional skills serve children long after the final whistle has blown.

There are, however, several disadvantages. Cutting back on study time can leave gaps in core subjects such as mathematics and languages, gaps that may become harder to fill once children enter high school where the syllabus moves quickly. Some active children also lose the habit of independent reading, which is closely linked to vocabulary growth and later academic success. In competitive school systems, parents who push sport too aggressively risk their child falling behind peers who spend their afternoons on extra tutoring.

In my view, the sensible approach is balance rather than extreme. Children should have protected time for sport every week, but the rest of their day should still include enough quiet study to keep up with schoolwork.

In conclusion, encouraging children to spend more time on physical activity brings real health and character benefits but also carries academic risks if pushed too far. A balanced weekly schedule allows families to enjoy the advantages while limiting the drawbacks.
