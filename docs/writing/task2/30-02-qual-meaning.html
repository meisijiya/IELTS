Task 2 — Educational qualifications and success (discuss-both-views)

Some people believe educational qualifications always bring success. Others think educational qualifications do not necessarily bring success. Discuss both views and give your own opinions. (2021.2.25)

People hold different views on whether educational qualifications always lead to success. While some argue that a good degree guarantees a bright future, others believe success depends on factors beyond formal qualifications. In my opinion, both views contain some truth, but qualifications alone are not enough to ensure success.

Those who believe qualifications always bring success have a strong point. In professions such as medicine and engineering, a recognised degree is the basic ticket to enter the field. Without it, talented people are screened out early. Studying for a degree also trains young people to think in a structured way and manage their time, which helps them perform well later. Parents see qualifications as a safe path, because they open doors that would otherwise stay closed.

However, those who question the value of qualifications also raise fair concerns. Success is measured in different ways: an entrepreneur who builds a small business may succeed without a university degree. Real-world performance also depends on qualities exams cannot measure, such as creativity and the ability to handle pressure. Many influential business leaders never finished formal study, which shows the link between qualifications and success is far from automatic.

My view is that qualifications are valuable but should not be the only measure of success. A degree provides a useful starting point, yet the choices people make afterwards and the networks they build all play an equal part. Schools should encourage young people to value learning itself, not only the certificate at the end.

In conclusion, educational qualifications open many doors and should not be dismissed, but they do not by themselves guarantee success. A balanced view that respects both formal learning and personal qualities offers the most useful guidance for young people today.
