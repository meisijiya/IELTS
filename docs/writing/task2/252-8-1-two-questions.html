Task 2 — Western dress worldwide (two-questions)

In many countries, people now wear western-style dresses such as suits and jeans rather than traditional clothing. Why is this? Is this a positive or negative development? (2023.11.4)

立场: 解释西方服饰因全球职场与媒体渗透而扩散，并判断整体为积极发展; 理由1: 全球职场规范与西方媒体推动日常服饰西化; 理由2: 积极为主因拓宽个人自由并保留仪式服装中的传统

The aim of this essay is to examine why Western-style clothing such as suits, jeans and T-shirts has largely replaced traditional dress in the daily lives of people around the world, and to consider whether this shift in everyday fashion should be regarded as a positive or negative cultural development.

Two related forces explain the trend. The first is the spread of a global workplace culture. As multinational companies, international schools and global business hotels have grown across Asia, Africa and Latin America, employers increasingly expect staff to wear suits or smart casual clothes, and employees comply in order to compete for jobs. The second force is media influence. Hollywood films, K-dramas and global social media constantly present jeans and T-shirts as the standard of modernity, persuading young people that traditional robes, saris or kimonos belong to grandparents rather than to themselves.

On balance, I consider the change a positive one. Western everyday clothing is generally practical, affordable and well suited to urban commuting, which means people can move easily between countries without feeling awkward. The change also frees individuals from rigid rules about what a man or a woman should wear, an important step for personal freedom and gender equality. Admittedly, some cultural heritage is weakened when traditional dress disappears from daily life, yet these costumes can still flourish at weddings, religious festivals and national ceremonies, places where their meaning is in fact heightened.

In conclusion, the rise of Western clothing reflects a global workplace and a global media environment, and although it sidelines traditional everyday dress, the overall impact is positive because it broadens personal choice, supports gender equality and leaves ceremonial costume to carry the cultural meaning that ordinary wear no longer has to bear.
