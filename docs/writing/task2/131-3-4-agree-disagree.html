Task 2 — Roads vs public transport (agree-disagree)

People think more public money should be spent on roads and motorways than the public transport system. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2023.8.19)

立场: 不同意，应优先投入公共交通; 理由1: 公共交通更环保、覆盖面广、利于低收入群体; 理由2: 公路扩建诱发更多车流，长期难解拥堵

Some people argue that more public money should be spent on roads and motorways than on the public transport system. I firmly disagree with this view, because investing in high-quality public transit delivers greater social, environmental and economic returns than yet more lanes for private cars ever can in the long run.

The first reason is that public transport moves far more people per dollar spent while serving the broadest section of society. A single metro line can replace hundreds of bus routes and thousands of private car journeys, cutting urban congestion, air pollution and traffic noise at a fraction of the cost of a comparable motorway expansion in the same corridor. Crucially, well-funded buses and trains are also the only realistic option for low-income workers, elderly residents and teenagers who cannot yet drive, so strengthening public transit promotes social inclusion rather than catering mainly to wealthier motorists on the road.

The second reason is that road-building tends to generate more traffic rather than relieve it, a phenomenon planners call induced demand. Were governments to widen motorways or build fresh bypasses through residential areas, many commuters would simply drive farther and more often, eroding any short-term gain within a few years of the new lanes opening. Public transport, by contrast, produces lasting reductions in vehicle use because every passenger represents one fewer car on the road, and frequent services encourage people to give up private vehicles entirely over time.

In conclusion, I still believe that public transport deserves a much larger share of public investment than roads and motorways, because it offers broader benefits to society and addresses the root causes of urban congestion far more sustainably than endless highway expansion ever will in modern cities.
