Task 2 — Parents giving everything (two-questions)

Some parents give their children everything that their children ask for or let them do anything they want to do. Is this good for children? What could be the consequences for these children when they grow up? (2026.1.31)

立场: 不是好事，长期有负面影响; 理由1: 缺乏规则与延迟满足训练会阻碍自律和情商发展; 理由2: 成年后难以应对挫折，在职场与人际中容易受挫。

The aim of this essay is to discuss whether giving children unrestricted freedom and material abundance is beneficial, and to consider the long-term consequences these young people may face in adulthood.

Regarding the first question, an over-permissive upbringing is generally not good for children. When parents automatically grant every wish, children lose the opportunity to learn that resources are finite and that effort is required to obtain them. Equally important, the absence of clear limits prevents young people from developing self-discipline and emotional regulation, two capacities that psychologists regard as the foundations of resilience. A child who has never heard the word no grows up with a distorted sense of reality, expecting the world to bend to his or her preferences. Without early lessons in patience and reciprocity, such children also struggle to appreciate the feelings of others.

The second question concerns what happens when such children reach adulthood. The consequences are often serious and visible across several domains. In the workplace, graduates who have always been accommodated tend to struggle with constructive criticism and routine authority, leading to repeated job changes and stalled careers. In personal relationships, an inability to compromise frequently results in conflict with partners, friends and colleagues. Over time, repeated disappointments may even erode mental wellbeing, as the gap between expectation and reality gradually widens with each unmet expectation. Were these patterns to remain unaddressed across an entire generation, social cohesion within families and broader communities could deteriorate significantly.

In conclusion, granting children unlimited freedom is harmful both immediately and in the long term. Parents who set reasonable boundaries, encourage patience and teach the value of effort give their children a far more enduring gift than material indulgence.
