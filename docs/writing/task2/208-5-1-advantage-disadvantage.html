Task 2 — Face-to-face business meetings (advantage-disadvantage)

Although modern technology has made international communication very easy, many business people still travel long distances for business meetings. Do the benefits of face-to-face meetings outweigh the disadvantages? (2025.9.13)

立场: 利大于弊; 理由1: 现场会议有助于建立信任与完成复杂谈判; 理由2: 但需承担时间与碳排放成本

Even though video-conferencing and instant translation have made remote collaboration almost effortless, many senior executives still travel thousands of kilometres to meet clients in person. While in-person meetings clearly carry higher costs, I believe the advantages of face-to-face encounters continue to outweigh the drawbacks in most significant business situations across the global economy.

The main advantage is that physical presence builds the kind of trust and shared understanding that screens rarely achieve. Body language, the firm handshake at the start and the relaxed dinner after a long day all contribute to a sense of mutual commitment that emails cannot replicate. A clear example is the long negotiation between two merging firms, where sensitive compromises became possible only after several days of side-by-side work, allowing each side to interpret tone and hesitation accurately and to repair small misunderstandings on the spot. Without this human contact, deals of real importance would either stall or be signed with hidden reservations that later cause disputes.

The main disadvantage is that international travel is expensive, exhausting and increasingly hard to justify on environmental grounds. A round trip from London to Singapore consumes far more carbon than a year of ordinary household activity, and senior staff often lose several valuable working days simply to recover from jet lag. Were companies to insist on travel for every routine matter, they would erode both their budgets and their public reputation for sustainability.

In conclusion, the benefits of face-to-face meetings still outweigh the costs when major partnerships, sensitive negotiations or client relationships are at stake, but routine exchanges should continue to rely on digital tools so that international travel remains reasonable and environmentally responsible.
