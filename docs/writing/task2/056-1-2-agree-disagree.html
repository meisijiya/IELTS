Task 2 — Stay in school until 18 (agree-disagree)

Everyone should stay in school until they reach the age of 18. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2022.10.29)

立场: 基本同意延长至18岁; 理由1: 保障基础学历与数字素养门槛; 理由2: 延缓过早分流,促进身心成熟

The suggestion that all young people should remain in some form of schooling until they are eighteen has been adopted by a growing number of governments. While I accept that there are rare cases in which an early vocational route can be justified, I broadly agree with the policy, because extending compulsory education protects both individual opportunity and the long-term health of society.

The first reason is that staying in school until eighteen guarantees that every young person acquires a baseline of academic and digital literacy that the modern economy now assumes. A clear example is the rapid spread of online banking, remote services and information-based work; without a solid secondary education, school-leavers at sixteen find themselves locked out of jobs that even a decade ago required only basic reading skills. Keeping students engaged until eighteen ensures they are not stranded on the wrong side of this rising threshold.

The second reason is that an extra two years of structured learning gives adolescents the cognitive maturity and emotional stability to make genuinely informed choices about their future. Decisions taken at fourteen or fifteen about whether to enter an apprenticeship, join the labour market or pursue higher education are often premature, since the teenage brain is still developing its capacity for long-term planning. Were young people to leave school at sixteen, many would later regret having closed doors that an additional year or two of schooling would have kept open.

In conclusion, I largely agree that universal education should continue until the age of eighteen, because it raises the floor of opportunity for individuals while simultaneously strengthening the skills base of the wider community.
