Task 2 — Annual driver test (agree-disagree)

Some people think that the best way to improve road transport safety is to let the driver test each year. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2021.5.8)

立场: 不同意，年检并非最佳方案; 理由1: 多数事故源于行为而非技能，针对性检查更有效; 理由2: 年检成本高、效益低，针对高龄与违规者重点考核更合理

Some people argue that the most effective way to improve road safety is to require all drivers to sit a fresh driving test every year. I firmly disagree with this view, because most accidents are caused by risky behaviour rather than fading skill, and an annual test would impose heavy costs on responsible drivers while doing little to deter the dangerous ones behind the wheel.

The first reason is that skill is rarely the decisive factor in road accidents. Detailed traffic studies consistently identify speed, distraction, fatigue and intoxication as the main causes of collisions, not drivers’ inability to handle a vehicle on a normal day. A driver who passed his test decades ago but now texts at the wheel poses a far greater threat than an elderly motorist who simply needs to renew his certificate, yet both would be treated identically under an annual-testing regime that ignores how they actually behave in traffic.

The second reason is that the practical costs of universal annual testing are enormous and the benefits questionable. Setting up examination centres, paying staff and forcing millions of drivers to take time off work every year would divert public money from more effective measures such as random breath tests, speed cameras and graduated penalties for repeat offenders on the road. A targeted approach, in which older drivers and those with previous violations are retested more often, would achieve the same safety gains at a fraction of the cost to the public purse.

In conclusion, I still believe that annual testing for all drivers is not the best path to safer roads, because it addresses the wrong cause of collisions and imposes excessive burdens on compliant citizens across the country.
