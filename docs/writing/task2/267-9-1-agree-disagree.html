Task 2 — Domestic aid priority (agree-disagree)

It is impossible to help all people in the world, so governments should focus on people in their own countries. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2024.5.25)

立场: 不同意; 理由1: 优先本国是基本政治责任; 理由2: 国际援助反哺本国安全与经济

It is often argued that, because governments cannot aid every person on the planet, they should concentrate their efforts on citizens at home. While I accept that domestic needs must come first, I disagree with the suggestion that foreign assistance should be abandoned, because well-directed international aid can protect the long-term interests of citizens as well as those of people abroad.

The first reason is that focusing on one's own citizens is a basic political duty that cannot easily be replaced. Governments collect taxes from residents and are elected to improve local services, so neglecting these duties in favour of distant aid would erode public trust and weaken democratic accountability. A clear example is the political backlash seen in many European countries when voters feel that their own pension system is underfunded while billions are sent abroad. When a state looks after its own people first, it builds the legitimacy needed to act internationally later on, and it avoids the charge of misplaced priorities that often topples governments.

The second reason is that well-designed foreign aid can exert influence on stability, trade and disease control far beyond the recipient country. By funding vaccination programmes in poor regions, wealthy states help to prevent pandemics that would otherwise reach their own borders within weeks. By supporting post-conflict reconstruction, they open new markets for their own exporters and reduce the pressure of large-scale migration on welfare systems. A compelling argument exists for treating overseas assistance as a strategic investment in shared global security, not a charitable gift.

In conclusion, I still believe that governments should look after citizens first, but they should also maintain a responsible role in global aid, because the two goals ultimately reinforce each other in an interconnected world.
