Task 2 — University or work experience (discuss-both-views)

Some people believe that studying at university or college is the best route to a successful career, while others believe that it is better to get a job straight after school and develop work experience. Discuss both views and give your opinion. (2024.11.23)

立场: 取决于行业性质,二者各有所长; 理由1: 大学培养专业能力与长远发展空间; 理由2: 早入职场积累实战与经济独立

The choice between continuing to university and entering the workforce immediately after secondary school confronts almost every young person. Some argue that higher education remains the smartest foundation for a successful career, while others maintain that practical work experience gained early offers stronger long-term returns. Both positions contain insights that merit careful examination.

Advocates of the university route stress that a degree provides deep, structured knowledge and professional accreditation that cannot be quickly picked up on the job. A clear example is engineering, where graduates without a formal qualification are rarely entrusted with major projects, no matter how gifted they may be. Universities also expose students to critical thinking and peer networks that continue to pay dividends throughout their working lives, and this preparation is often the difference between reaching the top of their field and remaining on its margins.

Those who favour starting work straight after school point out that early employment allows young adults to develop practical skills, financial independence and a realistic understanding of their industries. Many successful business owners and craftsmen have built expertise through years of hands-on experience rather than classroom study, and habits and contacts formed in real workplaces are difficult to acquire later. Furthermore, in fast-moving sectors such as digital marketing, knowledge gained from a degree can quickly be overtaken by what is learned on the ground.

Having considered both perspectives, I am more inclined to support a flexible view: university remains the wiser choice for highly specialised professions, whereas entering the labour market directly can be equally rewarding for careers built on practical skill. The right decision, in my view, depends less on a universal rule and more on the individual young person's chosen field.
