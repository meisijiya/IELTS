Task 2 — Aid for developing countries (discuss-both-views)

Developing countries require help offered by international organizations to ensure healthy and sustainable development. Some people think that financial aid is important. Others, however, believe practical aid and advice is more important. Discuss both these views and give your own opinion. (2021.10.16)

立场: 倾向以务实援助与咨询为主，财政援助为辅; 理由1: 财政援助解决紧急资金缺口; 理由2: 务实指导与制度建议带来可持续能力建设

When international organisations assist developing nations, debate frequently arises over the most effective form of that assistance. Some observers argue that money should be the priority, while others insist that practical training and expert advice deliver deeper and more lasting results.

Those who favour financial aid emphasise that capital is the most basic bottleneck in poorer economies. Without sufficient funds, governments cannot build roads, equip hospitals or import vaccines during a public health emergency, and ordinary families lack the savings to weather crop failures. A clear example is the rapid international response to the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami, where large cash donations allowed coastal communities to be rebuilt quickly, demonstrating how money can translate into immediate survival.

Advocates of practical aid and advice, however, point out that money without knowledge often evaporates. When experts train local doctors, help design effective tax systems or share techniques for sustainable farming, they build skills that remain in the country long after the funds have been spent. Were donors to flood a fragile state with cash but send no advisers, much of the money would be lost to mismanagement, and the country would remain dependent on the next donation. Practical help creates the institutions that make future financial aid unnecessary.

In my view, expert training and practical advice should form the core of international assistance, with financial support reserved for genuine emergencies and infrastructure projects that local institutions cannot yet finance themselves.

In conclusion, money responds swiftly to crisis, but practical guidance and skills transfer create the lasting capacity a developing country truly needs, and the wisest programmes combine both in carefully balanced proportions.
