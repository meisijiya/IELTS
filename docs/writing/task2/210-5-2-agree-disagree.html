Task 2 — Fat tax on unhealthy food (agree-disagree)

More and more people are seriously overweight. Some people suggest the solution to this problem is to increase the price of fattening foods. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2021.6.17)

立场: 不同意单一涨价; 理由1: 难以触达核心人群且伤及低收入者; 理由2: 应辅以教育与健康食物的可及性

As rates of serious overweight continue to climb in many countries, some commentators propose simply raising the price of fattening foods to discourage people from buying them. While a modest levy may play a small part, I firmly disagree with the idea that price alone can solve this complex health problem, because it unfairly burdens low-income families and ignores the deeper causes of unhealthy diets.

My first reason is that price signals often fail to reach the people most at risk, while imposing real hardship on those who can least afford it. People with addictive relationships to sugary snacks, or those whose long working hours leave no time to cook, are unlikely to change habits simply because a chocolate bar costs a few cents more. At the same time, a single mother buying a cheap treat for her children after a long shift would pay the price, deepening the financial stress that already drives poor dietary choices in the first place.

My second reason is that sustainable weight management depends on education, food availability and supportive environments, none of which a price rise alone can provide. School cooking classes, clear food labelling, safer streets for walking and reliable access to fresh vegetables in poor neighbourhoods would all do far more good than a blunt tax. Were governments to rely solely on price increases, the underlying drivers of obesity would remain untouched, and health statistics would barely shift.

In conclusion, I still believe that modest pricing measures may have a minor role, but lasting progress against overweight will come from combining education, better food environments and accessible exercise, rather than from penalising ordinary shoppers at the checkout.
