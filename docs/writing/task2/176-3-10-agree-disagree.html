Task 2 — Banning mobile phones in public places (agree-disagree)

Some people think that the use of mobile (cell) phones should be banned in public places such as in libraries, shops and on public transport. To what extent do you agree or disagree with this statement? (2023.10.7)

立场: 部分同意，主张有针对性限制而非全面禁止; 理由1: 限定场所禁机能保护安静与安全; 理由2: 全面禁令过度干预且应急通讯不可或缺

Some commentators argue that mobile phones should be banned outright in public places such as libraries, shops and on public transport. I partially agree with this view, because targeted restrictions are sensible where silence and safety matter, yet a sweeping ban would ignore the legitimate uses phones serve for ordinary citizens.

The first reason to support limited bans is that loud phone use genuinely damages shared public spaces. In libraries, where concentration is essential, a single conversation can ruin the work of an entire reading room, while on public transport, ring tones cause stress for passengers seeking a quiet journey. Hospitals, courts and small shops depend on calm environments to function properly, and reasonable rules against active calls in these settings protect service quality and human dignity. Voluntary phone etiquette, unfortunately, has not been enough, which is why clearly signposted bans can restore the basic civility that every citizen deserves.

The second reason is that a blanket prohibition would interfere with perfectly reasonable uses of phones. Many commuters rely on phones to navigate, check tickets or summon help in emergencies, and shoppers frequently use them to compare prices. Were a complete ban to be enforced, these ordinary activities would be criminalised, and vulnerable people, especially the elderly, would lose a vital lifeline. The practical answer, therefore, is to target the problems directly: ask users to take calls outside, silence ring tones in shared spaces, and reserve outright bans for places such as operating theatres where the case is overwhelming.

In conclusion, mobile phones should be restricted in clearly designated quiet zones, but a sweeping ban in every public place is unnecessary, because thoughtful etiquette rules and well-publicised limits can solve the real problems without sacrificing the genuine benefits phones provide.
