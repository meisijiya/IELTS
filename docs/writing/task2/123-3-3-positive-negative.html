Task 2 — Retirees spend on themselves (positive-negative)

In some countries, old people who retire from work spend the money on themselves (e.g. holiday) rather than save money for their children. Is this a positive or negative development? (2022.2.12)

立场: 倾向积极; 理由1: 自我消费提升退休生活质量与心理健康; 理由2: 拉动旅游与服务经济，也降低成年子女的经济依赖

In some countries, retired people increasingly choose to spend their savings on themselves, on holidays, hobbies and personal comfort, rather than passing every spare dollar on to their children. While this shift in family finances may seem self-centred, I would argue that, on balance, it is a positive development for both retirees themselves and society as a whole.

The first reason is that spending on themselves directly improves retirees’ quality of life and psychological well-being. After decades of working and raising families, older adults deserve the chance to enjoy their later years in comfort, and discretionary spending on travel, cultural activities or small luxuries gives them something meaningful to look forward to each month. Numerous studies of ageing populations show that retirees who remain socially and physically active tend to suffer less from depression and cognitive decline than those who live in constant financial restraint.

The second reason is that this kind of spending stimulates the wider economy and reduces unhealthy financial dependency within families. Money that flows into hotels, restaurants, theatres and adult-education classes supports local employment and small businesses, while retirees who maintain their own standard of living are less likely to become a long-term financial burden on their adult children. Granted, some families still rely on parental transfers to buy a first home, but a balanced approach, in which retirees help selectively without depleting their own resources, serves both generations well.

In conclusion, despite the worries of more traditional families, the trend of retirees spending on themselves is largely a positive development, because it enriches their remaining years and contributes to a healthier, more dynamic local economy.
