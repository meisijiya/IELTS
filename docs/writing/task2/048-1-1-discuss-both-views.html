Task 2 — All subjects vs best subject (discuss-both-views)

Some people believe that teenagers should concentrate on all school subjects. Others believe that teenagers should focus on the subject that they are best at or that they find the most interesting. Discuss both these views and give your own opinion. (2024.9.7)

立场: 倾向广博为先、专注在后; 理由1: 全面学习帮助发现未知兴趣与天赋; 理由2: 专注擅长科目能培养深度与信心

Whether teenagers should study a broad range of school subjects or focus on the ones they are best at is a question that divides parents and educators. Some argue for a wide curriculum that develops the whole person, while others believe that concentrating on a few favourite subjects produces deeper expertise. This essay will examine both views and offer my own opinion.

Supporters of a broad curriculum point out that the teenage years are a crucial period for discovering talents that students themselves may not yet recognise. Different subjects open different ways of thinking, and a teenager who drops one too early may close off a future path before realising its value. A clear example is a student who disliked biology at school but later discovered a passion for medicine after a single inspiring lecture; had that subject been removed from the timetable, the opportunity would never have arisen.

Advocates of concentration, however, argue that early specialisation produces deeper mastery. Young people who are allowed to focus on the subjects they enjoy or excel at can develop genuine expertise and build confidence through sustained achievement. This focused approach can give teenagers a meaningful head start in their chosen field.

In my view, both positions have merit, and the answer lies in sequencing, not choosing. Schools should ensure a broad foundation in the compulsory years, so that students sample enough disciplines to make informed choices, and only afterwards allow genuine specialisation in the subjects that engage them most.

In conclusion, I am more inclined to support the broad-curriculum model in early secondary education, because exposure to many subjects later enables teenagers to make a wiser, more confident choice of specialisation.
