Task 2 — Culture (positive-negative)

In more and more countries, people choose to give money on special occasions rather than giving gifts chosen personally. Why might be this case? Is it a positive or negative development? (2025.3.8)

In many countries today, it has become common for people to give money instead of carefully chosen presents on special occasions such as weddings, birthdays, or graduations. This essay will examine why this trend has appeared and explain why I consider it a largely negative development.

There are two main reasons for this change. First, modern life is extremely busy, and choosing a suitable gift for each friend or relative can take a great deal of time and thought. With long working hours and many social events to attend, giving cash is simply easier. Second, money has become more practical for both givers and receivers. Recipients can use the money to buy exactly what they need, which avoids the awkward situation of receiving an unwanted or duplicated gift, while givers do not have to worry about taste or size.

However, I believe that this trend has reduced the warmth and meaning of personal celebrations. A thoughtfully chosen gift shows that the giver knows the recipient well, and the time spent searching for it reflects genuine care. Giving cash, by contrast, can feel impersonal and may suggest that relationships have become more about convenience than connection. When everyone gives cash, the act loses its symbolic meaning. This loss of personal touch is, in my view, the main reason why the trend should be regarded as negative.

In conclusion, although giving money is convenient for busy modern lifestyles, it has weakened the emotional value of gift-giving. A small item chosen with care can communicate far more than a banknote. People should try to balance practicality with personal thought so that special occasions remain meaningful.
