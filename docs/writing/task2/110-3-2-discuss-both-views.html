Task 2 — Match local style vs own style (discuss-both-views)

Some people think the newly built houses should be the same as the old houses in local areas. Others argue that local authorities should allow people to build houses in their own style. Discuss both views and give your opinion. (2025.6.21)

立场: 倾向允许个人风格; 理由1: 风格统一保护地方特色; 理由2: 个人风格鼓励创新与自我表达

Whether new houses in established neighbourhoods should be required to match the existing local style, or whether homeowners should be free to build in any style they prefer, is a question that divides many communities today. Some argue that visual harmony matters most, while others maintain that personal creativity should take priority. Both perspectives carry weight and merit closer examination.

Supporters of strict uniformity emphasise that a consistent skyline gives a district its distinctive character and protects property values over the long term. When every new façade, roof pitch and window frame echoes what has stood for generations, the area feels coherent and visitors can immediately sense its history and identity. A clear example is the strict architectural code enforced in parts of rural England and New England, where new houses must use timber cladding and pitched slate roofs so that villages retain the quiet charm that attracts both tourists and long-term residents in equal measure.

Defenders of personal style counter that rigid uniformity can quickly become sterile and ignores the legitimate desire of families to express their own tastes and values. Allowing homeowners to experiment with modern materials, bold colours or innovative layouts encourages architectural innovation and keeps streets lively rather than museum-like and frozen in time. In cities such as Copenhagen and Melbourne, a deliberate mix of heritage terraces and contemporary designs has produced vibrant neighbourhoods that feel both rooted in the past and unmistakably of the present day.

Having considered both perspectives carefully, I am more inclined to support the second view, because personal freedom in design usually produces richer communities, provided that basic rules on safety, height and neighbourly respect are still firmly enforced by local authorities.
