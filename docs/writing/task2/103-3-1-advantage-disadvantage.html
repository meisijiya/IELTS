Task 2 — Rural youth migration (advantage-disadvantage)

Young people are leaving their homes in rural areas to study or work in the cities. What are the reasons? Do the advantages of this development outweigh the disadvantages? (2026.3.28)

立场: 优点大于缺点; 理由1: 城市提供更优质教育和就业机会; 理由2: 但缺点是乡村空心化与家庭分离

An increasing number of young adults are choosing to leave their rural hometowns behind in order to study or find work in large cities, and this pattern raises a clear question about its overall impact. While the trend does carry some serious drawbacks, on balance I believe its advantages outweigh its disadvantages for both individuals and the wider economy.

The main reason young people move is that cities offer better universities, more diverse jobs and higher starting salaries than most rural areas can provide. Graduates from elite programmes in fields such as medicine, engineering and finance can rarely practise their craft in villages that lack the necessary infrastructure, while ambitious entrepreneurs depend on the dense networks of suppliers and customers that only urban centres can supply. A clear example is South Korea, where the concentration of skilled young workers in Seoul has been a key driver of the country's technological growth over recent decades.

The disadvantages, however, are real and should not be overlooked. As the most productive age group leaves, rural communities often suffer from labour shortages, school closures and the slow disappearance of local shops, while elderly parents are left behind without daily support from their adult children. This pattern is now familiar across much of inland China and Eastern Europe, where villages that once bustled with young families have turned quiet and grey within a single generation.

On balance, I believe the advantages of this development outweigh the disadvantages, because the personal and economic gains for young migrants are substantial and long-lasting, while the rural costs can be mitigated through better transport links, remote working and targeted government investment in the countryside.
