Task 2 — School transport duty (discuss-both-views)

Some people say it is the government responsibility to transport children to school. While others believe parents should transport their children to school. Discuss both views and give your opinion. (2021.11.27)

立场: 政府与家长分担最佳; 理由1: 政府保障公平与安全; 理由2: 家长提供灵活陪伴

It is often argued that the state ought to take responsibility for taking children to school, while others maintain that parents should shoulder this duty. After weighing both views, I believe that the most sensible approach is a shared one, in which government schemes cover essential transport and parents handle the rest.

Those who favour government transport point to the importance of equal access and public safety. Where local authorities run dedicated school buses, children from low-income or single-parent households can reach lessons on time, regardless of whether their own adults own a vehicle. Such a system also reduces traffic around school gates, because coaches carry dozens of pupils at once. Drivers employed by the council can also be properly vetted, lowering the risk of accidents and protecting young passengers.

Opponents, however, argue that the family is the natural unit to handle this task. Parents who drive their own children to school can choose flexible routes, save time on busy mornings, and use the journey to talk about the day ahead, strengthening the parent-child bond. They also fear that heavy state involvement may erode parental authority and turn a private concern into an expensive bureaucratic programme, particularly when school budgets are already tight.

In my view, a balanced partnership is preferable to either extreme. The government should guarantee free or low-cost buses in rural areas, for students with disabilities, and for families in genuine need, while leaving parents free to drive when they wish. Such an arrangement combines the efficiency of public provision with the warmth of family care.

In conclusion, I still believe that school transport works best when the state and parents share the responsibility, because public safety and family closeness are both essential to a child's wellbeing.
