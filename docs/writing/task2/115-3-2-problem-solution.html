Task 2 — Cash gifts reasons (problem-solution)

In more and more countries, people choose to give money on special occasions rather than giving gifts chosen personally. Why might this be the case? Is it a positive or a negative development? (2022.8.11)

立场: 总体积极但需保留心意; 理由1: 实用性与移动支付便利; 理由2: 但削弱礼物挑选中蕴含的情感投入

The aim of this essay is to examine the reasons why an increasing number of people now prefer to give cash rather than personally chosen gifts on special occasions, and to assess whether this trend should be regarded as a positive or negative development.

Several clear reasons explain the move towards cash gifts. Modern households often live in small apartments with limited storage, so bulky presents risk becoming clutter, while a digital transfer can be used exactly when and how the recipient wants. The rise of mobile payment platforms has also made sending money across long distances almost effortless, which is particularly helpful when family members live abroad. A clear example is the growing use of e-wallets at weddings in South Korea, where guests can send congratulatory money to a couple's account with a single tap on their phone, sparing everyone the trouble of carrying envelopes to the reception.

From my perspective, this trend is largely a positive development because it reduces wasted spending on unwanted items and lets recipients put money towards something they truly value. Nevertheless, it does carry a small but real drawback: the careful thought that goes into choosing a meaningful present can fade when giving is reduced to a simple transaction. Were every birthday and wedding to be marked only by a transfer of funds, the rituals of anticipation and shared appreciation that small gifts bring would gradually lose their place in family life.

In conclusion, the main reasons for the shift to cash gifts are practicality and digital convenience, and on balance I consider this development to be a positive one, provided that senders occasionally add a personal note or symbolic item to preserve the warmth that money alone cannot convey.
