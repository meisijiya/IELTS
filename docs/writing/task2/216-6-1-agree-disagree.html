Task 2 — Media good news (agree-disagree)

The media should include more stories which report good news. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2025.5.17)

立场: 同意增加正面报道; 理由1: 正面新闻改善公众情绪与心理健康; 理由2: 正面新闻能放大可复制的成功经验

Many commentators argue that newspapers, broadcasters and online platforms should devote more space to good news rather than concentrating on conflict and disaster. While journalism must continue to expose wrongdoing, I firmly agree with this proposal, because positive coverage offers tangible psychological benefits and reminds citizens that cooperation and steady progress remain part of everyday life.

The first reason is that steady exposure to uplifting stories improves public well-being. Research in behavioural psychology consistently shows that prolonged focus on alarming reports increases anxiety, erodes trust in institutions and can even encourage withdrawal from civic life. When media outlets balance their front pages with reports on community projects, scientific breakthroughs and ordinary acts of generosity, readers experience a measurable lift in mood and a stronger willingness to engage with those around them. A clear example is the way regular readers of solutions-focused publications report higher levels of optimism than those who consume only crisis-driven feeds.

The second reason is that positive reporting highlights solutions that deserve wider imitation. When a city successfully reduces traffic accidents through careful street design, or a hospital programme lowers readmission rates among elderly patients, these stories encourage other municipalities and clinics to copy what is actually working. Without such visibility, successful innovations remain invisible and the public never learns which approaches actually deliver results, which arguably weakens the very accountability journalism claims to serve. A broader menu of encouraging coverage therefore complements, rather than competes with, the watchdog role the press is expected to play in a healthy democracy.

In conclusion, I still believe that more good news in the media would benefit both individual well-being and collective progress, provided that newsrooms retain the independence to expose wrongdoing whenever it occurs.
