Task 2 — Who should pay for university (agree-disagree)

Some people believe that university students should pay all the costs of studies because university education only benefits students themselves not the society as a whole. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2021.12.25)

立场: 不同意学生全额承担; 理由1: 大学教育外溢整个社会与公共领域; 理由2: 全自费会固化为不平等的阶层壁垒

Some people argue that university students should bear the full cost of their studies, on the grounds that higher education only benefits the individuals who receive it. I strongly disagree with this view, because a society that prices its graduates out of advanced learning will quickly lose both its public good and its basic sense of fairness.

The first reason is that universities generate benefits that extend far beyond their graduates. Doctors trained in public universities heal future patients; engineers design bridges used by millions; teachers educated in normal universities shape the next generation of citizens across the country. The knowledge produced in academic laboratories feeds industries, governments and charities, often without any direct payment to the original researchers. A clear example is the development of mRNA vaccine technology, which depended on publicly funded basic research long before any private company commercialised the resulting product.

The second reason is that charging students the full price would erode social mobility and entrench inherited privilege. Families with greater wealth could easily send their children to the best institutions, while talented students from poorer backgrounds would be locked out by debt aversion alone. Were higher education to depend entirely on private funding, universities would quietly become a closed club for the rich, and the social contract that links talent to opportunity would collapse in practice. Society as a whole would then pay the cost in lower productivity, weaker civic life and growing resentment.

In conclusion, I still believe that higher education is a shared public investment, and that students should contribute only what they can reasonably afford after substantial government support has been provided.
