Task 2 — Print books no longer needed (agree-disagree)

More and more people are using computers and electronic devices to access information, therefore there is no need to print books, magazines and newspapers on paper. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2022.4.16)

立场: 不同意全面取消纸质印刷; 理由1: 纸质材料提供深度阅读体验与可靠保存; 理由2: 电子阅读尚未覆盖所有读者与场景

Some observers argue that, since most readers now obtain information from screens, the printing of books, magazines and newspapers on paper is no longer necessary. I firmly disagree with this view, because print continues to offer benefits that digital formats have not yet replicated, and because moving too quickly towards an entirely paperless culture would exclude millions of readers.

The first reason is that printed materials provide a depth of engagement and a sense of permanence that screens rarely match. Readers consistently report better concentration when working through a long paper book, and academic studies link paper-based revision to higher scores on reading comprehension tests than equivalent work on a tablet. Print editions also survive for decades without depending on software updates or licensing agreements, which matters for libraries, courts and government archives that require documents to remain accessible long after their original publisher might vanish.

The second reason is that electronic reading still leaves large groups of people behind. Households without affordable broadband, elderly readers who find touchscreens confusing and schoolchildren in underfunded districts all depend on printed materials as their main route into long-form reading. Were publishers to abandon print entirely, they would deepen the existing reading gap between rich and poor communities, with serious consequences for literacy and lifelong learning. A clear example is the way many rural schools in developing countries continue to rely on donated paper textbooks because e-readers and charging facilities remain unreliable.

In conclusion, I still believe that printed books, magazines and newspapers remain an essential complement to digital media, because they support deeper reading, long-term preservation and equitable access that screens alone cannot yet guarantee.
