Task 2 — Transportation vs technology for business (discuss-both-views)

Some people believe that cheap and fast transportation is essential for successful international business, while others argue that advanced technology is more important. Discuss both views and give your opinion. (2026.5.23)

视角1: 高效运输是国际供应链命脉; 视角2: 数字技术让远程协作取代面对面; 立场: 两者互补, 技术略占上风

Whether international business now depends more on cheap and fast transportation or on advanced digital technology is a question that divides many commentators. This essay will examine both arguments before concluding that, while both are indispensable, advanced technology is beginning to play the slightly more decisive role.

Those who emphasise transport point out that physical goods still need to cross oceans and continents, and the cost of doing so can make or break a supply chain. Just-in-time manufacturing, perishable food exports and emergency parts deliveries all rely on fast ships, cargo planes and reliable trucking networks. A clear example is the electronics industry, where a delay of a few days at a congested port can idle entire assembly lines and erase the profit on millions of components.

Those who emphasise technology counter that information now moves far faster than containers, and that many international deals are signed, designed and serviced entirely through cloud platforms and video calls. Design teams separated by twelve time zones can collaborate on a single prototype, while payment and logistics software keep customers informed in real time. Were global firms to abandon these tools in favour of constant travel, costs would soar and decision-making would slow to a crawl, making them uncompetitive against rivals who had gone digital.

My own view is that transportation and technology complement each other, but advanced technology now exerts a slightly greater influence because it enables businesses to operate even when physical movement is restricted, as recent global disruptions have shown. Investment in both, however, remains essential for truly resilient international commerce.

In conclusion, cheap transport keeps the goods moving, but advanced technology increasingly decides who can sell, design and support them at the speed the modern market expects.
