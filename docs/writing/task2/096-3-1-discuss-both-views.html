Task 2 — Urban population growth (discuss-both-views)

Some people think rapid population growth in cities only benefits people who live there while others believe that there are disadvantages. Discuss both these views and give your own opinion. (2025.2.8)

立场: 缺点大于优点，取决于规划; 理由1: 城市规模效应降低公共服务人均成本; 理由2: 人口过密加剧房价、交通和污染压力

Some people argue that rapid population growth in cities only benefits the residents who live there, while others maintain that it creates serious disadvantages for urban life. Both views carry weight and merit closer examination, yet on balance I am inclined to believe that the drawbacks outweigh the advantages unless careful planning is undertaken well in advance.

Supporters of urban growth point out, with a compelling argument, that concentrating more people in one place produces powerful economies of scale. Hospitals, universities and public transport networks can serve a larger customer base at a lower per-capita cost, while dense labour markets allow small firms and skilled professionals to find one another quickly. A clear example is Shenzhen, where the steady influx of millions of workers in three decades transformed a small fishing town into a global technology hub, raising average incomes and creating economic opportunities that simply did not exist before.

Critics of this trend counter that the same concentration exerts intense pressure on housing, mobility and the local environment. Once a city's population outruns its infrastructure, property prices rise sharply, daily commutes lengthen and air quality deteriorates, posing a serious threat to the wellbeing of long-term residents. Without sufficient green space, sewage capacity or waste management, public health also tends to deteriorate, which is why expanding megacities such as Lagos or Dhaka now struggle to provide even basic services to millions of their inhabitants.

Having considered both perspectives carefully, I remain convinced that the disadvantages of unmanaged urban growth tend to be longer-lasting and more widespread than the benefits, since the latter depend entirely on whether adequate infrastructure genuinely precedes the influx of new residents.
