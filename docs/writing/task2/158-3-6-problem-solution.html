Task 2 — Bicycles no longer main transport (problem-solution)

Many people believe that bicycles are a healthy and environmentally friendly mode of transport. However, it is no longer the main form of transport. What are the reasons? What could be done to encourage the use of bicycles among the wider population? (2021.4.10)

立场: 揭示汽车文化与道路设施问题阻碍骑车，并提出基础设施与激励方案; 理由1: 汽车普及与道路危险使骑车不便; 理由2: 自行车专用道与税收激励可推动回归

The aim of this essay is to explore why bicycles, despite their obvious health and environmental benefits, are no longer the dominant mode of transport in most cities, and to suggest ways to persuade wider populations to ride them once again.

Two main factors explain the decline. The first is the relentless spread of private cars: as household incomes have risen, car ownership has become a status symbol and a daily convenience, and many families now treat a vehicle as essential as a refrigerator. Cycling, by contrast, is often portrayed as slow, sweaty and unglamorous, especially for commuting in work clothes. The second factor is the physical environment. Many cities have spent decades widening roads for motor traffic while neglecting cycling infrastructure, so riders must share busy lanes with trucks and buses, a danger that discourages all but the most confident athletes. Bad weather and the lack of secure parking at workplaces further weaken the appeal.

Reversing the trend requires safer infrastructure and meaningful incentives. Municipal authorities should build protected cycle lanes that physically separate bicycles from motor vehicles, and should provide secure bike-parking facilities near offices and transit stations so riders do not fear losing their machines. Financial measures can help as well: employers could offer cycle-to-work schemes, governments could give modest tax breaks to households that regularly commute by bike, and insurers could lower premiums for frequent cyclists. Public campaigns that frame cycling as a smart, modern choice rather than a sacrifice would also shift attitudes, particularly among younger commuters.

In conclusion, the bicycle has lost its central place largely because of car culture and unsafe roads, and a serious revival will depend on protected lanes, secure parking and policies that actively reward everyday cycling.
