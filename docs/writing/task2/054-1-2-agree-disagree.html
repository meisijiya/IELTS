Task 2 — Schools turning into good citizens (agree-disagree)

Some people think that schools are merely turning children into good citizens and workers, rather than benefiting them as individuals. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2022.6.16)

立场: 不同意学校仅培养公民与劳动者; 理由1: 个人成长是教育的核心目的; 理由2: 忽视个人福祉的教育会损害长远幸福感

Some people argue that schools exist mainly to turn children into good citizens and productive workers, rather than to benefit them as individuals. I firmly disagree with this view, because education that ignores the personal growth of each student fails in one of its most important purposes.

The first reason is that personal development is the central purpose of education, not a by-product of it. Schools are places where young people discover their talents, refine their characters and learn what gives their lives meaning, regardless of whether those discoveries fit neatly onto a job application. A clear example is a teenager who finds a lifelong love of poetry through a literature class; that student's happiness and sense of identity are valuable in their own right, even if poetry never appears on a payslip. Were schools to treat every pupil as raw material for the labour market, they would reduce education to a sorting machine and overlook the intrinsic value of learning for its own sake.

The second reason is that an education focused exclusively on producing workers and citizens can quietly damage individual well-being. When schools prioritise conformity, test scores and obedience above curiosity, imagination and self-expression, they create graduates who may be employable yet feel hollow inside. Long-term studies suggest that people who were allowed to follow their interests at school enjoy stronger psychological resilience and report greater life satisfaction decades later. Schools whose mission stops at citizenship and employment risk producing generations who succeed on paper but struggle to find genuine fulfilment.

In conclusion, I still believe that schools exist first and foremost to benefit children as individuals, because personal growth is the foundation on which both responsible citizenship and meaningful work later rest.
