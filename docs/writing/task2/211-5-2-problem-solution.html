Task 2 — Unhealthy food habits (problem-solution)

Many people like to eat unhealthy food even though they know it's bad for themselves. Why? What effective way can improve people's healthy eating habits? (2021.12.18)

原因: 便利、广告与情绪驱动; 方案: 学校教育结合可负担的健康饮食选项

The aim of this essay is to explain why so many people continue to choose unhealthy food despite knowing the risks, and to suggest practical ways to improve everyday eating habits. Although the gap between knowledge and behaviour is often frustrating, a combination of education and better access can close it.

The first reason for the gap is convenience. After a long working day, few people have the energy to plan meals, shop for fresh ingredients and cook from scratch, so a ready-made pizza or a packet of instant noodles becomes the default option. A clear example is the office worker who knows a salad would be healthier but settles for a fried snack from the nearest stall because it can be eaten at the desk within minutes. The second reason is aggressive advertising by fast-food chains, which repeatedly links their products to happiness, friendship and success, making healthy food seem dull by comparison.

The most effective solution is to begin practical food education early in life and to make healthy choices the easy choices at every price point. School curricula should teach simple cooking skills, while governments can support local markets, subsidise fresh fruit and vegetables in poor neighbourhoods and require clearer calorie labels on menus. A clear example is Japan, where school lunches and family cooking traditions have kept obesity rates far lower than in countries with similar incomes but weaker food education.

In conclusion, people choose unhealthy food because it is faster, cheaper and emotionally comforting, and the most effective remedy is to combine honest education with affordable, attractive healthy options rather than relying on warnings alone.
