Task 2 — Global shop uniformity (discuss-both-views)

Many different countries have most shops and products as the same. Some consider it a positive development, whereas others consider it negative. Discuss both views and give your opinion. (2023.7.29)

The growing similarity of shops and products across many countries has sparked wide debate about whether this trend is something to welcome or to worry about. While some observers treat this uniformity as a positive sign of global progress, others see it as a negative loss of local character. This essay will discuss both views and offer my own opinion.

Those who view the trend positively point out that international brands and global supply chains bring real practical benefits. A traveller who finds the same trustworthy pharmacy or coffee chain in every city can feel safer abroad, and families with relatives in different countries can share familiar products with one another. International standards also push local producers to improve their packaging and customer service, because consumers today can easily compare what is on offer.

Critics, however, argue that the spread of identical shops and products weakens local culture and traditional skills. When global fast-food outlets replace neighbourhood family restaurants, recipes that have been passed down for generations may disappear. Small farmers who cannot compete with cheap imports are often forced to leave their land, and young people now prefer branded clothing over the handmade textiles of their grandparents. Over time, this homogenisation can make a city centre in Asia look almost the same as one in Europe.

In my view, the trend carries both clear gains and clear losses, and the right response is balance rather than blanket refusal. Governments and communities should welcome the convenience and quality that global trade brings, while still protecting local shops, traditional crafts and regional food culture through fair financial support and reasonable regulation.
