Task 2 — Universal car, TV, fridge (advantage-disadvantage)

It is suggested that everyone in the world should have a car, a TV and a fridge. Do you think the disadvantages outweigh the advantages for society? (2022.5.21)

立场: 缺点大于优点; 理由1: 这些电器提升生活便利; 理由2: 但全球普及将加剧资源消耗与环境压力

Some commentators have suggested that universal ownership of a car, a television and a refrigerator should be a basic aspiration for every person on the planet. While each of these appliances offers clear benefits, on balance I believe the disadvantages of pushing for their global ownership outweigh the advantages for society as a whole.

The main advantage is that these three items genuinely raise living standards and open up new opportunities. A car can connect remote villagers to jobs and hospitals, a television brings news and educational programmes into homes that might otherwise be isolated, and a refrigerator allows families to store fresh food safely for days rather than shopping daily. In many developing regions, the spread of these goods has been closely linked to longer life expectancy, better schooling for children and the empowerment of women who gain time previously spent on manual household tasks.

The main disadvantage is that producing and operating billions of additional cars, televisions and refrigerators would dramatically worsen the strain on the planet's resources and climate. Car manufacturing and driving release vast amounts of carbon dioxide, refrigerators use refrigerants that can be potent greenhouse gases, and television production consumes rare earth metals that are already mined under harsh conditions. Were every household on earth to be equipped with all three, global emissions would rise sharply, and the resulting climate damage would fall most heavily on the very poorest communities who gained least from the extra gadgets.

On balance, I believe the disadvantages of universal ownership outweigh the advantages, because the environmental costs are global and long-lasting, while the gains are largely individual and can be achieved through more efficient, shared alternatives such as public transport, community screens and neighbourhood food hubs.
