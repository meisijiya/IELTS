Task 2 — Cars and motorcycles vs environment (problem-solution)

Many people continue to use cars and motorcycles even though they know that they are bad for the environment. Why is this? What can be done to reduce the use of these vehicles? (2022.5.28)

立场: 私家车惯性是出行结构与政策共同作用的结果, 需要交通、税收与城市规划协同改变; 理由1: 习惯依赖、出行可达性差、燃油价格与环保成本脱节导致人们明知有害仍开车; 理由2: 改善公交、推广电动车、自行车道、拥堵收费与远程办公可有效减少私家车使用。

The aim of this essay is to examine why so many drivers continue to use cars and motorcycles despite awareness of their harmful environmental effects, and to discuss what governments and individuals can do to reduce this dependence on private motorised travel.

Several practical and structural reasons explain why people keep choosing private vehicles. In most suburbs and small towns, public transport is infrequent, indirect or simply absent, so a private car often becomes the only reliable means of commuting, shopping or taking children to school. Long-established habits and the social status attached to car ownership reinforce this pattern, even among environmentally conscious drivers. Meanwhile, the price of fuel rarely reflects the wider environmental and healthcare costs of burning it, leaving petrol and diesel substantially cheaper than the damage they cause. Limited safe infrastructure for cycling and walking further discourages people from switching to low-emission alternatives.

Effective solutions therefore have to attack the problem from several angles at once. Investment in frequent, affordable and well-connected public transport can make car-free commuting realistic for many households. Urban planners should prioritise protected cycling lanes, wider pavements and mixed-use neighbourhoods so that short journeys can be made on foot or by bicycle. Economic measures such as fuel taxes, congestion charges in city centres and subsidies for electric vehicles help to align the price of driving with its true social cost. Finally, employer incentives for remote working and flexible hours can reduce the daily need for commuting altogether, easing both emissions and congestion.

In conclusion, people use private vehicles mainly because current transport systems are designed around them, and only sustained investment in alternatives, supported by smarter pricing and planning, can break that dependence over the long term.
