Task 2 — Advertising economic vs social (discuss-both-views)

Some people say advertising has positive economic effects. Others think it has negative social effect because it will make people dissatisfied with who they are and what they are. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (2021.5.29)

立场: 折中（经济价值显著，心理代价需管理）; 理由1: 广告支撑就业、推动创新与信息流通; 理由2: 理想化形象制造不满与身份焦虑，需要媒体素养与监管

The question of whether advertising produces more economic benefit or social harm continues to divide opinion. Some observers point to the contribution advertising makes to growth and innovation, while others worry that the same messages erode personal confidence and community values.

Those who emphasise the economic benefits note that advertising funds a great deal of media that audiences would otherwise have to pay for directly, including commercial television, free newspapers and many online platforms. Campaigns also inform consumers about new products, lower the cost of searching for the best option and reward companies that invest in genuine innovation. Small businesses in particular rely on affordable advertising channels to reach customers beyond their immediate neighbourhood, which in turn creates jobs and encourages local entrepreneurship.

Those who emphasise the social costs argue that advertising systematically distorts self-image, especially among young people. By promoting unrealistic body standards, luxury lifestyles and constant comparison with peers, commercial messages can leave ordinary viewers feeling inadequate and chronically dissatisfied. A clear example is the way research links heavy exposure to image-based advertising with higher rates of anxiety and lower self-esteem in teenage girls. From this perspective, advertising quietly damages the psychological well-being on which healthy communities depend.

In my view, neither view is wholly correct. The economic contribution of advertising is genuine, but its social costs are real enough that responsible regulation, accurate representation and stronger media literacy in schools are necessary to keep the balance fair.

In conclusion, advertising brings clear economic advantages and serious social risks, so the most sensible policy is to harness its commercial value while educating citizens to interpret its messages critically.
