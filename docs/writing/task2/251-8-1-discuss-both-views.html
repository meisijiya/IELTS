Task 2 — Global brand uniformity (discuss-both-views)

Many different countries have most shops and products as the same. Some consider it a positive development, whereas others consider it negative. Discuss both views and give your opinion. (2023.7.29)

立场: 倾向支持统一便利但担忧本土文化被边缘化，需政策平衡; 理由1: 全球连锁带来可预测品质与游客便利; 理由2: 本土小店与文化多元正在被挤压消失

Anyone who has travelled from Bangkok to Berlin, or from Mexico City to Manila, may notice that the same coffee chains, clothing brands and convenience stores now line the streets of very different capitals. Whether this growing uniformity among shops and products is positive or negative is a question that divides observers around the world.

Those who see the trend as positive emphasise the predictability and consumer power it brings. A traveller who recognises the menu of a familiar chain can order a meal without language anxiety, and shoppers in distant cities enjoy the same quality guarantees they trust at home. Multinational retailers also pressure local competitors to modernise, raising service standards and offering lower prices than family-run shops could match. For young professionals and tourists, this global sameness is a quiet comfort.

Critics, however, argue that the spread of identical brands erases local character. Independent bookshops, family bakeries and neighbourhood markets are steadily replaced by glossy franchise outlets, and entire districts lose the distinctive charm that once made them worth visiting. When a city's main square looks the same as a hundred other cities, cultural diversity quietly deteriorates, and small entrepreneurs find their livelihoods under threat. Were local councils to ignore the problem, historic neighbourhoods could become indistinguishable within a generation.

In my view, the rise of global brands is welcome for the convenience it brings, but governments should actively protect small shops and distinctive local businesses so that uniformity does not crowd out cultural variety.

In conclusion, identical shops across borders offer real convenience and quality assurance, yet their unchecked growth threatens independent businesses and cultural diversity, and the most balanced approach welcomes global brands while safeguarding the local character that gives each city its identity.
