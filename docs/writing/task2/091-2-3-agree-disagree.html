Task 2 — Four-week holiday (agree-disagree)

Employers should give their staff at least four weeks holiday a year employees can be better at their work. To what extent do you agree or disagree with this view? (2023.2.25)

立场: 同意四周假期; 理由1: 减少倦怠提升长期产能; 理由2: 改善身心健康与工作生活平衡

The view that employers should guarantee their staff at least four weeks of paid holiday every year is often dismissed as unrealistic, yet I firmly agree with it, because generous leave entitlement relieves chronic burnout and ultimately makes workers more productive when they are on the job.

The first reason is that extended rest periods prevent the accumulated exhaustion that steadily erodes the quality of work over a long career. Employees who never fully disconnect from their duties are far more likely to experience stress-related illness, repeated errors and a gradual loss of motivation that is difficult to reverse once it sets in. A clear example can be found in countries such as Sweden and Denmark, where long annual leave is the norm and where studies consistently show high levels of workplace efficiency and innovation across many sectors. The second reason is that four weeks of holiday allows staff to repair the physical and mental health that demanding jobs put under constant pressure, a benefit that shorter leave simply cannot provide in any meaningful way. Workers who spend real time with their families, pursue hobbies or simply sleep properly return to their desks with sharper concentration and greater creative energy. Were companies to keep pushing their employees without such recovery time, both individual wellbeing and overall output would deteriorate over time, and the savings from withheld leave would soon be lost to rising absenteeism and staff turnover.

In conclusion, I strongly believe that a minimum of four weeks' annual holiday is not a luxury but a sound investment, because it sustains the energy and commitment on which any successful organisation depends.
