Task 2 — Adult literacy funding (agree-disagree)

Education of young people is highly prioritized in many countries. However, educating adults who cannot write or read is even more important and governments should spend more money on this. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2026.5.16)

立场: 部分同意，成人扫盲确实需要更多投入，但不应当削减青少年教育; 理由1: 成人扫盲能在短期内提升劳动生产率与社会融入，边际收益高; 理由2: 投资应来自新增预算而非削减青少年教育，两者是互补关系。

It is often argued that adult literacy deserves greater government investment than it currently receives, even if this means spending less on the education of young people. I partly agree with this view, because adult literacy does indeed generate substantial social returns, yet I disagree with the suggestion that funding should be diverted from youth schooling.

The first reason adult literacy programmes deserve more money is that they produce remarkably quick and tangible returns. An adult who learns to read can apply new skills immediately at work, follow written safety instructions and help his or her own children with schoolwork, multiplying the benefit across an entire household. In contrast, investment in young people, however important, typically takes fifteen to twenty years to translate into measurable economic gains. For governments facing tight budgets and urgent social problems, expanding adult literacy is therefore a compelling argument in policy terms.

Nevertheless, funding for adult literacy should come from additional resources rather than from cuts to youth education. The two groups are complementary, not interchangeable: literate parents improve educational outcomes for their children, and well-schooled young people later become the adults who need fewer remedial programmes. Were governments to reduce spending on schools in order to pay for literacy classes, the long-term pipeline of skilled workers would deteriorate noticeably, ultimately undermining the very goals that adult education is meant to achieve in the first place.

In conclusion, I partly agree that adult literacy deserves greater investment, but this should be financed through expanded education budgets rather than reallocation away from young people. Balanced funding for both groups offers the strongest path forward for any society.
