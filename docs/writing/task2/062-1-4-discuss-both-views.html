Task 2 — University education and success (discuss-both-views)

Some people think that the best way to be successful in life is to get a university education. Others disagree and say that nowadays, this is no longer true. Discuss both views and give your own opinions.

立场: 大学并非唯一路径,但仍是重要阶梯; 理由1: 大学仍提供系统训练与人脉网络; 理由2: 创业与技能学习开辟新成功路径

Whether a university degree remains the surest route to a successful life is a question that generates sharply divided opinions. Some people still insist that higher education is the most reliable path to prosperity, while others argue that the modern economy offers many equally valid alternatives. Both views raise points that deserve careful consideration.

Supporters of the traditional view emphasise that universities provide structured knowledge, professional credentials and access to influential networks that are difficult to replicate elsewhere. A clear example is the medical profession, where a degree is not merely advantageous but strictly required before any patient can be treated. Beyond regulated fields, graduates benefit from peer connections formed during their studies, and these relationships frequently translate into job offers, partnerships and mentorships years later. For students from families without professional contacts, a university can therefore act as a powerful equaliser that opens doors otherwise closed to them.

Critics counter that today's success stories increasingly emerge from outside the lecture hall. Technology entrepreneurs, skilled tradespeople and content creators have built prosperous careers through self-learning, apprenticeships or short, intensive boot camps, often bypassing university altogether. Furthermore, in fields that change rapidly, the specific facts taught in a three-year degree can quickly become outdated, while practical skills acquired through hands-on work tend to retain value. Were the university route the only dependable path, the steady stream of successful dropouts from leading firms would be hard to explain.

Having considered both perspectives, I believe that a university degree is still a valuable stepping stone, yet no longer the single best guarantee of success. In my view, the most successful individuals are those who, regardless of their starting point, keep refining their skills and adapt quickly to whatever the labour market demands next.
