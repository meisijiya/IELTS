Task 2 — Government responsibility for childhood obesity (agree-disagree)

In many countries, children are becoming overweight and unhealthy. Some people think that the government should have the responsibility. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2022.1.22)

立场: 同意政府负有主要责任; 理由1: 食品环境与学校配餐由政策塑造; 理由2: 监管广告与体育投入需要公共资源

Childhood obesity has reached worrying levels in many countries, prompting fierce debate over who should bear the responsibility for reversing this deeply worrying trend. I firmly agree that governments must take the lead, because the daily environments in which children eat, play and grow are largely shaped by public policy rather than by individual family choice.

My first reason is that the food environment surrounding children is largely a product of government regulation, from school canteen standards to the subsidies that determine what ordinary families can afford. Where fresh fruit is taxed heavily and fast-food outlets multiply near schools, even the most careful parent struggles to provide healthy options day after day. A clear example is the success of city-wide school lunch programmes in countries like Japan and Finland, where balanced meals have been shown to lower average body weight and improve concentration in class, outcomes that home cooking alone could never have achieved on such a large scale.

My second reason is that protecting children from commercial pressure and sedentary lifestyles requires resources that only the state can realistically mobilise. Banning junk-food advertising during children's television hours, mandating daily physical education and building safe playgrounds in every neighbourhood all demand coordinated funding and clear legislation. Were these tasks to be left entirely to private charities or busy parents, the gains would be small and uneven, leaving the poorest children just as vulnerable as before.

In conclusion, I still believe that governments should accept primary responsibility for tackling childhood obesity, since only coordinated public action can reshape the food, advertising and exercise environments that are making our children unhealthy today.
