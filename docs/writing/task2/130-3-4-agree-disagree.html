Task 2 — Raise driving age (agree-disagree)

Some people believe that the best way to improve road safety is to increase the minimum legal age for driving cars and motorbikes. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2023.7.22)

立场: 不同意，提高法定年龄并非最佳方案; 理由1: 事故多发于有经验的中青年司机，年龄并非主要风险变量; 理由2: 渐进式驾照、风险教育与严格执法更直接有效

Some people argue that the surest path to safer roads is to raise the minimum legal age for driving cars and motorbikes. I disagree with this view, because age alone is a poor predictor of accident risk, and other policy tools address the real causes of collisions far more effectively in modern traffic.

The first reason is that drivers of all ages cause accidents, and there is little evidence that simply being older makes a motorist safer on the road. Insurance data from many countries shows that middle-aged drivers, not teenagers, are responsible for the largest number of collisions involving distraction or fatigue, simply because they spend the most time on the road every day. Raising the legal driving age would therefore remove a relatively small group of cautious young drivers while leaving the largest at-risk population entirely unaffected and free to keep driving as before.

The second reason is that better results come from improving driver behaviour rather than delaying licences. Graduated systems, in which new drivers must first gain experience under supervision, carry fewer passengers and avoid night-time driving for a fixed period, have dramatically reduced crash rates wherever they have been introduced across Europe and elsewhere in the developed world. Combined with stricter penalties for speeding, mobile-phone use and drink-driving, and with ongoing public-education campaigns in schools, these measures tackle the actual causes of accidents and protect road users of every age group.

In conclusion, I still believe that raising the minimum driving age is not the best way to improve road safety, because experience, training and enforcement address the underlying causes of collisions far more convincingly than a single age threshold ever can in practice.
