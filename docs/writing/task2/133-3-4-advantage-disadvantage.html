Task 2 — Vehicle-free days (advantage-disadvantage)

Some cities have vehicle-free days, when private cars, trucks and motorcycles are banned in the city centre. Public transportation like buses, taxis, and metros are advised. To what extent do you think the advantages outweigh the disadvantages? (2024.3.2)

立场: 利大于弊; 理由1: 空气质量与公共空间使用显著改善; 理由2: 缺点短暂且可控，居民逐渐适应后可形成长期行为转变

An increasing number of cities now designate occasional vehicle-free days, banning private cars, trucks and motorcycles from their centres while encouraging residents to use buses, taxis and metro services. While the policy inevitably causes short-term inconvenience, I believe the advantages clearly outweigh the disadvantages and should therefore be expanded.

The most visible benefit is the immediate improvement in air quality and the reclaiming of public space. With thousands of petrol vehicles removed for a single day, concentrations of nitrogen oxides and fine particles drop sharply, giving children, the elderly and people with asthma a rare chance to breathe cleaner urban air. Streets that normally queue with traffic can be filled with cyclists, pedestrians and outdoor café seating, demonstrating how much livelier a city becomes when cars retreat. Over time, these temporary experiments often convince residents that lower-car lifestyles are both feasible and enjoyable.

The principal drawback is the inconvenience imposed on drivers, delivery firms and patients who need urgent transport. Some shopkeepers worry about losing weekend trade, and disabled residents may struggle to reach medical appointments if alternative transport is poorly organised. These disadvantages, however, are largely short-term and can be mitigated with frequent buses, temporary taxi permits and clear advance notice. Once citizens experience the cleaner air and quieter streets for themselves, complaints typically fade and support for the policy grows, turning a one-day experiment into a long-term shift in urban culture.

In conclusion, although vehicle-free days bring real but manageable inconvenience, their lasting benefits to public health, urban life and environmental awareness far outweigh the temporary drawbacks, making the policy well worth adopting on a regular basis in many cities.
