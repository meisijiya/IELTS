Task 2 — TV watching children's behavior (discuss-both-views)

Some people say that what children watch on television influences their behavior while others say the amount of time children spend watching television influences their behavior. Discuss both views and give your opinion. (2024.2.3)

立场: 折中（内容与时长共同作用）; 理由1: 节目内容对儿童价值观与模仿行为有直接塑造作用; 理由2: 屏幕时长影响注意力、家庭互动与身体活动，二者不可偏废

Whether it is the content of television programmes or the sheer amount of time children spend watching them that most influences their behaviour continues to provoke debate. Some experts focus on what young viewers actually see, while others argue that the duration of viewing is the decisive factor.

Those who emphasise content point out that children imitate what they observe, particularly when characters they admire model aggression or cruelty. A child who regularly watches programmes where conflict is resolved through violence may come to see hitting or shouting as an acceptable response to frustration. Educational programmes, by contrast, can introduce pro-social messages about sharing, persistence and empathy that parents often struggle to teach on their own. From this perspective, the question is not how long children watch but what they absorb while they do so.

Those who emphasise viewing hours argue that quantity matters just as much as quality. Long, uninterrupted screen time reduces the chance for outdoor exercise, face-to-face conversation and the unstructured play that develops creativity. Researchers consistently find that children who watch more than a few hours a day perform worse on language tests and exhibit higher rates of attention difficulties at school. Paediatric associations now recommend strict daily screen budgets for children under five, on the grounds that any content, however benign, displaces the activities young brains need.

In my view, neither factor alone tells the whole story. Parents should curate both the content and the duration of their children's television viewing, since harmful messages in small doses and harmless content in excess can both undermine healthy development.

In conclusion, content and viewing hours each shape children's behaviour in important ways, so the most effective approach combines careful programme selection with sensible limits on screen time.
