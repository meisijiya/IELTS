Task 2 — Technology and free time (opinion)

It is predicted that with the development of technology, people in the 21st century have much more free time. To what extent has the prediction come true? Give some reasons from your perspective and experience. (2023.4.29)

立场: 仅部分成真; 理由1: 家务自动化释放出可观时间; 理由2: 数字娱乐与远程工作反向侵蚀自由时间

At the start of the 21st century, many futurologists confidently predicted that smart machines would give ordinary people far more leisure time than their grandparents ever enjoyed. Drawing on my own experience and observations of people around me, I would argue that this prediction has come true only partly, because time saved at home has been quietly absorbed by screens and the new expectations of modern working life.

The first reason the prediction holds true is that household technology has indeed removed hours of drudgery from daily life. Washing machines, robot vacuum cleaners and dishwashers mean that a family can complete essential chores in a fraction of the time their great-grandparents needed. A clear example is my own household: what used to take an entire Saturday of cleaning now finishes by mid-morning, leaving the afternoon genuinely free for hobbies, exercise or simply resting. Without these tools, working parents would have far less energy to spend quality time with their children.

The second reason the prediction falls short is that the time technology saves is constantly being captured by other digital demands. Remote-working platforms blur the boundary between office and home, so messages arrive late into the evening, while algorithm-driven video apps keep users scrolling for hours. Were these pressures to intensify, the modest gains from domestic automation would be cancelled out, and families would feel just as rushed as a generation ago, despite owning more labour-saving devices.

In conclusion, I believe the prediction has been only half fulfilled: technology has freed us from physical chores, yet it has also invented fresh demands on our attention, so the great leisure boom predicted at the start of the century remains a promise rather than a reality for many of us.
