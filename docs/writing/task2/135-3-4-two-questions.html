Task 2 — Drivers violate road rules (two-questions)

In all countries, there are laws designed to ensure road safety. However, some drivers still violate these regulations, for example, by using mobile phones while driving or by speeding. What are the reasons for this behaviour? What are the most effective solutions to this problem? (2026.2.28)

立场: 现象普遍，需要技术与制度合力; 理由1: 违规源于时间压力、手机依赖与执法薄弱; 理由2: 技术拦截、严格处罚与持续教育可以显著降低违规率

The aim of this essay is to examine why some drivers continue to break road-safety regulations such as the bans on using mobile phones while driving and on speeding, and to suggest the most effective measures that governments and communities can take to address these violations.

Several factors help to explain why road-safety laws are routinely ignored. Many drivers face constant time pressure from demanding jobs, school runs and long commutes, and they treat small violations such as sending a quick text or nudging the speedometer above the limit as harmless time-savers rather than real risks. Mobile-phone addiction makes the temptation almost irresistible, since the device is always within reach and pings constantly with notifications. Equally important is the widespread perception that enforcement is weak: when speeding cameras are rare and on-the-spot fines for phone use are uncommon, many motorists conclude that the odds of being caught are simply too low to worry about.

The most effective solutions combine technology, stricter penalties and sustained education. Modern speed cameras linked to number-plate recognition, together with in-car systems that block phone calls while the vehicle is moving, can make dangerous behaviour much harder to commit undetected. Governments should pair these tools with tougher, more visible penalties such as licence points, instant fines and temporary confiscation of the vehicle for repeat offenders. Public campaigns in schools, on social media and through driving schools can shift social norms, so that speeding and phone use behind the wheel become as socially unacceptable as drink-driving has become over recent decades.

In conclusion, road-safety violations will not disappear on their own, but a coordinated mix of technological enforcement, meaningful sanctions and long-term education offers the best chance of changing driver behaviour and saving lives.
