Task 2 — Traditional vs fast food (agree-disagree)

In many countries, traditional foods are being replaced by international fast foods. This is having a negative effect on both families and societies. To what extent do you agree or disagree with this opinion? (2021.4.24)

立场: 部分同意，健康与文化冲击是真实的，但家庭纽带并不完全依赖饮食传统; 理由1: 快餐普及损害饮食健康并弱化文化身份; 理由2: 家庭关系更多由时间与陪伴而非菜系决定

Around the world, traditional home-cooked meals are giving way to burgers, pizzas and fried chicken, and many commentators worry that this shift is harming both families and the wider community. While I partly share their concern, I would only partially agree, because the damage to health and cultural identity is real, but family bonds depend far more on shared time than on the dishes served at the table.

It is undeniable that the spread of international fast food has produced serious health and cultural costs. Frequent consumption of calorie-dense, low-nutrition meals is closely linked to childhood obesity, diabetes and heart disease, placing a heavy burden on public health systems. At the same time, traditional recipes that once carried stories of migration, harvest festivals and family history are quietly forgotten when younger generations cannot even name the ingredients. When a grandmother's special dish is replaced by a takeaway box, an important channel of cultural memory is closed.

Family relationships, however, are not chiefly sustained by cuisine. A family that gathers every Sunday to cook and eat pizza together is still bound by conversation, laughter and mutual support, while a household that insists on traditional food but rarely talks at the table may be emotionally distant. The real threats to family life are long working hours, screen addiction and urban isolation, problems that no menu change can solve. Were governments simply to ban fast food, the underlying pressures on families would remain untouched.

In conclusion, I partially agree that the rise of fast food damages public health and cultural heritage, but I still believe that family cohesion rests on shared time and attention rather than on the choice of cuisine, so the problem demands education rather than alarm.
