Task 2 — Returning historical objects (agree-disagree)

Historical objects in museums all over the world should be returned to their country of origin. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2024.9.28)

立场: 倾向同意—道德应归还; 理由1: 文化纽带不可替代、归还有助修复历史正义; 理由2: 普世博物馆论述基础不稳，结合技术与资金援助更合理

The question of whether historical objects held in museums around the world should be returned to their country of origin has become increasingly pressing. I largely agree with the call for repatriation, because the cultural ties between an artefact and its homeland are often irreplaceable, and continuing to display these items abroad risks perpetuating an outdated colonial order.

The first reason is that returning objects to their place of origin restores a cultural continuity that no foreign museum can fully replicate. The Benin Bronzes, removed from present-day Nigeria by British forces in 1897, hold religious and civic meaning that is inseparable from the court rituals for which they were originally created. When such pieces are displayed thousands of miles away, visitors see them as exotic curiosities rather than living heritage. Were the original communities to regain permanent possession, the objects could once again play their proper role in ceremonies, education and national memory.

The second reason is that the universal-museum argument, however appealing, rests on shaky moral foundations. Proponents point to the Louvre and the British Museum as global classrooms, but the public they mainly serve is composed of foreign tourists rather than the descendants of the cultures that produced the work. Continued retention can also distract attention from the harder task of supporting museums in the originating countries themselves. Were wealthy nations to combine restitution with technical and financial assistance, the objects would be both better cared for and more meaningfully appreciated.

In conclusion, I still believe that historical objects in museums around the world should largely be returned to their country of origin, because doing so restores cultural dignity, repairs historical injustice and allows the artefacts to be appreciated in their proper context.
