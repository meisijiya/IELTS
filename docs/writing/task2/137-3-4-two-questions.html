Task 2 — Phone use while driving (two-questions)

Many countries have laws banning phone use while driving. However, some drivers still violate these regulations by using mobile phones while driving. What are the reasons for this behaviour? What are the most effective solutions to this problem? (2026.6.6)

立场: 揭示习惯依赖并以严惩与教育双管齐下; 理由1: 通讯即时性与多任务习惯难以根除; 理由2: 重罚+技术锁+安全教育

The aim of this essay is to analyse why so many drivers continue to use their phones despite legal bans and to propose the most effective solutions that governments and communities can adopt to address this persistent problem.

Several reasons explain this widespread breach of the law. To begin with, mobile phones have become a constant source of instant communication, and many drivers feel unable to ignore work messages or family calls, even when they are behind the wheel. Furthermore, decades of habitual multitasking have led people to underestimate the cognitive cost of glancing at a screen while driving; a quick reply feels harmless, although it can effectively double the reaction time of an ordinary driver. Lastly, the chance of being caught is still low in many regions, which weakens the deterrent effect that any law is supposed to produce.

The most effective solutions, in my view, must combine enforcement, technology and education working in parallel. Higher fines, the immediate suspension of licences and visible roadside checks can deter offenders by raising the perceived cost of breaking the rule. At the same time, in-car systems that automatically disable non-essential notifications while the vehicle is in motion can remove the temptation altogether. Were schools and driving instructors to integrate realistic simulations of phone-related accidents into formal training, younger drivers would internalise the danger far more deeply than they do from merely reading an official leaflet.

In conclusion, the reasons behind illegal phone use are largely psychological and structural in nature, while the solutions must be technological, legal and educational at the same time if any lasting progress on the roads is to be made.
