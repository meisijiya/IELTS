Task 2 — Walking less daily (problem-solution)

Health experts claim that walking is the best exercise. However, people are walking less on a daily basis. What has made this happen and how to deal with it? (2021.3.27)

立场: 揭示汽车依赖与城市设计导致步行衰退，并提出政策与社区激励方案; 理由1: 私家车与远程办公取代短途步行; 理由2: 街道改造与步数奖励可恢复步行习惯

The aim of this essay is to examine why people are walking far less in their daily routines than previous generations did, and to suggest practical measures that could encourage citizens to put one foot in front of the other once again.

Several reasons explain this decline. The most obvious is the rapid spread of private cars: many households now own at least one vehicle, and short trips that earlier generations would have walked are routinely taken by car, even when the destination is only a few streets away. Modern urban design has compounded the problem by separating homes from shops, schools and workplaces with wide roads and vast intersections that discourage pedestrians, while suburbs without pavements leave walkers feeling unsafe. The arrival of online shopping, food delivery and remote work has further reduced the need to leave the house at all, so even stay-at-home days no longer require any meaningful movement.

Governments and communities can reverse the trend through a mix of policy and persuasion. City councils should redesign streets to favour walkers by adding shaded pavements, protected crossings and traffic-calming measures that make a short stroll feel pleasant rather than stressful. Employers and schools could introduce walking clubs, organised lunch-time strolls or step-count challenges that turn ordinary exercise into a friendly social activity. Health authorities, working with insurers, might also offer modest rebates or vouchers for citizens who consistently meet recommended daily step targets, giving busy households a tangible reason to leave the car at home.

In conclusion, the fall in daily walking is driven by cars, sprawling cities and digital convenience, and reversing it will require safer streets, social encouragement and small but real incentives for ordinary citizens.
