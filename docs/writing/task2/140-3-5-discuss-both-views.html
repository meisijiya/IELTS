Task 2 — Team sports vs individual sports (discuss-both-views)

Some people think that it is more beneficial to take part in sports which are played in teams, like football. But others think taking part in individual sports is better, like swimming. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (2021.10.09)

立场: 折中（按性格与目的选择）; 理由1: 团体运动培养合作与归属感; 理由2: 个人运动塑造自律与专注

Whether people should engage mainly in team sports such as football or in individual pursuits such as swimming remains a matter of personal preference. Some argue that team-based activities are more beneficial, while others maintain that solo sports offer superior development.

Supporters of team sports emphasise the social skills these activities cultivate. A footballer must communicate with teammates under pressure, accept a coach's instructions and share both victories and defeats without losing composure. Through repeated practice and competition, young players develop a sense of belonging, learn to manage conflict calmly and build friendships that often last into adulthood. A clear example is the way school football teams frequently become a training ground for future workplace collaboration, where trust and quick coordination under pressure are essential.

Advocates of individual sports, however, focus on the personal discipline these activities demand. A swimmer who trains alone at dawn, or a tennis player who refines serve technique without a partner, must rely entirely on self-motivation and inner focus. This kind of solitary training builds mental resilience and the ability to set personal goals, qualities that translate directly into academic study and later career progress. When something goes wrong in a solo contest, only the athlete bears the result, which teaches accountability in a way that team sports rarely can.

In my view, both kinds of sport offer valuable but distinct lessons. The best choice depends on the personality of the participant and the goals they hope to pursue.

In conclusion, team and individual sports should be seen as complementary rather than competing options, and individuals are wise to experience both at some point in life.
