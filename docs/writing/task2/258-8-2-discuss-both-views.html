Task 2 — Travel vs virtual experience (discuss-both-views)

Some people think traveling abroad is necessary while others think this is not necessary because TV and the internet can give the same information. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (2021.6.26)

立场: 倾向支持实地旅行，因感官与跨文化理解无法被屏幕复制; 理由1: 媒体足以获取知识与视觉素材; 理由2: 实地旅行带来独特感官与跨文化共情，无法替代

Whether overseas travel is still necessary in an age of high-resolution documentaries and instant internet access is a debate that resonates with many budget-conscious travellers. Some commentators now argue that television and online platforms already provide sufficient information about foreign lands, while others insist that physical travel offers irreplaceable personal value.

Those who consider foreign travel unnecessary emphasise how powerful modern media have become. A student can watch a documentary on the Amazon rainforest, take a virtual tour of the Louvre, or follow a live news feed from Tokyo without ever leaving home, gaining factual knowledge that rivals a travel brochure. For people with limited budgets, disabilities or caring responsibilities, this kind of armchair exploration is genuinely useful and removes barriers that once kept whole groups from engaging with the wider world.

Advocates of overseas travel, however, stress that information is not the same as understanding. Standing in a crowded market in Marrakech, tasting street food in Hanoi or watching the Northern Lights move across an Arctic sky delivers sensory and emotional experiences that no screen can replicate. Direct contact with people of another culture also builds humility and empathy in ways that curated videos rarely can. Were everyone to rely solely on screens, international curiosity would gradually flatten into passive consumption, and the cross-cultural friendships that emerge from genuine visits would quietly disappear.

In my view, media are an excellent supplement to travel, but they cannot replace the perspective-shifting impact of physically standing in a foreign place.

In conclusion, television and the internet do provide rich information about the world, yet overseas travel remains necessary for the emotional depth, sensory richness and authentic human contact that turn knowledge of a country into genuine understanding of its people.
