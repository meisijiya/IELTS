Task 2 — Bicycle spending for traffic (problem-solution)

Some countries have spent a large amount of money on bicycles. Why has this happened? Is it the best way to solve traffic problems? (2022.2.17)

立场: 自行车投资是积极信号但并非万能解; 理由1: 政府投资自行车反映拥堵、污染与公共健康三重压力; 理由2: 治堵需多模式配合，单押自行车存在局限

The aim of this essay is to examine why some governments now spend large sums on cycling infrastructure, and to consider whether bicycle investment alone is the best response to traffic congestion in modern cities.

Several factors help to explain the growing public investment in cycling. City centres have become choked with private cars, producing not only frustrating congestion but also severe air pollution and rising rates of respiratory illness among urban residents of all ages. At the same time, public-health authorities have grown alarmed by sedentary lifestyles, and transport planners recognise that short urban journeys of under five kilometres can be shifted from cars to bikes with relative ease. Widespread public concern about climate change adds a further incentive, since bicycles produce no emissions at all and offer one of the cheapest ways to cut a city’s carbon footprint.

Whether bicycle spending is the best solution to traffic problems is a more difficult question to settle. Cycling clearly relieves pressure on the busiest corridors, reduces emissions and encourages healthier living, but it cannot serve every commuter in every neighbourhood on its own. People with young children, elderly residents and those living in hilly suburbs or extreme climates still need buses, trains or cars for their daily journeys. A truly effective transport strategy therefore combines protected cycle lanes with frequent public transit, sensible parking policy and, where appropriate, congestion charges, so that citizens can choose the mode that best fits their journey.

In conclusion, the recent wave of bicycle investment reflects genuine urban problems and delivers real benefits to many residents, yet it works best as one element of a broader, multi-modal transport plan rather than as a stand-alone remedy for congestion in any single city.
