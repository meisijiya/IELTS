Task 2 — Young people as leaders (agree-disagree)

The leaders of directors of organizations are often older people, but some people say that young people can also be a leader. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2023.12.9)

立场: 同意, 年轻人也能胜任领导岗位; 理由1: 年轻领导者更具新视角与技术敏锐度; 理由2: 领导力关键在于能力而非年龄

In most organisations, senior leadership tends to be dominated by people well past middle age, and the assumption that young adults can also lead is rarely tested. While experience certainly matters, I agree with the view that young people can be highly effective leaders, and age alone should not be the criterion for selecting those who run a company or a country.

The first reason is that young leaders often bring fresh perspectives and an intuitive grasp of new technology. Industries from entertainment to finance are now being reshaped by digital tools, and executives who grew up alongside the internet frequently spot opportunities that older leaders miss. A clear example is the success of numerous technology start-ups founded by chief executives in their twenties, whose comfort with rapid experimentation has allowed them to outmanoeuvre longer-established rivals. Were such talent to be excluded from leadership purely on the basis of age, these organisations would lose a decisive competitive edge.

The second reason is that age does not automatically confer wisdom or judgement. Leadership depends on qualities such as empathy, decisiveness and the ability to inspire others, and many of these traits are distributed evenly across age groups. Having managed teams early in their careers, some young professionals already demonstrate the poise and strategic thinking that older colleagues take decades to acquire. Given the chance, they can lead organisations through difficult transitions just as effectively as more senior figures do.

In conclusion, I still maintain that young people can absolutely be effective leaders, and that organisations should select leaders on the basis of skill and vision rather than on the number of years they have lived.
