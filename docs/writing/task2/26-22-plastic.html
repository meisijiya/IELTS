Task 2 — Plastic waste in cities, countryside and oceans (problem-solution)

More and more plastic is polluting our cities, the countryside and the oceans. What problems does this plastic waste cause, and what measures should be taken to solve this issue? (2021.1.30)

Plastic waste is now everywhere, from busy city streets to remote farmland and the deep ocean. This essay will examine the main problems caused by plastic pollution and suggest practical measures that governments, businesses and individuals can take to reduce it.

Plastic pollution creates several serious problems. First, it harms wildlife. Sea turtles mistake plastic bags for jellyfish, seabirds feed bottle caps to their chicks, and larger pieces entangle seals and dolphins, killing thousands of animals every year. Second, plastic breaks down into micro-particles that enter the food chain: they are eaten by fish and shellfish, and eventually reach the humans who eat them. Third, plastic waste blocks city drains and rural ditches, which worsens flooding when heavy rain falls, and it spoils the appearance of beaches and countryside areas, hurting the tourism industry that many coastal communities depend on.

These problems can only be addressed through combined action. Governments should introduce a clear ban on the single-use plastic items that cause most litter, such as shopping bags, cutlery and coffee cup lids, while charging companies that still produce hard-to-recycle packaging. Supermarkets and manufacturers, in turn, need to redesign their products and use refillable or fully recyclable materials instead of mixed plastic films. Individuals can also help by refusing unnecessary plastic, sorting their household waste and joining local river or beach clean-up events. Together, these three groups can greatly reduce the amount of plastic that enters the environment.

In conclusion, plastic pollution causes real harm to wildlife, human health and the economy, but it is a problem with known solutions. With government regulation, business redesign and everyday changes in personal habits, the flow of plastic into our cities, countryside and oceans can be brought under control.
