Task 2 — Banning extreme sports (agree-disagree)

Some people think that dangerous extreme sports such as skydiving and rocks climbing should be banned. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2023.9.23)

立场: 不同意全盘禁止; 理由1: 禁令会把活动推向更不安全的地下状态; 理由2: 牌照与安全规则已足以降低风险

Some people argue that extreme sports such as skydiving and rock climbing should be banned outright because of the very serious risks they carry to both participants and rescue workers. I firmly disagree with this position, because prohibition would not eliminate the underlying desire to practise these activities and would instead expose participants to even greater, less supervised danger.

The first reason is that blanket bans tend to drive risky hobbies underground rather than abolish them altogether. When an activity is officially forbidden but remains popular among enthusiasts, those people will continue to pursue it in remote places, well beyond the reach of regulators and rescue services. A clear example is the way alcohol prohibition in several countries in the last century did not reduce drinking, but simply created unsafe black markets. The same pattern would likely repeat itself with extreme sports: training would happen in secret, equipment would go unchecked, and amateur attempts would rise sharply, producing far more injuries and fatalities than any regulated environment would tolerate.

The second reason is that well-designed safety systems can already mitigate most of the inherent risks. Mandatory instruction, certified instructors, regular equipment inspection and weather-based scheduling have made modern skydiving and rock climbing far safer than their reputations suggest. Were governments to complement these measures with stricter licensing of operators and clearer emergency protocols, the number of accidents could fall further without removing the freedom of competent adults to challenge themselves.

In conclusion, I still believe that extreme sports should be governed through strong safety frameworks rather than banned outright, because careful regulation protects lives far more effectively than blanket prohibition ever can in practice.
