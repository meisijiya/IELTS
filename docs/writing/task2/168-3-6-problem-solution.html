Task 2 — Excessive sitting harms health (problem-solution)

Nowadays, people spend a great deal of time sitting, leading to health problems. Why does this happen? What are the best ways to encourage people to sit less? (2025.8.16)

立场: 揭示办公与娱乐久坐结构性问题，并提出工作场所与城市规划方案; 理由1: 办公室工种与屏幕娱乐使久坐成为常态; 理由2: 站立式办公与步行城市规划可减少久坐

The aim of this essay is to consider why the average person in modern societies spends so many hours each day sitting down, and to identify practical measures that could persuade citizens to interrupt their sedentary habits before serious illness develops.

Two structural forces explain the modern sitting epidemic. The first is the nature of work. Office jobs, call centres and remote workforces all require employees to remain at a desk for eight hours or more, with meetings conducted on video calls, leaving little built-in opportunity to stand or walk. The second force is leisure. Streaming platforms and social media feeds are designed to keep users glued to a comfortable seat for hours, while car culture and delivery services minimise even the small amounts of walking that previous generations would have done automatically. The result is a daily rhythm in which almost every waking hour involves sitting, and the body pays the price through back pain, obesity and cardiovascular disease.

Reversing the pattern requires action in workplaces, cities and homes. Employers should provide height-adjustable desks, walking meetings and regular stretch breaks, and should reward teams that integrate movement into the working day. Urban planners can redesign transport hubs and office districts to favour walking, with shaded pavements and safe crossings that turn short journeys into natural exercise. At home, families can establish simple rules such as standing during phone calls and taking a short walk after dinner, gradually rebuilding a culture in which sitting is the exception rather than the default.

In conclusion, people sit too much because jobs and leisure are both designed around the chair, and the remedy lies in redesigning workplaces, neighbourhoods and household habits so that standing and moving become the natural choice for ordinary citizens.
