Task 2 — Art classes compulsory (single-question)

Art classes, such as painting or drawing, are as important for children's development as other subjects, so it should be compulsory in high school. Do you agree or disagree? (2024.12.14)

Many people today believe that art classes should be a compulsory part of high school alongside subjects such as mathematics, science and languages. While I accept that core academic subjects must remain central, I strongly agree that making art compulsory would benefit students in several important ways.

First, art classes help young people develop creative thinking skills that purely academic subjects often overlook. When students experiment with colour, shape and composition, they learn to see a problem from more than one angle and to come up with original solutions. This habit is increasingly valuable in modern workplaces, where routine tasks are being automated and employers look for staff who can propose fresh ideas. A student who only studies facts and formulas may pass exams yet struggle later when asked to design a product, plan an event or solve an unfamiliar problem.

Second, art classes give students an emotional outlet that other subjects rarely provide. Many teenagers experience stress from heavy study loads and constant exams, and painting, drawing and other hands-on creative activities offer them a calm space to express feelings they cannot easily put into words. Schools that have introduced regular art sessions often report better classroom atmosphere and stronger concentration in other lessons.

Admittedly, some parents worry that compulsory art classes will reduce time for the core subjects that universities and employers value most. This concern can be addressed by keeping the art component modest, perhaps one or two periods a week, so students still gain real creative and emotional benefits.

In conclusion, I firmly believe that art classes should be compulsory in high school, because they nurture creativity and well-being in ways that traditional academic subjects cannot.
