Task 2 — Academic vs practical courses (agree-disagree)

Students should be allowed to choose academic courses or practical courses, e.g. carpenters. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2022.11.19)

立场: 完全同意学生自主选择学术或实用课程; 理由1: 尊重个体天赋差异,提高学习投入; 理由2: 缓解技能人才短缺,推动经济平衡

It has been suggested that secondary and post-secondary students should be allowed to opt freely between purely academic courses and more vocational programmes such as carpentry, plumbing or electronics. I strongly agree with this proposal, because it respects individual talent while also serving the wider needs of the labour market.

The first reason is that students differ markedly in their strengths, interests and career aspirations, and forcing them all down an academic pathway wastes both public funding and personal potential. A clear example is the experience of countries such as Germany and Switzerland, where teenagers who choose apprenticeships from age sixteen consistently report higher motivation and stronger on-the-job competence than peers pushed into general academic streams that do not suit them. Allowing genuine choice ensures that a student who learns best by doing can build a satisfying career in carpentry, just as another who thrives on theory can pursue medicine or engineering.

The second reason is that practical courses address the chronic shortage of skilled trades that many economies now face. If every young person is steered towards universities, sectors such as construction, advanced manufacturing and renewable-energy installation struggle to recruit competent workers, driving up costs and delaying essential infrastructure projects. Were governments to expand practical options and treat them as equal in status to academic study, young people would be more willing to enrol, employers would gain the technicians they urgently need, and unemployment among graduates could fall at the same time.

In conclusion, I firmly believe that permitting students to choose between academic and practical tracks is in the best interest of individuals, employers and society as a whole.
