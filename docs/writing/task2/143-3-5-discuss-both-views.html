Task 2 — Taxes and civic responsibilities (discuss-both-views)

Some people think that paying taxes is enough to contribute to the society. Others argue that being a citizen involves more responsibilities. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (2023.4.15)

立场: 同意纳税只是最低门槛; 理由1: 纳税无法替代日常公民参与; 理由2: 公共生活需要邻里互助与志愿服务

Whether paying taxes is sufficient as a form of social contribution, or whether citizens should accept wider obligations, remains a contentious question. Some argue that financial support through taxation is already a heavy enough duty, while others insist that good citizenship demands much more than a tax return each year.

Those who consider taxes sufficient emphasise the practical value of compulsory financial contributions. Roads, hospitals, schools and pensions are all funded from the public purse, and most citizens already feel burdened by the proportion of income that disappears in deductions. From this perspective, asking people to volunteer extra hours when they have already paid their share risks breeding resentment, and the state should not exploit the goodwill of ordinary workers who simply want to enjoy their private time after long working days.

Advocates of broader civic duty, however, point out that a healthy society cannot run on money alone. Public meetings need citizens who attend and speak, neighbourhood safety depends on people who watch out for one another, and charitable organisations rely on volunteers willing to give their time. A clear example is the way local libraries, food banks and youth sports teams often survive only because parents and retired residents contribute unpaid labour. Were every citizen to limit their involvement to paying taxes, the social fabric would quietly unravel.

In my view, taxes are the minimum entry fee of citizenship, not the full ticket. A functioning democracy requires the active engagement of its members beyond any financial contribution.

In conclusion, I believe that paying taxes is a necessary but far from sufficient way of serving society, and citizens should welcome additional forms of participation wherever possible.
