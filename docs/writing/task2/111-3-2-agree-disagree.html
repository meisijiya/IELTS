Task 2 — Learn a culture via language (agree-disagree)

The best way to learn a culture is to learn its language. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2021.5.20 / 2025.8.30)

立场: 不同意为唯一最佳途径; 理由1: 语言是文化重要载体; 理由2: 但饮食、习俗、艺术同样不可或缺

Some people believe that the most effective way to understand a foreign culture is to master its language. I disagree with this being the single best approach, because language is only one of several gateways into a culture, and a learner who relies on it alone will inevitably miss important dimensions of daily life and shared meaning.

The first reason I acknowledge the importance of language is that words carry the history, humour and values of the people who speak them. Idioms, proverbs and folk tales reveal how earlier generations made sense of the world, and being able to read original literature gives access to emotional layers that subtitles simply cannot convey. A clear example is the difference between reading a Chinese poem in translation and appreciating its tonal patterns in the original, where nuances of mood and allusion depend entirely on the listener's grasp of Mandarin.

The second reason I stop short of full agreement is that culture also lives in food, rituals, music and the unspoken rules of social interaction that no textbook fully explains. Sharing a meal with a local family, joining a religious festival or watching a traditional dance teaches participants what it feels like to belong, in ways that vocabulary lists never can. Were learners to spend years studying a language but never set foot in a community that speaks it, they would understand the dictionary while remaining blind to the heartbeat of the culture it represents.

In conclusion, I still believe that language is a powerful gateway to culture, but it is not by itself the best or the only route, since everyday customs, shared rituals and lived experience complete what words alone can only begin to describe for learners.
