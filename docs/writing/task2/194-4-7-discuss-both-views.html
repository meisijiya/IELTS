Task 2 — Individual vs collective environmental action (discuss-both-views)

Some people think that environmental problems are too big for individuals to solve. Others, however, believe that these problems cannot be solved if individuals do not take action. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (2021.12.11)

立场: 政府、企业与个人必须联动, 单靠任何一方都不够; 理由1: 个人减排相对全社会碳排微不足道, 真正的杠杆在国家立法与工业转型; 理由2: 没有公众参与, 政策缺乏执行基础, 公众诉求与日常选择能够塑造市场与推动立法。

Whether ordinary individuals can really make a difference to environmental problems, or whether those problems are too large for personal action, is one of the most debated questions. Both views contain elements of truth, and any convincing answer must recognise the distinctive role each level of society can play.

Those who doubt individual action point out that global emissions are dominated by energy systems, heavy industry and agriculture, sectors ordinary citizens cannot influence directly. A family that switches off lights shrinks its own footprint only marginally, while a single coal-fired plant continues to release millions of tonnes of carbon every year. Meaningful change therefore requires binding international agreements, ambitious national legislation and industrial transformation, and focusing on personal virtue risks distracting voters from systemic reforms that actually matter.

Advocates of individual responsibility counter that governments move only when pressured by informed citizens, and markets change only when consumers change their habits. Recycling, cycling, eating less meat and insulating homes are not symbolic gestures: across millions of households they significantly shift demand for energy, packaging and agricultural land. The clearest example is the rapid fall in single-use plastic bag consumption after consumer charges were introduced. Without sustained public engagement, environmental policies lose democratic legitimacy and even well-designed laws fail at the point of enforcement.

In my view, neither approach is sufficient alone. Governments and industries must create the framework for low-carbon choices, while individuals must use these choices to demonstrate demand and hold leaders accountable. The combination of collective rules and personal commitment is what finally shifts outcomes at scale.

In conclusion, individual action and systemic reform are partners rather than rivals. Real progress requires laws that set ambitious limits and citizens who make those limits work in everyday life.
