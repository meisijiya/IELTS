Task 2 — Personal data online (positive-negative)

Today, many people put their personal and private information online (address, phone number, and credit card number) to facilitate everyday activities, such as banking, shopping, and socializing. Is this a positive or negative development? (2022.3.3)

立场: 倾向负面，整体风险大于便利; 理由1: 便利与效率提升; 理由2: 风险大于收益因隐私泄露与诈骗激增

Many people today upload personal details such as home addresses, phone numbers and credit card information in order to enjoy smoother banking, shopping and socialising online. While these conveniences are genuinely attractive, I would argue that, on the whole, the practice of placing private data on the internet is a negative development because the risks to security and dignity far outweigh the practical benefits.

The first benefit is convenience. Online banking lets users transfer money at midnight, e-commerce platforms remember their favourite sizes, and social media help friends locate one another across continents. Without sharing such data, modern life would involve more paperwork, longer queues and far less choice. A clear example is the way a single online form now allows a customer to apply for a mortgage, compare insurance and order groceries in just a few minutes, a sequence that once demanded separate visits to banks, brokers and shops.

The drawbacks, however, are severe. Once personal data is uploaded, it can be harvested, sold and exploited by companies the user has never heard of, and serious data breaches regularly expose millions of records to criminal markets. Identity theft, fraudulent loans and targeted phishing messages have become routine annoyances for ordinary households. Continuous tracking also undermines personal dignity: advertising algorithms construct detailed profiles that follow users from one site to another, and even law-abiding citizens feel surveilled in their own homes. While stronger privacy laws and security tools help, they cannot fully restore control over information that has already been shared.

In conclusion, sharing personal details online brings genuine efficiency, yet the combination of large-scale data leaks, identity theft and constant digital surveillance makes the trend, on balance, a negative one for individual privacy and public trust.
