Task 2 — Sacrifice for the environment (single-question)

The key to solving environmental problems is simple: the present generation is to accept a less comfortable life for the sake of the future generation. To what extent do you agree or disagree? （2021.12.4）

Many people today argue that environmental problems can only be solved if the present generation is willing to accept a less comfortable life in order to leave a safer world for their children and grandchildren. While I accept that some inconvenience is necessary, I disagree with the view that a simple reduction in personal comfort is the real key to solving these problems.

There are two main reasons why I hold this position. First, the most serious environmental threats, such as climate change and the loss of biodiversity, are caused largely by industrial production, energy generation and large-scale farming, not by ordinary household decisions. Asking individuals to accept smaller cars, colder homes or shorter holidays may raise public awareness, yet it does very little to lower the actual level of pollution, which is decided in boardrooms and government ministries. Second, a focus on personal sacrifice can distract attention from the policy changes that would really make a difference, such as clear carbon pricing, stricter rules on polluting industries and larger investment in renewable energy.

I do accept that personal choices still matter. Reducing unnecessary travel, wasting less food and choosing energy-efficient products are all sensible habits, and they send an important signal to businesses about what consumers really want. Nevertheless, these everyday actions work best when they are supported by strong government action, not when they are expected to replace it.

In conclusion, although the present generation should certainly be ready to make some changes to protect the environment, I do not believe that accepting a less comfortable life on its own is the key to solving environmental problems. Real progress depends far more on collective policy decisions than on individual sacrifice.
