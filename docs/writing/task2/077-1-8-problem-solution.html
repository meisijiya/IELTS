Task 2 — Harder to study at university (problem-solution)

Many students find it harder to study when they are at the university or college than when they were at school. Why is this? What can be done to solve the problem? (2024.10.19)

立场: 问题有多重根源，可以缓解; 理由1: 大学要求自主学习、时间管理与深入钻研，与中学被动听课模式差距大; 理由2: 高校应提供过渡性辅导（学习方法、时间管理），学生本人也应主动寻求帮助。

The aim of this essay is to explain why many students experience greater difficulty studying at university than at school, and to propose some practical solutions to ease this transition.

Several factors account for the academic struggles that new undergraduates often report. The most obvious is the sudden shift from highly structured school life to the largely self-directed environment of a university. Whereas teachers at school chase up missed homework, mark attendance and constantly remind students of deadlines, university lecturers typically expect learners to plan their own reading, revise independently and seek help only when truly necessary. Compounding this, the volume and difficulty of material increase sharply, while social distractions such as new friends and part-time jobs compete fiercely for attention. Students who never learned how to organise their own time during school often flounder as a result.

Fortunately, the problem can be addressed by a combination of institutional and personal measures. Universities should offer compulsory first-year modules on study skills, covering time management, academic writing and exam techniques, which would smooth the transition for new arrivals considerably. Pairing each freshman with a slightly older mentor would also provide informal guidance that formal lectures cannot. On the personal side, students themselves can mitigate these difficulties by attending every class, forming study groups and using campus writing centres whenever they feel overwhelmed by the workload. Without such deliberate effort, even bright students risk underperforming badly in their first year.

In conclusion, the gap between school and university study explains why so many undergraduates struggle at first. With structured support from institutions and disciplined habits from students themselves, this difficulty can be substantially reduced.
