Task 2 — TV content vs screen time (discuss-both-views)

Some people say that what children watch on television influences their behavior, while others say the amount of time children spend watching television influences their behavior. Discuss both views and give your opinion. (2024.2.3)

The question of whether television influences children more through what they watch or through how long they watch has drawn wide public attention. While some argue that the content of programmes is what really shapes a child's behaviour, others believe the number of hours spent in front of the screen is the more important factor. This essay will discuss both views and give my own opinion.

Those who emphasise content point out that young viewers are highly impressionable. A child who regularly watches cartoons filled with violent or aggressive acts may begin to treat shouting and hitting as normal ways of solving problems. By contrast, programmes that include storytelling, scientific experiments or clear moral lessons can teach cooperation, patience and curiosity. In this view, parents should select programmes carefully rather than simply limit viewing time, because a few hours of poor-quality content can do more harm than a long stretch of well-chosen programmes.

Others, however, stress that it is the sheer volume of screen time that does the damage. When children sit in front of the television for several hours every day, they have less time for physical exercise, face-to-face play and homework. Even if every programme is educational, the long hours still lead to obesity, shorter attention spans and weaker communication skills. From this perspective, parents should set a clear daily limit and remove the set from the bedroom, whatever is being broadcast.

In my opinion, both views carry weight, yet I lean towards the content side. A well-chosen programme watched for an hour often benefits a child more than a mediocre programme watched for three. Parents therefore need to combine careful programme selection with reasonable time limits.
