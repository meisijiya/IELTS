Task 2 — Employment skills vs academic study

Some people believe that university education should focus on skills of employment for the future. Others think they should focus on academic study only. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (2021.6.12)

The question of whether universities should focus on employment skills or pure academic study has sparked considerable debate. While some argue that higher education ought to prepare graduates for the job market, others believe that universities should remain devoted to traditional academic disciplines. This essay will discuss both views and offer my own opinion.

Those who favour an employment-focused curriculum point out that most students enrol in university with the aim of securing a stable career afterwards. In practice, graduates who have only studied abstract theory often struggle when they enter the workplace, because employers usually expect candidates to demonstrate practical abilities such as teamwork, communication and basic technical literacy. By integrating internships and case studies into the syllabus, universities can shorten the gap between campus learning and real working life. A business student who has managed a mock marketing campaign during college is likely to adapt to a junior position faster than one who has only memorised textbook models.

Advocates of pure academic study, however, emphasise that universities are first and foremost places of intellectual exploration. Subjects such as philosophy, literature and theoretical physics may not lead directly to a specific job, yet they cultivate critical thinking, independent reasoning and a broad understanding of human knowledge — qualities that any profession ultimately relies on. If higher education becomes too vocational, students may lose the chance to discover interests outside the obvious career path, which can actually limit their long-term personal growth.

In my view, neither approach is sufficient on its own. Universities should keep a strong academic core while introducing clearly employment-related components, so that students graduate with both the ability to think independently and the practical competence required by today's labour market.
