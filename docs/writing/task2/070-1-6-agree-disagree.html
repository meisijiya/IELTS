Task 2 — Family values vs school skills (agree-disagree)

The values that we learn from our parents and family have greater influence on our future success than knowledge and skills we learn at school. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2024.11.2)

立场: 基本同意，家庭价值观对长期成功影响更深; 理由1: 家庭塑造的品格、习惯与态度是从小内化的，直接决定职场与人际表现; 理由2: 学校知识易过时，但价值观的影响贯穿终身。

Some people claim that the values absorbed from parents and family exert a deeper influence on long-term success than the knowledge and skills acquired at school. I largely agree with this view, because the character traits formed in early childhood tend to shape every subsequent decision a person makes.

The first reason family values carry such weight is that they are internalised during the earliest and most impressionable years of life. A child who grows up observing honesty, perseverance and respect for others gradually absorbs these principles through daily interaction rather than formal instruction. By the time this child enters the workforce, such traits have already become second nature, guiding how he or she handles colleagues, clients and ethical dilemmas. School, by contrast, can only transmit information and techniques during fixed lesson hours, leaving far less opportunity for the deeper conditioning that family life provides.

In addition, the practical knowledge taught in school tends to deteriorate in relevance over time, whereas core values retain their influence throughout a lifetime. A medical student may forget half of the textbook content learned in the first year, yet the discipline and humility instilled by his parents will continue to govern his conduct as a doctor. Were educational success to depend solely on academic skills, countless highly qualified graduates would not struggle to find fulfilling careers, a pattern that illustrates how powerfully character shapes professional outcomes.

In conclusion, I firmly believe that family values, though often overlooked in public debate, exert a more lasting influence on future success than the technical knowledge gained at school. Schools should therefore work with families, not in place of them, to nurture the next generation.
