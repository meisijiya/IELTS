Task 2 — Why some work harder (problem-solution)

In education and employment, some people work harder than others. Why do some people work harder than others? Is it always a good thing to work hard? (2021.6.19)

立场: 主因为内在动机，但过度努力不一定好; 理由1: 目标感与成长型思维推动努力; 理由2: 过劳损害健康与创造力

The aim of this essay is to analyse why some individuals consistently work harder than others in education and the workplace, and to consider whether such relentless effort is always beneficial to their lives.

Several factors explain the difference in effort. The most powerful is internal motivation: people who have clear goals, a growth mindset and a sense of purpose tend to push themselves further than those who work mainly to satisfy external demands. A student aiming for medical school, for example, will usually spend far longer on revision and clinical reading than a classmate who studies only to pass the next exam. Beyond personal drive, environmental influences matter just as much: supportive families, inspiring teachers and competitive peers all raise the standard that individuals quietly set for themselves every day. A clear example is the visible difference in effort between students who attend well-resourced schools with high expectations and those who study in neglected institutions where discipline is rarely reinforced.

Hard work, however, is not an unqualified virtue. When effort tips into overwork, it can deteriorate mental and physical health, leading to burnout, strained relationships and even premature resignation from the profession that the person once loved. Continuous exertion without adequate rest also narrows creativity, because exhausted minds rarely produce the kind of original insight that emerges from relaxed contemplation. Were overwork to become the cultural norm in a society, long-term productivity would suffer and family life would quietly unravel.

In conclusion, people work harder mainly because of inner motivation and supportive surroundings, yet moderation and balance are essential if hard work is to remain genuinely beneficial in the long run.
