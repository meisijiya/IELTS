Task 2 — Scientific research by governments (agree-disagree)

Scientific research should be carried out and controlled by governments rather than private companies. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2023.10.21)

立场: 部分同意, 政府应主导并监管; 理由1: 基础研究回报慢, 私人资本不愿承担; 理由2: 政府监管可防止危害公众安全的研究

There is a compelling argument that scientific research should be led and supervised by governments rather than left to private companies. While I partly agree with this position, I would also argue that a healthy partnership between public bodies and private laboratories often produces the very best results, provided the state keeps strict regulatory control.

My first reason is that many fields of science are simply too slow or too uncertain to attract private investment. Decades of patient work in particle physics, astronomy or tropical-disease medicine rarely produce an immediately marketable product, yet they generate the discoveries that later underpin entire industries. A clear example is the publicly funded research into mRNA technology that ultimately enabled private firms to produce Covid-19 vaccines at astonishing speed. Without sustained government backing, scientists would have abandoned such inquiries long before the breakthrough arrived.

My second reason is that public supervision provides essential protection against research that could pose a threat to ordinary citizens. Private firms under shareholder pressure may be tempted to cut corners on safety, hide unfavourable trial results or experiment with risky technologies that pollute the environment or compromise the privacy of users. Where governments fund laboratories, demand open publication and enforce rigorous ethical review, citizens can trust that progress will not come at the cost of their health. Were such oversight to disappear, scandals on the scale of past pharmaceutical disasters would surely recur.

In conclusion, I still believe that governments should play the leading role in funding, guiding and regulating scientific research, even if some collaboration with skilled private teams remains useful in carefully selected areas of applied science.
