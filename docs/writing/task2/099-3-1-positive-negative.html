Task 2 — Cities replace farmland (positive-negative)

Many cities replace farmlands and parks with houses. Is it positive or negative? (2022.3.19)

立场: 消极发展; 理由1: 农田与绿地消失危及生态与食物供应; 理由2: 居民失去亲近自然空间损害身心健康

The phenomenon of cities bulldozing farmlands and public parks to make room for new housing estates has become increasingly common in many rapidly urbanising countries. From my perspective, this trend is largely a negative development, and I will outline my main reasons below.

The first reason is that the disappearance of farmland and green space poses a serious threat to both ecological balance and long-term food security for the surrounding region. Every hectare of fertile soil that is paved over reduces a city's ability to source fresh produce locally, raises dependence on distant imports, and destroys habitats that birds, insects and small mammals rely on for survival. When these green patches vanish, urban heat islands intensify, stormwater drains become overwhelmed during heavy rain, and the air that residents breathe steadily deteriorates, an outcome that no amount of extra housing can realistically compensate for.

The second reason is that residents gradually lose the natural spaces where they can recover from the daily pressures of urban life. Parks provide cheap, accessible exercise, social contact across generations and a calm setting for children's play, all of which are now treated as luxuries rather than essential public services. A clear example is the rapid loss of community gardens in fast-growing Asian cities, where recent health surveys show rising rates of childhood obesity, sleep disorders and stress-related illness in districts that have sacrificed their greenery for tower blocks.

In summary, I consider the replacement of farmlands and parks with housing to be a negative development, because it damages the environment and quietly undermines the physical and mental health of the very people it claims to serve.
