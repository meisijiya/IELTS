Task 2 — Four language skills (agree-disagree)

When learning a foreign language, the four skills (listening, speaking, reading and writing) are equally important. Do you agree or disagree? (2021.1.23)

立场: 大体同意四项技能同等重要; 理由1: 输入与输出必须并重才能形成内化能力; 理由2: 不同场景对技能需求互补,缺一不可

It is often claimed that when learners acquire a foreign language, the four core skills of listening, speaking, reading and writing deserve equal weight in the classroom. While I accept that a learner's immediate goals can sometimes shift the balance between them, I broadly agree with this position, because genuine fluency only emerges when all four skills are developed together.

The first reason is that input and output skills reinforce each other in ways that make balanced practice essential. Reading and listening expose learners to vocabulary, sentence patterns and authentic pronunciation, while speaking and writing force them to produce language under pressure, a process that consolidates what they have absorbed. A clear example is the experience of immigrants who, after years of silent reading in English, still struggle to hold a casual conversation: their passive knowledge has grown, but without regular speaking practice the active side of their competence has atrophied. Conversely, learners who speak fluently but rarely read or write soon find their grammar and spelling deteriorating.

The second reason is that real-life situations call on the four skills in constantly changing combinations. Following a film requires listening; replying to a colleague's email requires writing; reading a foreign newspaper demands comprehension; and ordering food in a restaurant requires confident speech. Were any of these abilities to be neglected, learners would struggle with routine encounters abroad or in a multinational workplace. Furthermore, employers increasingly expect staff to handle meetings, draft reports and read contracts in English, so strong performance across all four skills has become a practical necessity.

In conclusion, I believe that listening, speaking, reading and writing are all indispensable components of foreign-language mastery, and that a serious learner should cultivate them in parallel rather than in isolation.
