Task 2 — Research funding (agree-disagree)

Scientific research should be founded by the government, not commercial organizations. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2022.9.15)

立场: 部分同意; 理由1: 政府资助保障公共利益; 理由2: 商业投入可加速应用转化

Some people argue that scientific research should be funded entirely by the government rather than by commercial organisations. While I agree that public money must remain the backbone of basic science, I disagree with the claim that companies should be excluded, because private capital can usefully complement state funding and accelerate the path from laboratory to everyday life.

The first reason is that government grants protect the intrinsic value of curiosity-driven inquiry. Research into pure mathematics, particle physics or tropical diseases rarely produces a quick profit, and pharmaceutical firms have little incentive to invest in therapies for rare conditions. When state agencies fund such projects, they keep the entire scientific community honest and guarantee that findings are published openly, instead of being locked away as trade secrets. Were this public role to disappear, the long-term pipeline of ideas that underpins later innovation would quickly deteriorate.

The second reason is that commercial sponsorship exerts a healthy influence on applied research. Private firms possess manufacturing plants, distribution networks and skilled engineers, all of which shorten the gap between a successful experiment and a usable product that ordinary citizens can actually buy. Without their involvement, vaccines, semiconductors and renewable energy devices would take far longer to reach ordinary consumers, and many life-saving treatments might never leave the laboratory. A compelling argument therefore exists for allowing business to fund clearly defined applied projects, provided that the underlying methodology and data remain transparent.

In conclusion, I believe that public money should lead basic research while companies contribute to applied work, because both sectors possess strengths the other lacks, and a sensible policy will let each side concentrate on what it does best.
