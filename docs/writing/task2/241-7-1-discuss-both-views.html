Task 2 — Can anyone create art (discuss-both-views)

Some people believe that every human can create art. Other people think that art can only be created by people with special talents. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (2024.2.24)

立场: 倾向创作普世—多数人能创作、但天赋影响高度; 理由1: 创造力分布均匀、练习与教育能培养; 理由2: 顶尖艺术仍需天赋，例证莫扎特/毕加索

Many people disagree about who is capable of creating art. Some argue that every human being can create, given sufficient encouragement and training, while others insist that genuine artistic creation demands an innate talent that only a few possess. This essay will examine both views before giving my own opinion.

Those who believe anyone can create art point to the wide range of activities that fall under the term. Cooking, gardening and handicrafts involve choices about colour and form, and millions practise them daily. Research also suggests that creative ability is more evenly distributed than the public assumes: with patient guidance, most children can learn to draw portraits or compose simple melodies. Were society to drop the assumption that art belongs only to the gifted, far more people would enjoy the confidence that creative work brings.

Those who insist on the importance of talent raise a compelling argument. The symphonies that move millions to tears and the paintings that command record prices share a mastery and originality that practice alone rarely produces. Innate gifts such as perfect pitch or unusually sharp sensitivity to colour appear repeatedly in the biographies of great artists. Were talent irrelevant, anyone could become a Mozart or a Picasso through lessons alone, yet history shows this has never happened.

In my view, both positions capture something true. Almost everyone can create art in some form, yet the heights that distinguish outstanding work seem to require innate gifts alongside disciplined effort. Schools should encourage everyone to create, while remaining realistic that excellence will always be rarer than participation.

In conclusion, ordinary creativity is widely accessible, but exceptional artistry depends on a blend of talent and training that only a minority will ever achieve.
