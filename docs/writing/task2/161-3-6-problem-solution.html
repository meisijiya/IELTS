Task 2 — Rise in anti-social behaviour (problem-solution)

Many people think there is a general increase in anti-social behaviour and a lack of respect for others. What do you think are the causes of this and how to improve it? What solutions can you suggest? (2021.9.19)

立场: 揭示家庭弱化与媒体影响削弱相互尊重，并提出教育与社区治理方案; 理由1: 家庭时间减少与榜样缺失削弱尊重教育; 理由2: 学校课程与社区活动可恢复公共礼仪

The aim of this essay is to analyse why so many people feel that anti-social behaviour and a lack of respect for others have become more common in modern life, and to consider what schools, families and governments can do to reverse this disturbing trend.

Two underlying forces explain the decline in everyday courtesy. The first is the steady weakening of family influence: long working hours, frequent relocation and the constant distraction of personal devices mean that parents spend less time modelling polite behaviour or resolving conflicts calmly. Children therefore grow up without the everyday examples that earlier generations absorbed at the kitchen table. The second force is the media environment. Public figures routinely insult rivals on television, and many popular programmes reward humiliation, sarcasm and confrontation as entertaining. Young viewers easily absorb these patterns and reproduce them in classrooms and on social media.

Improving the situation requires deliberate action at several levels. Schools should teach character and civility as seriously as they teach mathematics, with structured lessons on empathy, conflict resolution and the consequences of thoughtless words. Local councils can fund community projects that bring neighbours together through shared gardens, street festivals or volunteer schemes, rebuilding the casual familiarity that discourages hostile behaviour. Governments could also support parenting programmes that help busy adults develop the listening and disciplinary skills their own children need. Sensible regulation of broadcasting and online content would reduce the supply of crude role models without resorting to censorship of ordinary speech.

In conclusion, the rise in anti-social behaviour stems from weakened families and aggressive media, and the remedy lies in teaching empathy at school, rebuilding neighbourly ties and insisting on public standards of civility in both real life and the digital world.
