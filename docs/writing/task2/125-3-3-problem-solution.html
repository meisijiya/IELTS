Task 2 — Older people exercise barriers (problem-solution)

Doctors recommend that older people exercise regularly. However, many older people do not exercise enough. What could be the reason for this? What can be done to encourage them to do physical activities? (2023.6.4)

立场: 障碍客观存在，可通过针对性措施解决; 理由1: 健康担忧、交通不便与缺乏同龄社群使老人回避运动; 理由2: 社区组织低强度课程、家庭医生开具运动处方能显著提高参与

The aim of this essay is to examine why many older adults fail to take enough physical exercise despite medical advice, and to discuss what governments, communities and families can do to encourage them to become more active.

Several factors help to explain why older people avoid regular exercise. Chronic conditions such as arthritis, osteoporosis and heart problems make vigorous activity feel risky, and many older adults fear that a fall or a strained joint will lead to a long hospital stay. Access is another barrier: gyms tend to be designed for younger users, public transport is often inconvenient in suburban areas, and walking routes may lack safe pavements or seating. Finally, motivation collapses when exercise becomes a solitary chore; without friends or family to walk with, many older people give up long before the habit takes root in their daily lives.

The most effective solutions combine medical reassurance, community programming and family involvement. Family doctors can write specific exercise prescriptions, referring patients to classes tailored for their conditions rather than offering vague advice to “keep fit”. Local councils can subsidise gentle group activities such as tai chi, water aerobics or short neighbourhood walks, and they can provide free transport to community centres for those with mobility problems. Families, meanwhile, can offer to exercise together, transforming exercise from a lonely task into a shared routine that older relatives genuinely enjoy.

In conclusion, the gap between medical advice and actual behaviour among older people is real, but it is not insurmountable. Targeted medical guidance, accessible community programmes and family encouragement together offer the most promising path towards a more active older population.
