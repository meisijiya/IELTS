Task 2 — College or work after high school (discuss-both-views)

Some young people decide to go to college or university. Others, however, choose to go straight to work as they think it is important to satisfy the needs of society and the country. Discuss both views and write your opinion. (2026.3.14)

立场: 倾向于先工作再深造; 理由1: 工作积累真实经验与社会责任感; 理由2: 大学应建立在实践基础上效果更佳

Whether young people should proceed directly to college or join the workforce immediately after high school remains a practical dilemma in many countries. Some argue that higher education is the natural next step, while others maintain that entering work early helps meet urgent social and economic needs.

Those who favour going straight to work emphasise the maturity and practical awareness that early employment fosters. Young people who earn their own money learn to manage a household budget, to cooperate with colleagues from different backgrounds and to accept responsibility for the quality of their output. A clear example is the way apprentices in trades such as plumbing or software testing often develop sharper professional judgement than peers who spend the same years mostly in lecture halls. In communities facing skill shortages, the early contribution of these young workers is genuinely valuable.

Advocates of university study, on the other hand, point out that formal education equips young adults with theoretical frameworks and broad knowledge that cannot be acquired on the shop floor. University exposes students to research methods, critical analysis and the company of ambitious peers, all of which shape their long-term trajectory. Were every student to choose immediate employment, societies would lose the doctors, engineers and policy analysts who require years of structured learning before they can serve the public.

In my view, the more compelling path is to work first and then study later, because practical experience gives university education a sharper focus and a stronger sense of purpose.

In conclusion, both routes can be valuable, but combining work and study in sequence tends to produce citizens who are both capable and socially grounded.
