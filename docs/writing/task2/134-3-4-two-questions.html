Task 2 — Bicycle ease spending (two-questions)

Some countries spend a lot of money to make bicycle use easier. Why is this? Is this the best solution to traffic congestion? (2024.10.12)

立场: 投资有意义但不是唯一答案; 理由1: 自行车友好政策反映拥堵、污染与公共健康压力; 理由2: 单凭自行车无法根治拥堵，需要公共交通与需求管理共同发力

The aim of this essay is to discuss why some governments spend substantial sums on making bicycle use easier, and to evaluate whether such investment is the best solution to traffic congestion in modern cities.

The first question has a fairly clear answer. Rising urban congestion, deteriorating air quality and growing public-health concerns about sedentary lifestyles have pushed many governments to encourage cycling as a quick, cheap and zero-emission alternative to driving. Building protected cycle lanes, installing bike-sharing stations and offering subsidies for electric bicycles all help to make two-wheeled travel safer and more appealing, while signalling a broader commitment to climate-friendly transport. Voters who regularly cycle also tend to demand such facilities, turning the policy into a popular political issue in many cities, and encouraging neighbouring regions to follow suit over time.

The second question is harder to resolve. Cycling infrastructure certainly eases pressure on the busiest urban corridors, reduces tailpipe emissions and improves residents’ fitness, yet on its own it cannot solve congestion for everyone in modern cities. People with disabilities, parents of young children, elderly residents and those living in hilly suburbs or extreme climates still require buses, trains or cars to meet their daily needs comfortably. The most effective answer combines generous cycling investment with frequent public transit, sensible parking policy and, where appropriate, congestion charges, so that commuters can choose the mode that best fits their journey to work or school each day.

In conclusion, the wave of bicycle spending reflects genuine urban problems and delivers real benefits to many residents, but it works best as one element of a broader transport strategy rather than as a single remedy for congestion in any major city.
