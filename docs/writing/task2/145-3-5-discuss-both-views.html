Task 2 — Dependent or independent individuals (2024) (discuss-both-views)

Some people think individuals are more and more dependent on each other. Some people think individuals are more and more independent. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (2024.7.6)

立场: 二者并存且同步加深; 理由1: 全球供应链与服务业加深协作依赖; 理由2: 个人选择空间与生活路径前所未有的宽广

Whether modern individuals are becoming more dependent on one another or more independent is once again a topic of lively debate. Some commentators emphasise the deepening ties between people, while others insist that personal freedom has never been greater.

Those who stress growing interdependence point to the way global supply chains and service economies now knit the entire world together. A simple breakfast may involve farmers, long-haul truckers, factory workers, supermarket staff and app developers, all of whom must coordinate for the meal to appear on the table. Specialised professions have made people increasingly reliant on the competence of strangers, and the COVID-19 crisis exposed how fragile this web becomes when a single link breaks. From this perspective, modern life is a vast collaboration in which independence is a comforting illusion.

Advocates of growing independence, by contrast, focus on the unprecedented range of choices open to the individual. People can study online, work remotely, invest through global platforms, date across borders and choose lifestyles that would have astonished their grandparents. Compared with previous generations, individuals no longer have to obey the family trade, the local religion or the village expectation, and they can construct personal identities that genuinely belong to them. A clear example is the rapid rise of digital nomads who deliberately design their working lives around travel rather than around any single employer.

In my view, both observations capture a genuine trend, and the two are mutually reinforcing rather than mutually exclusive.

In conclusion, I believe that individuals today are simultaneously more connected and more free, and that the apparent contradiction reflects the dual nature of modern technology.
