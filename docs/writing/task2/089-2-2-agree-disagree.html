Task 2 — Senior manager salaries (agree-disagree)

Some people think it is a good thing for senior managers to have much higher salaries than the other workers in a company. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2024.8.17)

立场: 不同意过高差距; 理由1: 削弱团队凝聚力与一线士气; 理由2: 损害组织公平与长期信任

It is sometimes argued that senior managers should be paid salaries that are several times higher than those of ordinary employees, on the grounds that their decisions carry greater strategic responsibility for the organisation. I strongly disagree with this view, because such a steep pay gap undermines team cohesion and erodes the sense of fairness that any healthy company requires in order to function effectively.

The first reason is that excessive executive pay damages morale and teamwork at every level of the company. When junior staff learn that a chief executive earns several hundred times their own salary, even after factoring in their longer hours and higher qualifications, it is difficult for them to feel that their efforts are genuinely valued by the people above them. A clear example of this can be seen in the retail sector, where widespread reports of huge executive bonuses have repeatedly coincided with strikes over low wages on the shop floor and with higher rates of staff turnover. The second reason is that extreme salary inequality weakens the trust that holds a company together, a benefit that balanced pay structures tend to preserve over many years. Employees who perceive their workplace as fundamentally fair are more willing to share ideas, accept difficult assignments and remain loyal during economic downturns. Were the gap between top and bottom to widen further, that trust would deteriorate, and the very managers receiving the high salaries would find their boldest decisions harder to implement day by day.

In conclusion, I firmly believe that paying senior managers vastly more than other workers is unwise, because it corrodes cooperation across the firm and undermines the mutual trust on which long-term commercial success depends.
