Task 2 — TV watching children development (discuss-both-views)

Some people think watching TV is bad for children in every way. Others think it is good for developing children as they grow up. Discuss both these views and give your own opinion. (2022.12.3)

立场: 折中（适度有益，过量有害）; 理由1: 优质节目能扩展知识面并激发好奇心; 理由2: 屏幕时间过长影响视力、注意力与户外活动

Whether television is fundamentally harmful or genuinely useful for children continues to divide parents and educators. Some insist that watching TV damages young viewers in every respect, while others argue that carefully chosen programmes can support healthy development.

Those who emphasise the benefits of television point out that high-quality programmes expose children to ideas, languages and places they would never otherwise encounter. A documentary about marine biology, for example, can spark a lifelong interest in science, while a well-made historical drama can introduce complex moral questions in a form that holds a young viewer's attention far better than a textbook. Educational broadcasters also design programmes that teach vocabulary, numeracy and social skills in ways that complement classroom learning, which is especially valuable in households where parents have limited time to read with their children.

Those who emphasise the harms of television counter that, in practice, most children watch far more than the recommended daily limit. Excessive screen time is linked to poorer sleep, weaker eyesight and reduced physical activity, while the constant stream of fast-paced cartoons can shorten attention spans and leave children less able to focus on slower, real-world tasks. A clear example is the way paediatricians in many countries now treat television overuse as a contributing factor in childhood obesity and behavioural problems.

In my view, neither blanket condemnation nor uncritical enthusiasm is justified. The deciding factors are the quality of what children watch and the amount of time they spend in front of the screen, both of which responsible parents can manage without eliminating television altogether.

In conclusion, television can be both beneficial and harmful to children, so the most sensible approach is to curate programmes carefully and to enforce reasonable limits on daily viewing.
