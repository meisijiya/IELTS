Task 2 — Free clean water supply (agree-disagree)

Access to clean water is a basic human right. Therefore every home should have a water supply that is provided free of charge. Do you agree or disagree? (C20T1)

立场: 同意家庭应获得基本免费用水; 理由1: 清洁水是生存必需品, 收费会直接危及健康与社会公平; 理由2: 政府可通过税收与阶梯水价保障基建与浪费控制, 实现可持续免费供给。

The suggestion that every home should receive a basic supply of clean water free of charge has gained wide support on the grounds that access to clean water is a fundamental human right. While some critics worry about the public expense involved, I firmly agree with the proposal, because water is essential to health in a way that other utilities are not, and treating it as a market commodity can produce serious social harm.

The first reason is that clean water is a basic necessity rather than a luxury. When households have to choose between paying for drinking water and paying for food or rent, dehydration, waterborne disease and childhood mortality all rise sharply. Surveys in low-income communities consistently show that even modest water bills deter families from collecting the daily minimum that health agencies recommend. Guaranteeing a lifeline quantity of free water therefore protects the most vulnerable and, in the long run, reduces the medical costs that governments would otherwise bear.

The second reason is that a free basic allowance is fully compatible with sustainable financing. Authorities can fund universal provision through general taxation and progressive tariffs on heavy domestic users, while still encouraging conservation. Many European cities already supply the first few cubic metres per household at no charge, then charge higher rates for excessive use. This hybrid model ensures that no family is denied a minimum supply, while wealthier households continue to bear the marginal cost of the water they consume.

In conclusion, I still believe that every home should receive a basic supply of clean water free of charge, provided that wasteful consumption is discouraged through well-designed tariffs. The right to safe water is too important to be left entirely to the market.
