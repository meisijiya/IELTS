Task 2 — Computers and reading writing skills (agree-disagree)

Some people think that the increasing use of computers and mobile phones for communication has a negative effect on young people's reading and writing skills. Do you agree or disagree? (2022.8.14)

立场: 部分同意（特定场景下确有负面影响）; 理由1: 频繁使用碎片化沟通使青少年缺乏长篇阅读的耐心; 理由2: 缩写与表情符号让书写变得不够规范，但可通过教育改善

Many parents and teachers worry that the growing use of computers and mobile phones for everyday communication is damaging young people's reading and writing skills. While digital tools undeniably bring educational benefits, I largely agree with this concern, because the habits they encourage are quietly eroding the patience and precision that traditional literacy demands.

The first reason is that constant messaging trains young readers to expect short, fast-paced exchanges rather than long, carefully constructed texts. Teenagers who spend several hours a day scrolling through social feeds often lose the concentration required to follow a sustained argument in a novel or an academic article, which in turn affects their ability to organise their own written work. Classroom evidence is striking: teachers across many countries report that students now skip paragraphs in essays, mix register in formal assignments and struggle to read aloud fluently from a printed page.

The second reason is that the informal language of messaging shapes how young people write. Abbreviations such as u and gr8, together with emoji and image-based reactions, can drift into schoolwork and undermine the careful spelling and punctuation that formal writing requires. A clear example is the way university tutors now spend a disproportionate amount of time correcting basic grammar in essays from otherwise capable students. Admittedly, technology itself is not to blame, and well-designed classroom activities can teach young users to switch between casual and formal registers without difficulty.

In conclusion, I still believe that heavy reliance on digital communication does weaken young people's reading and writing skills in measurable ways, though the right teaching can convert the same tools into a powerful aid rather than a hindrance.
