Task 2 — Global food variety year-round (advantage-disadvantage)

In the past, people ate local food in season. Nowadays, people buy a variety of food from all over the world and any season. Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages? (2022.4.9)

立场: 利大于弊，但需政策支持本地农业; 理由1: 进口丰富饮食并稳定供应; 理由2: 碳足迹与本地农业受损但政策可修复

Whether the modern shift from local seasonal eating to year-round global food shopping represents genuine progress is a question that touches health, the environment and identity. The phenomenon offers meaningful benefits, but it also carries costs that warrant careful consideration.

The advantages are considerable. Global supply chains let consumers enjoy a varied diet throughout the year, free from the nutritional monotony that often affected communities relying on local harvests. Imported fruit in winter supplies vitamins to households that would otherwise have had a narrower range of foods. International trade also stabilises prices: when one region suffers a poor harvest, suppliers elsewhere can step in, reducing the risk of scarcity. A clear example is the way modern supermarkets keep strawberries available even in cold winters, a luxury that earlier generations would have found unimaginable.

The disadvantages, however, should not be ignored. Shipping food across continents consumes large amounts of fuel, leaving a heavy carbon footprint and contributing to climate change. Local farmers frequently struggle to compete with cheaper imports, leading to abandoned fields and shrinking rural communities. The loss of seasonal eating also erodes cultural traditions such as harvest festivals. Fortunately, these drawbacks can be addressed through sensible policies: governments can label carbon-intensive imports, support local producers and encourage citizens to buy seasonal food whenever available.

On balance, I believe the advantages of global food variety outweigh the disadvantages, because nutritional diversity and price stability bring genuine benefits to households, and the environmental and cultural costs can be mitigated through deliberate policy.

In conclusion, year-round access to global food is worth keeping, but governments should pair it with measures that actively protect local farmers, lower the carbon cost of imports and preserve seasonal traditions that bind communities together.
