Task 2 — Advertising economic vs social effects (discuss-both-views)

Some people think advertisements may have positive economic effects whereas others think there are negative social effects because individuals are not satisfied with who they are and what they have. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (2024.4.6)

立场: 折中（经济红利显著，心理代价需正视）; 理由1: 广告支撑媒体、推动竞争并加速新产品扩散; 理由2: 反复比较加剧身份焦虑与不满足感

The question of whether advertising creates more economic value than social harm continues to divide opinion. Some observers point to the contribution campaigns make to growth and innovation, while others emphasise the way advertising quietly damages personal contentment and community values.

Those who emphasise the economic benefits note that advertising pays for much of the free media that audiences enjoy, including commercial television, online news outlets and many streaming services. Campaigns also inform consumers about new products, encourage competition on quality and reward companies that invest in genuine innovation. Small businesses in particular benefit when affordable digital advertising lets them reach customers far beyond their immediate neighbourhood, which in turn supports local employment and entrepreneurship across many communities.

Those who emphasise the social costs argue that advertising systematically distorts how people evaluate their own lives. By promoting glossy body images, luxury lifestyles and constant comparison with peers, commercial messages can leave ordinary viewers feeling chronically dissatisfied with who they are and what they already own. A clear example is the way research links heavy exposure to image-based advertising with higher rates of anxiety and lower self-esteem in teenagers. From this perspective, the relentless pressure to consume quietly weakens the psychological well-being on which healthy communities depend.

In my view, neither position is wholly correct. The economic contribution of advertising is real, but its social costs are significant enough to justify clearer regulation, accurate representation and stronger media literacy in schools.

In conclusion, advertising brings clear economic advantages alongside serious social risks, so the most sensible policy is to harness its commercial value while teaching citizens to interpret its messages critically.
