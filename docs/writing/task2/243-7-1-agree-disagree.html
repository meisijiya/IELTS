Task 2 — Music as a universal language (agree-disagree)

Music has always been and will continue to be a universal language to mankind. To what extend do you agree or disagree? (2021.3.13)

立场: 同意音乐是普世语言; 理由1: 跨文化唤起情感共鸣、不依赖共同词汇; 理由2: 历史上连接人群、巩固共同身份

The claim that music has always been, and will continue to be, a universal language for humanity captures something profound. I firmly agree with this view, because musical emotion crosses linguistic borders with unusual ease and it has historically served as a bridge between communities that share little else.

The first reason is that music can convey emotion without requiring a shared vocabulary. A slow, descending melody in a minor key tends to sound mournful to listeners whether they were raised in Tokyo, Lagos or Stockholm, and a fast, rhythmic drum pattern can spark physical excitement across every continent. Studies of infants too young to understand language show that they respond to tempo and pitch in much the same way, which suggests that musical perception is wired into human biology rather than learned through culture alone. For this reason, even audiences who cannot translate a single word of a song often leave the concert hall moved.

The second reason is that music has long served as a bridge between peoples who otherwise have little in common. Religious chants bind worshippers across continents, folk songs preserve the identity of diaspora communities, and national anthems unite strangers at the Olympic Games. When a South African choir performed with a Chinese orchestra at a recent cultural exchange, neither audience understood the other's lyrics, yet the applause was unmistakably shared. Were music to lose this connective power, the world would forfeit one of the few experiences that reliably brings diverse populations into emotional alignment.

In conclusion, I still believe that music is, and will remain, a universal language, because it speaks directly to human feeling, unites strangers across cultural divides and offers a shared vocabulary of emotion that no spoken tongue can match.
