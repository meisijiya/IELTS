Task 2 — Watching films alone (two-questions)

People these days prefer to watch films and TV programmes alone rather than with other people. Why is that? Do you think it is a positive or negative development? (2026.7.25)

立场: 偏负面（孤独观影削弱家庭与社交联结）; 理由1: 个性化推荐与便携设备使独自观看更便捷; 理由2: 家庭共同观影的仪式感与共享情感逐渐消失

The aim of this essay is to examine why so many people now prefer to watch films and television programmes alone rather than with family or friends, and to consider whether this shift is a positive or negative development.

The first question concerns the reasons behind the change. The rise of personalised recommendation systems has trained viewers to expect content tailored precisely to their tastes, which makes shared viewing feel like a compromise. Streaming platforms also allow individuals to start, pause and resume programmes on their own schedule, an arrangement that fits busy lives far better than synchronising with housemates. Younger audiences in particular have grown up with earphones and mobile screens, so the habit of watching alone often feels more natural than gathering around a shared television set.

The second question asks whether this trend is positive or negative. On balance, I would argue that it is largely a negative development, because much of the value of film and television historically came from the conversations and shared memories it created. A clear example is the way families that once gathered for a weekly film night now scatter to separate screens, losing the simple ritual of laughing, arguing and reflecting together over a single story. Independent viewing can still be personally enjoyable, but the gradual erosion of communal viewing weakens the small daily bonds that hold households and friendships together, with subtle but real costs for social cohesion.

In conclusion, the convenience of personalised streaming explains why people now watch alone, but the trend is largely negative because it quietly diminishes the shared rituals that bring families and friends closer.
