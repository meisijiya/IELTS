Task 2 — Adult literacy vs youth education (agree-disagree)

The education of young people is highly prioritized in many countries. However, educating adults who cannot write or read is even more important and governments should spend more money on this. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2021.7.10)

立场: 不同意，二者同等重要，不应厚此薄彼; 理由1: 成人扫盲关系家庭代际脱贫，但青年教育影响未来劳动力素质; 理由2: 政府应平衡投入，而非将资源从青少年教育中转移。

It is often argued that, because adult illiteracy poses an immediate threat to social welfare, governments should redirect educational funding away from young people and towards literacy programmes for adults. I disagree with this view, as both groups deserve sustained investment and reallocating money from one to the other would create serious new problems.

The first reason adult literacy matters is that literate parents exert a powerful influence on the next generation. A mother who learns to read can help her children with homework, navigate school communications and break the cycle of educational disadvantage that often traps low-income families. Conversely, a child whose parents cannot read tends to fall behind from the very first year of school, rarely catching up with peers. For this reason, adult literacy programmes deserve substantial government support, particularly in regions where illiteracy rates remain stubbornly high despite past efforts.

However, scaling back investment in young people would be a serious strategic mistake. School education builds the skills, knowledge and habits that determine a country's economic competitiveness and civic health for decades ahead. Were governments to divert large sums from youth schooling in order to fund adult programmes, classroom sizes would balloon, qualified teachers would leave the profession and the long-term quality of the workforce would deteriorate noticeably. A balanced budget that supports both populations, rather than choosing one over the other, is the only responsible approach to public education.

In conclusion, I do not believe that adult literacy should be prioritised over youth education, because both groups require and deserve adequate funding. The most sensible policy is for governments to expand the overall education budget so that no demographic is left behind.
