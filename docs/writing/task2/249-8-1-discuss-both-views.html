Task 2 — Study abroad vs stay home (discuss-both-views)

Some people think it is a better way to go abroad to work or study, while others think staying in their own country is a better choice. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (2022.5.7)

立场: 倾向支持出国经历但不应成为唯一选项; 理由1: 出国拓宽视野并培养跨文化能力; 理由2: 留在本国可贡献社区并避免人才流失

Whether young people should pursue their studies and careers abroad or build their lives in their home country remains a widely debated question. Some commentators celebrate international experience as the gateway to a successful life, while others defend the value of staying close to family, culture and local community.

Those who advocate going abroad emphasise the unique personal and professional growth that foreign environments provide. Living in a different country forces young people to master a new language, navigate unfamiliar customs and build friendships across cultures, skills that are now prized in multinational employers. A clear example is the Indian software engineer who studies in the United States and returns with both technical expertise and an international network, opening doors for trade and investment that benefit an entire sector back home.

Advocates of staying at home, however, warn that a culture of leaving can drain a nation of its most ambitious talent. When large numbers of doctors, engineers and teachers emigrate, hospitals in poorer regions close wards and rural schools lose their best educators. Strong national development also depends on people who understand local dialects, local problems and local politics, and these insights cannot be gained through foreign diplomas. Were every ambitious young person to leave, the country would lose the very citizens needed for reform.

In my view, going abroad for a period of study or work can be extremely valuable, but the choice should be balanced with the duty to bring new skills back and contribute to the home community.

In conclusion, overseas experience brings genuine advantages in confidence and career, while staying home preserves the social fabric and allows reform-minded citizens to serve their own people, and the strongest societies encourage both paths to coexist.
