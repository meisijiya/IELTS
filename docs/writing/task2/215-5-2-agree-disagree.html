Task 2 — Ban selling unhealthy food (agree-disagree)

Shops should not be allowed to sell any food or drink that has been scientifically proved to be bad for people's health. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2021.12.02)

立场: 不同意全面禁令; 理由1: 个人自由与执法成本过高; 理由2: 监管标签与教育更有效

Some people argue that any food or drink proven harmful to health should be banned from sale in ordinary shops. While I understand the concern behind the proposal, I firmly disagree with such a sweeping prohibition, because it would erode personal freedom, punish responsible adults and produce serious unintended consequences for both businesses and the courts.

My first reason is that adults should retain the right to make informed choices about what they consume, and that removing those choices rarely delivers the promised health benefits. Many products classed as unhealthy, such as strong spirits or salty snacks, can be enjoyed in moderation without serious harm, and a complete ban would simply push sales into an unregulated black market, where no quality control or age limit applies. A clear example is the long experience with alcohol prohibition in several countries, which created organised crime without noticeably improving public health, and was eventually repealed.

My second reason is that clear labelling, targeted taxes and widespread education can guide consumers far more effectively than outright prohibition, and at a fraction of the enforcement cost. Warning labels on cigarettes, for instance, have cut smoking rates sharply over recent decades without removing the product from shop shelves. Were governments to switch to outright bans, shopkeepers would face criminal liability for selling a birthday cake, and the legal system would be overwhelmed by trivial cases, diverting attention from genuinely dangerous products.

In conclusion, I still believe that education, transparent labelling and well-designed taxes offer a smarter route to better public health, and that an outright ban on unwholesome food and drink would do more harm than good.
