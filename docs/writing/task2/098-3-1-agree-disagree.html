Task 2 — Congestion: move to cities (agree-disagree)

The best way to solve traffic and transportation problems is to encourage people to live in cities rather than suburbs and the countryside. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2021.11.20)

立场: 不同意迁徙是最优解; 理由1: 城市过度集中反加剧拥堵与污染; 理由2: 应以公共交通和远程办公多管齐下

Some commentators suggest that the most effective solution to traffic congestion is to push people out of suburbs and the countryside and into dense urban centres. I disagree with this approach, because forcing everyone into cities tends to worsen the very problems it is meant to solve, and far more balanced strategies are available to modern governments.

The first reason is that encouraging further urban concentration actually puts greater strain on city roads rather than easing them. Once a metropolis reaches a certain size, each new resident adds to traffic, air pollution and the demand for parking, so the marginal benefit of moving yet another commuter off a country lane is quickly cancelled out by worsening congestion downtown. A clear example of this dynamic is Beijing, where aggressive inward migration over recent decades has produced some of the most severe rush-hour jams and air-quality alerts of any capital in the world.

The second reason is that effective transport policy depends on a wider set of tools, including frequent public transit, sensible road pricing and the option to work remotely. Modern light-rail networks, congestion charges and hybrid working arrangements can all reduce car use without uprooting millions of families from their homes. Were governments to rely solely on relocation, they would ignore the substantial emissions reductions that better buses, cycling lanes and remote-work policies can already deliver in cities such as Copenhagen or Amsterdam.

In conclusion, I still believe that persuading people to live in cities is not the best answer to traffic problems, because denser populations amplify congestion and far more effective, far less disruptive solutions already exist for modern cities.
