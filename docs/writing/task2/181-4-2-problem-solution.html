Task 2 — Plastic pollution (problem-solution)

More and more plastic is polluting in world city, countryside and oceans. What are the problems caused by plastic waste? What measures should be taken to solve it? (2021.1.30)

立场: 塑料污染严峻，需要源头减量 + 综合治理; 理由1: 海洋生物受困、微塑料进入食物链、城乡排水堵塞均造成严重后果; 理由2: 解决方案涵盖立法限塑、提升回收基建、推广可降解材料与公众教育。

The aim of this essay is to outline the principal problems caused by the rapid accumulation of plastic waste in cities, rural areas and oceans, and to consider the measures that governments, businesses and individuals should adopt to address it.

Plastic waste causes a particularly wide range of environmental and public-health problems. In the oceans, discarded bottles, bags and fishing gear entangle turtles, seals and seabirds, while breaking down into microplastics that are now found throughout the marine food chain. On land, blocked storm drains in cities increasingly contribute to flash floods during heavy rain, and burning plastic in the countryside releases highly toxic dioxins that damage local air quality and crop safety. These effects are interconnected: once plastic enters rivers, it almost always ends up in the sea, so urban and rural pollution ultimately becomes a global crisis.

A meaningful response must therefore combine national legislation with industrial redesign and behavioural change. Governments should impose stricter restrictions on single-use plastics, raise producer responsibility fees and invest in modern recycling facilities that actually handle mixed materials. Industry, for its part, must accelerate the development of biodegradable alternatives and adopt refillable packaging models. At the individual level, schools and media campaigns can encourage households to reduce, reuse and sort their waste, while deposit-return schemes give people a direct financial incentive to return bottles rather than discard them.

In conclusion, plastic pollution poses a serious and growing threat to ecosystems and human health, but a combination of regulation, technological innovation and everyday consumer choices can steadily reduce its impact. The longer coordinated action is delayed, the more expensive the eventual environmental clean-up will become.
