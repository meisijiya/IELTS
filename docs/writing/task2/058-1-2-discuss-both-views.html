Task 2 — Purpose of education (discuss-both-views)

Some people believe the purpose of education is to prepare individuals to be useful to society. Others say the purpose of education is to achieve personal ambitions. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (2023.9.2)

立场: 两者皆为教育的核心目的; 理由1: 服务社会促进公共福祉与代际传承; 理由2: 个人抱负驱动创新与自我实现

The question of what education is fundamentally for has long divided educators and policymakers. Some insist that its main purpose is to mould individuals who will contribute to the wider community, while others argue that it exists primarily to help each learner pursue personal ambitions. Both perspectives carry genuine weight and deserve close examination.

Those who view education as a social instrument argue that schools and universities have a duty to transmit shared values, civic responsibility and the practical skills that a functioning state requires. A clear example is the inclusion of civics, environmental awareness and basic economics in national curricula: these subjects exist less to enrich individual students than to ensure that future citizens understand how a healthy society is maintained. Were education to ignore this collective dimension, graduates might possess brilliant talents yet lack the cooperative instincts that keep communities stable.

Those who view education as a vehicle for personal ambition point out that most great discoveries and creative achievements began as private dreams rather than public assignments. Entrepreneurs, research scientists and artists typically pursue lines of work that serve their own curiosity, and the wider world benefits only afterwards, often in unexpected ways. If curricula were designed solely to produce obedient workers, the kind of bold, idiosyncratic thinking that gives rise to medical breakthroughs or literary masterpieces would be discouraged from an early age.

Having considered both arguments, I am more inclined to support a balanced vision in which education serves society without crushing personal aspiration. A system that nurtures ambition while also teaching the obligations of citizenship is, in my view, the most likely to produce both responsible communities and remarkable individuals.
