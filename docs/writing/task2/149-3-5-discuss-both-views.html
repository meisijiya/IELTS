Task 2 — Dangerous sports freedom (discuss-both-views)

Some people think that dangerous sports should be banned. Others, however, think people should have the freedom to choose sports activities. Discuss both these views and give your own opinion.

立场: 不同意全面禁止，应以安全规范替代; 理由1: 禁止剥夺个人选择且转入地下; 理由2: 安全规范与许可制度足以管控风险

Whether inherently dangerous sports should be legally banned or simply left to individual choice continues to spark debate. Some argue that the state must protect citizens from harm, while others insist that personal freedom should take priority over paternalistic controls.

Supporters of a ban emphasise the heavy medical and emotional costs that extreme sports impose. Helicopter rescue teams, hospital intensive care units and grieving families all bear the consequences when a base jumper or amateur boxer makes a mistake. From this perspective, allowing such activities to flourish is a quiet form of cruelty to the wider community, which ends up paying for the choices of thrill-seekers. A clear example is the way some alpine regions have restricted off-piste skiing after a series of accidents placed enormous strain on local emergency services.

Advocates of personal choice, by contrast, point out that banning an activity rarely removes the desire to practise it. History shows that prohibition often drives behaviour underground, where it becomes even more dangerous because it escapes official supervision. Were skydiving, deep-sea diving or motor rallying to be banned outright, enthusiasts would still pursue them in remote locations without proper equipment or training, and the resulting injury rates would likely climb rather than fall. Licensing schemes, mandatory safety briefings and strict equipment standards can mitigate most of the risks while preserving the freedom to participate.

In my view, the freedom to choose should be respected, provided that participation is properly regulated rather than outlawed.

In conclusion, dangerous sports should be governed through rigorous safety rules and licensing, not through outright prohibition that would only push them into less accountable settings.
