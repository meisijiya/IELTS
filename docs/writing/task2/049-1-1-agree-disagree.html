Task 2 — Art classes compulsory (agree-disagree)

Art classes, such as painting or drawing, are as important for children's development as other subjects, so it should be compulsory in high school. Do you agree or disagree? (2024.12.14)

立场: 同意艺术列入必修; 理由1: 艺术培养创造力与情感表达; 理由2: 艺术与其他学科相互促进而非竞争

Some people argue that art classes, such as painting or drawing, should be a compulsory part of high school education because they are as important for children's development as other subjects. I firmly agree with this view, since the skills that art teaches are essential to both individual growth and a balanced school curriculum.

The first reason is that compulsory art classes cultivate creativity and emotional expression, qualities that purely academic subjects often leave underdeveloped. When students experiment with colour, line and form, they learn to take artistic risks, to refine their work through iteration and to translate inner feelings into outward form. These habits of mind are precisely what employers and creative industries later demand, and they cannot easily be acquired through lectures or textbooks alone. A clear example is the way a teenager who has spent years in an art class often becomes more comfortable proposing original ideas in group discussions, something that students trained only on examination technique rarely achieve.

The second reason is that art reinforces learning in other subjects rather than competing with them. Research suggests that students who engage with visual arts tend to perform better in reading comprehension and spatial reasoning, because they are practised in observing detail and interpreting symbol. Were art classes to be removed from the timetable, schools would forfeit a powerful tool for engaging pupils who struggle with text-heavy lessons, and the broader curriculum would become narrower, not stronger.

In conclusion, I still believe that art classes should be compulsory in high school, because they cultivate creative minds and quietly strengthen performance across the rest of the curriculum, ultimately producing better-rounded graduates.
