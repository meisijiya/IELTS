Task 2 — News from internet (positive-negative)

More and more people no longer read the newspaper or watch TV programs to get news. They get news about the world through the internet. Is this a positive or negative development? (2021.4.3)

立场: 倾向积极，但伴随一定风险; 理由1: 网络新闻更便捷、多元，扩展读者的视野; 理由2: 信息过载与假新闻削弱了深度报道与公共理性

An increasing number of people now rely on the internet for their daily news, leaving traditional newspapers and television channels behind. While this shift inevitably raises concerns about misinformation, I would argue that, on the whole, it represents a positive development that broadens access to information while still leaving room for improvement.

The first reason is that online news makes global events accessible to almost anyone with a connection, at virtually no cost. A reader in a small town can follow parliamentary debates in another continent within minutes, compare reports from several outlets and read long-form analyses that printed newspapers would never have space to carry. This breadth and immediacy deepen public understanding of international affairs in ways the traditional broadcast schedule simply could not match, particularly for younger audiences who would otherwise never pick up a broadsheet.

The second reason is that the same abundance carries genuine risks. Online platforms are saturated with sensational headlines, unverified rumours and sponsored content that mimics real reporting, which can easily mislead citizens who lack the time or training to evaluate sources. Concentrated ownership of major news websites also means that recommendation algorithms tend to reinforce existing opinions rather than expose readers to genuinely different perspectives. These problems do not, however, outweigh the gains, since digital and traditional media alike can mitigate them through transparent sourcing, dedicated fact-checking sections and improved media literacy taught in schools.

In conclusion, despite the real risks of misinformation and filter bubbles, the move towards online news is largely a positive development because it democratises access to information, and its drawbacks can be addressed through better education and editorial standards.
