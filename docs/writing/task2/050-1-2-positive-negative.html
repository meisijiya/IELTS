Task 2 — Children given more freedom (positive-negative)

In many parts of the world, children are given more freedom than in the past. Is this a positive or negative development? (2025.4.19)

立场: 倾向积极; 理由1: 自由培养独立性与责任感; 理由2: 自由激发创造力与抗挫韧性

In many parts of the world, parents today give their children far more freedom in everyday decisions than previous generations received. Although this shift inevitably causes concern among older relatives and educators, I would argue that, on the whole, it is a positive development that benefits both individuals and the wider society.

The first reason is that increased freedom helps children develop independence and a stronger sense of responsibility. When youngsters are trusted to manage their own time, travel short distances alone or choose their own hobbies, they gradually learn to weigh options, handle small setbacks and accept the consequences of their decisions. These are precisely the qualities that future employers and university programmes consistently seek. A clear example is the way teenagers who have practised making everyday choices often adapt more easily to life away from home, while peers raised under strict supervision frequently struggle with basic tasks such as budgeting or cooking.

The second reason is that freedom of exploration nurtures creativity and resilience, capacities that a heavily controlled childhood tends to suppress. When children are allowed to experiment, fail and try again without an adult constantly stepping in, they develop flexible thinking that later proves invaluable in art and science. Schools and research labs often note that their most original contributors were given plenty of unstructured time to follow their curiosity as children. Admittedly, granting more freedom does carry genuine risks, yet these concerns are better addressed through open communication than by returning to restrictive parenting styles of earlier decades.

In conclusion, despite the genuine worries it raises, the trend towards giving children greater freedom is largely a positive development, because it builds the resilience and self-reliance that young people need in modern society.
