Task 2 — Performing arts funding (discuss-both-views)

Some people think the government should provide funds for performing art training courses, while others believe it is the responsibility of businesses and students' families. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (2025.10.11)

立场: 公共为主, 私人补充; 理由1: 公费保障公平与文化传承; 理由2: 私人赞助可缓解财政压力

The question of who should pay for performing-art training courses, the state or private contributors, has divided opinion for many years. After considering both views, I believe that public funding is necessary, although businesses and families should also contribute in clearly defined ways.

Those who support government funding emphasise the cultural and educational value of performing arts. Drama, dance and music courses teach discipline, teamwork and public speaking, abilities that benefit every career. When the state covers tuition, talented students from low-income backgrounds can train alongside wealthier peers, preventing the arts from becoming a luxury only the rich can afford. A compelling argument can also be made in terms of national identity, because a society that neglects its performers gradually loses the stories and songs that hold communities together.

Opponents, however, argue that taxpayers' money should be directed at more urgent services and that private sponsors already play a strong role. Major companies often fund conservatoires in exchange for prestige, while parents can choose to invest in their own children's training. Critics also worry that state grants may be wasted on courses with little measurable outcome.

In my view, a shared model is most sensible. The government should fund core teaching posts and scholarships for gifted students from poor families, while businesses and parents can top up fees for additional coaching, equipment and overseas study. Such an arrangement protects access to the arts and keeps the entire system financially sustainable over the long term.

In conclusion, I still believe that performing-art training needs a substantial public subsidy, but the private sector and families have a legitimate part to play alongside it.
