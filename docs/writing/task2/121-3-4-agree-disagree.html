Task 2 — Celebrities media privacy (agree-disagree)

In some countries, celebrities complain about the way the media publicizes their private lives. Some people say that they should accept it as part of their fame. Do you agree or disagree? (2024.10.30)

立场: 不同意，隐私权不应因名气被完全剥夺; 理由1: 过度曝光损害明星及其家人的心理健康与日常尊严; 理由2: 名声带来的义务有边界，公众利益不等于个人丑闻

Some people argue that celebrities, having chosen a public career, should accept media intrusion into their private lives as an unavoidable part of fame. I firmly disagree with this view, because privacy is a basic human right that does not disappear with professional success, and excessive intrusion can cause real harm to the people involved.

The first reason is that constant media scrutiny places enormous pressure on celebrities and their families, often producing serious mental-health consequences. Paparazzi photographs of celebrities’ children, unfounded rumours about their relationships and relentless speculation about their bodies can erode self-esteem and trigger anxiety or depression. A clear example is the well-documented rise in psychological distress among young stars whose every mistake is amplified into a global headline, sometimes with tragic outcomes for the individuals concerned.

The second reason is that the duties attached to public status have clear limits and should not be extended to every corner of a person’s life. The public has a legitimate interest in a politician’s policy decisions or an actor’s professional conduct on stage, but almost no genuine interest in their private phone calls, holiday destinations or medical records. Were the media to treat every detail of a celebrity’s life as fair game, ordinary citizens would rightly fear that the same erosion of privacy could one day reach their own doorstep, and public trust in journalism would certainly suffer as a consequence.

In conclusion, I still believe that celebrities deserve meaningful protection of their private lives, because fame grants the public access to their work, not to their personal dignity, and respecting that boundary is essential for a humane society.
