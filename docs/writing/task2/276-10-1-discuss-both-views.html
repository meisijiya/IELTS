Task 2 — Prison alternatives (discuss-both-views)

Some people think that all the lawbreakers should be taken into the prison, while others believe that there are better alternatives, (for example, doing some work or learning some skills in the community). Discuss both views and give your opinion. (2023.11.18)

立场: 严重暴力犯应入狱,非暴力犯更适合社区矫正; 理由1: 监禁能隔离危险罪犯并形成威慑; 理由2: 社区矫正帮助非暴力犯维持工作家庭并降低再犯率

There is ongoing debate about whether all lawbreakers should be sent to prison or whether community-based alternatives such as supervised work and skill-training programmes might be more appropriate. This essay discusses both views before presenting my own position on the matter.

On the one hand, supporters of imprisonment argue that prison remains the most reliable way to protect the public from dangerous individuals. A violent offender, once locked away, can no longer harm ordinary citizens, and the visible certainty of punishment is widely believed to deter others from crossing the law. Prison also signals a society's moral condemnation of the most serious crimes. For crimes such as murder and armed robbery, no community alternative can offer the same level of public safety.

On the other hand, advocates of community alternatives point out that prison often fails to rehabilitate non-violent offenders and may even harden them. Programmes such as community service, vocational training, and electronic monitoring allow minor offenders to keep their jobs, maintain family ties, and learn new skills, all of which reduce the likelihood of further crimes. These alternatives also cost the state far less than full incarceration, freeing up resources for education and victim support.

My own view is that the choice between prison and community sanctions should depend on the severity of the offence. Violent and repeat offenders must still be incarcerated to protect public safety, but non-violent offenders, particularly first-time lawbreakers, would benefit more from structured rehabilitation in the community, where they can keep their livelihoods intact while making amends.

In conclusion, a balanced criminal justice system should reserve prison for genuinely dangerous offenders while channelling minor offenders into rehabilitation programmes that address the root causes of their behaviour.
