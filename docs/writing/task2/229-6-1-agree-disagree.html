Task 2 — Museums no longer needed (agree-disagree)

In the past, important knowledge about culture and history was stored in museums. Nowadays, information is freely available on the internet; therefore, there is no longer any need for museums. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2024.4.27)

立场: 不同意取消博物馆; 理由1: 实物承载历史与艺术的真实性，是屏幕无法替代的; 理由2: 博物馆提供公共教育空间与文化遗产保护

Some observers argue that, since cultural and historical knowledge is now freely available online, traditional museums have lost their purpose. I firmly disagree with this view: although the internet has democratised access to information, museums perform essential functions in safeguarding heritage and educating the public that no website can fully replicate.

The first reason is that physical objects carry an authenticity and emotional power that digital images can only approximate. Standing before an ancient scroll, a hand-woven tapestry or the actual handwriting of a historical figure delivers a sense of connection to the past that a high-resolution scan rarely evokes. Curators and educators consistently report that visitors who encounter artefacts in person remember them far more vividly than those who view similar material on a screen, and the same objects often inspire research questions that no digital catalogue would have suggested, which deepens public engagement with culture.

The second reason is that museums provide a public space for shared learning that the internet cannot substitute. They organise school visits, guided tours and community workshops that bring together students, families and retired residents in ways an online archive never does, and they play a crucial role in conserving fragile artefacts that would otherwise deteriorate in private hands. Were governments to close museums on the assumption that information alone is enough, they would also dismantle the cultural infrastructure that supports tourism, local identity and the intergenerational dialogue that holds communities together.

In conclusion, I still believe that museums deserve continued public investment, because they offer authentic encounters with history, expert conservation and shared learning spaces that online information alone cannot provide for society.
