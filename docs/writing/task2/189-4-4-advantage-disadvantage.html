Task 2 — Giving up flying (advantage-disadvantage)

Some people have decided to reduce the number of times they fly every year or to stop flying altogether. Do you think the environmental benefits of this development outweigh the disadvantages for individuals and businesses? (C20T2)

立场: 环保收益大于代价, 应得到政策与文化支持; 理由1: 减少航班直接降低碳排放, 有助气候与本地空气质量; 理由2: 个人失去部分便利、企业需调整运营模式, 但损失有限且可以被远程技术消化。

A growing number of people now choose to fly less or to stop flying altogether, citing environmental concerns. While this trend forces individuals and businesses to adapt, on balance I believe the environmental benefits clearly outweigh the disadvantages, because aviation is one of the hardest sectors to decarbonise and reducing demand there delivers some of the fastest climate gains.

The principal benefit of this development is a sharp reduction in aviation emissions. Long-haul flights are responsible for a disproportionate share of an average country's personal carbon footprint, so even a modest drop in flights per household can meaningfully cut national emissions. Less air traffic also eases noise pollution around airports and improves local air quality in nearby communities, with measurable benefits for residents' health. Importantly, the trend accelerates a cultural shift in which low-carbon choices such as domestic rail travel and local holidays begin to feel normal rather than exceptional.

The most significant drawback, by contrast, is the loss of personal convenience. Families with relatives abroad, tourists who value unique destinations and businesspeople who rely on face-to-face meetings all have legitimate reasons to board planes. Companies in tourism, hospitality and aviation will also see revenue fall, leading in some cases to job losses that governments must manage through retraining schemes. Yet these costs remain limited in scale compared with the global consequences of runaway climate change, and many meetings can be replaced by high-quality video conferencing without any loss of productivity.

In conclusion, although reducing flights creates real inconvenience for some travellers and industries, the long-term environmental gains far outweigh those temporary drawbacks, and societies should actively support the shift through rail investment, virtual-meeting infrastructure and fair retraining for affected workers.
