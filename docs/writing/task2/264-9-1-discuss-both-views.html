Task 2 — Health and education duty (discuss-both-views)

Some people think the government should pay for health care and education, but others believe it is not the government's responsibility. Discuss both views and give your opinion. (2023.2.18)

立场: 公共与个人共担; 理由1: 政府兜底保障公平; 理由2: 个人补充提升服务质量

It is frequently argued that the state should bear the cost of health care and education, while others insist these services are largely a personal or family matter. After examining both sides, I believe the most reasonable policy is one where the government guarantees a basic level, and individuals can buy extra services when they wish.

Supporters of full state funding emphasise equal access and social stability. When hospitals and schools are free, children of low-income families can still become doctors or engineers, and talent is not wasted. Publicly funded systems also mitigate the anxiety caused by sudden medical bills, which is why countries with universal coverage enjoy stronger public trust. Were the government to withdraw, the gap between rich and poor patients would widen quickly, and preventable diseases would spread.

Those who oppose the idea argue that taxpayers' money should not cover every service, and that personal responsibility keeps systems efficient. If patients pay a small share of their treatment, they are less likely to misuse emergency rooms for minor complaints. Likewise, families who top up school fees can choose smaller class sizes or better facilities, creating variety that a single state system cannot provide. Heavy state spending, critics warn, may also push up national debt and leave less money for transport.

In my view, a balanced system is preferable. The state should fund essential treatment, primary schooling and teacher salaries, while optional services, such as private wards or special tutoring, can be paid for by individuals. Such a model guarantees fairness and preserves choice at the same time.

In conclusion, I still believe that governments must lead on basic health and education, but personal contributions have a legitimate role to play alongside public funding.
