Task 2 — News online vs TV (positive-negative)

More and more people no longer read the newspaper or watch TV programs to get news. They get news about the world through the internet. Is this a positive or negative development? (2022.2.19)

立场: 倾向积极，但需改进媒体素养; 理由1: 互联网让信息获取更即时、更全球化; 理由2: 算法过滤与假新闻风险可通过编辑规范与教育缓解

An increasing number of people now obtain their daily news from online platforms rather than from printed newspapers or scheduled television programmes. While this shift inevitably raises concerns about accuracy, I would argue that, on balance, it represents a positive development because it broadens access to information and gives readers more control over what they consume.

The first reason is that online news delivers global events to readers at almost no cost and at remarkable speed. A farmer in a remote village can read about a trade negotiation in another continent within minutes of its conclusion, compare coverage from several outlets and consult long-form analyses that printed newspapers would never have space to carry. This breadth and immediacy deepen public understanding of international affairs in ways the traditional broadcast schedule simply could not match, especially for younger audiences who would otherwise never pick up a broadsheet.

The second reason is that the same abundance carries genuine risks. Online feeds are saturated with sensational headlines, sponsored content and unverified rumours, while recommendation algorithms often reinforce existing opinions instead of exposing readers to different perspectives. These drawbacks, however, do not outweigh the gains, because both newsrooms and schools can mitigate them through transparent sourcing, dedicated fact-checking desks and improved media literacy programmes. A clear example is the way several countries have introduced compulsory digital news evaluation in their secondary curricula, which has measurably reduced the spread of viral misinformation among teenagers.

In conclusion, despite the real dangers of misinformation and filter bubbles, the move towards online news is largely a positive development, because it democratises access to information, and its drawbacks can be addressed through better education and stronger editorial standards.
