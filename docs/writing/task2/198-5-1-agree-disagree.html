Task 2 — Scientific research: government or private (agree-disagree)

Scientific research should be carried out and controlled by government rather than private companies. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2021.8.21)

立场: 同意政府主导; 理由1: 公益研究与长期议题需公共资金; 理由2: 防止科研被商业利益扭曲

Some people argue that scientific research ought to be funded and steered by the government instead of private companies. I firmly agree with this view, because public funding can support long-term public-interest projects that the market ignores, and it offers the most reliable way to keep dangerous research from being misused for private profit.

My first reason is that government bodies are uniquely placed to finance projects whose value lies in social benefit rather than short-term profit. Areas such as basic physics, public-health epidemiology or climate science often deliver returns only after decades of patient work, and few private investors are willing to wait. A clear example is the state-funded research that produced vaccines for neglected tropical diseases, which pharmaceutical firms had previously ignored because the patients could not pay. Without public money, society would lose the quiet, slow inquiry that ultimately underwrites every commercial breakthrough.

My second reason is that public control provides essential oversight over research that may pose a threat to public safety. Private firms are sometimes tempted to suppress unfavourable findings, hide uncomfortable side-effects or rush products to market in order to protect shareholders. Where governments supervise laboratories, demand open publication and enforce strict ethical review, citizens can trust that results serve the common good rather than a quarterly profit report. Were research to be left entirely to private hands, society would have far less ability to detect dangerous experiments before they harm ordinary people.

In conclusion, I still believe that scientific research should be largely led and regulated by the government, since only public authority can balance curiosity, safety and the long-term interests of society.
