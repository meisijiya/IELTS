Task 2 — Police firearms (agree-disagree)

Some people believe if a police force carries guns, it will encourage the level of violence in the society. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2021.3.20)

立场: 部分同意武装化会升级日常冲突但在紧急情况下不可或缺; 理由1: 武装警力能在致命事件中迅速止损; 理由2: 日常佩枪容易使警民对峙升级为致命冲突

Some people argue that arming the police force will inevitably drive up the overall level of violence in society. I partly agree with this concern, because while firearms give officers a decisive edge in genuinely dangerous situations, their routine presence tends to erode public trust and aggravate ordinary confrontations.

The first reason is that armed officers are able to contain serious incidents far more quickly than their unarmed counterparts could ever manage. When a hostage-taker openly threatens lives, every minute matters, and a negotiator without a credible deterrent is forced to wait indefinitely for specialist backup units. A clear example can be drawn from several European capitals, where armed response teams have ended sieges within a single hour, sparing countless innocent civilians. Without firearms, those same officers would have watched helplessly as the situation deteriorated, with predictable tragic consequences for everyone involved.

The second reason is that the visible presence of guns tends to transform ordinary encounters into deadly standoffs. Even a routine traffic stop that might once have ended with a warning now feels life-threatening to nervous drivers, who may react in unpredictable ways. Research from several American cities suggests that heavily armed patrols correlate with higher fatality rates among young suspects, and a similar troubling trend is now surfacing in parts of Latin America. Were firearms to become standard equipment in every neighbourhood, communities already marginalised by the justice system would withdraw cooperation, leaving the police less informed, less effective, and more inclined to use force in the absence of public trust.

In conclusion, I still believe that routine arming risks raising the overall level of violence, even if firearms remain indispensable for the narrow range of emergencies where lives are at immediate stake.
