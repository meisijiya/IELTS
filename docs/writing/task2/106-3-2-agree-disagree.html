Task 2 — Preserving historical sites (agree-disagree)

Old buildings of historical importance should be preserved in cities and the countryside. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2025.3.29)

立场: 强烈同意; 理由1: 历史建筑承载文化身份; 理由2: 保护带来旅游与经济收益

It is sometimes suggested that old buildings of historical importance should be preserved both in cities and in the countryside. I firmly agree with this view, because such structures connect present generations with their past and can also generate tangible benefits for the local economy.

The first reason is that historical buildings embody the cultural identity and craftsmanship of the communities that created them. Walking through a preserved medieval street, a colonial warehouse or a traditional wooden farmhouse allows citizens to experience, with all their senses, the history they have only read about in textbooks. This tangible link to the past helps younger generations understand where they come from and strengthens the social cohesion that is so easily eroded in fast-changing modern cities. A clear example is the restored historic centre of Kyoto, where centuries-old temples and wooden townhouses still define the rhythm of daily life and remind residents of their enduring traditions.

The second reason is that preservation can deliver genuine economic rewards through tourism and small-business activity. Visitors attracted by well-maintained castles, churches and old quarters spend money on hotels, restaurants and local crafts, creating jobs that would not otherwise exist in rural areas with few other industries. Were governments to neglect this heritage in favour of cheap modern redevelopment, they would forfeit both the cultural richness that attracts visitors and the steady income that supports thousands of ordinary workers in the surrounding region.

In conclusion, I still firmly believe that old buildings of historical importance deserve to be preserved in both urban and rural settings, because they safeguard cultural memory and provide sustainable economic value to the communities that care for them.
