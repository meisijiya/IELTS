Task 2 — Healthcare spending (discuss-both-views)

Nowadays, people believe that governments should invest taxpayers' money in healthcare, while others believe that money should be used in other areas. Discuss both points of view and give your opinion. (2024.8.24)

立场: 重点投入但保持平衡; 理由1: 医疗投入是健康与经济基础; 理由2: 不可忽视教育与基础设施

It is often argued that the largest share of taxpayers' money should be spent on healthcare, while others insist that resources are better directed elsewhere. After weighing both perspectives, I believe that healthcare deserves a generous budget, but it should not crowd out equally important areas such as education and infrastructure.

Those who favour heavy spending on healthcare emphasise the moral and economic importance of a healthy population. When hospitals are well funded, patients receive treatment in time, productivity in the workforce rises, and the spread of contagious disease can be brought under control. State investment in medical research also produces new therapies that benefit the whole country. A clear example is the rapid development of vaccines during the recent pandemic, which was made possible only because governments had previously supported long-term laboratory work.

Opponents, however, point out that money poured into hospitals cannot solve every social problem. They argue that better roads, modern schools and reliable public transport are equally vital, because these services lift overall living standards and reduce future medical costs. Critics also worry that healthcare budgets can become bloated by bureaucracy, with much of the spending absorbed by administration rather than actual patient care. Some therefore suggest that other sectors deserve a larger slice of public investment.

In my view, healthcare should certainly be protected, but a balanced budget is preferable. A sensible policy is to set a clear minimum share for the health service, while allowing other ministries to compete for additional funding through transparent performance reviews.

In conclusion, I still believe that taxpayers' money must prioritise healthcare, but a country cannot afford to neglect education, transport and environmental protection at the same time.
