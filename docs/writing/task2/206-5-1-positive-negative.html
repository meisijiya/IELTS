Task 2 — Internet without face-to-face contact (positive-negative)

In modern society, it is possible to go shopping, work and communicate via the Internet without face-to-face contact with one another. To what extent do you think this is a positive or negative development? (2024.11.16)

立场: 整体偏负面; 理由1: 削弱真实人际中的共情与信任; 理由2: 拉大数字鸿沟并带来隐私风险

Many daily activities such as shopping, working and socialising can now be completed entirely online, removing the need for face-to-face contact. While this shift offers genuine convenience, I consider it to be largely a negative development, because it weakens the human bonds that hold communities together and exposes citizens to new forms of harm.

My first reason is that relationships built without physical presence tend to be shallower and easier to abandon. A quick video call cannot fully replace the reassuring touch of a friend in times of grief, nor the spontaneous conversations that begin with strangers in a market or a queue. A clear example is the way many office workers now feel lonely even though they message colleagues all day, because they no longer share coffee breaks or celebrate small victories in person. Without regular face-to-face contact, empathy and trust are difficult to build and even harder to repair when misunderstandings arise.

My second reason is that online-only interaction widens social inequalities and exposes users to data exploitation. Elderly neighbours, disabled citizens and rural families without reliable broadband are gradually excluded from services that move entirely to the internet, while companies harvest personal data on a scale that would be impossible in a physical shop. Were governments to accelerate this transition without support, vulnerable groups would be left behind, and privacy would quietly erode across the whole population.

In conclusion, I still believe that the online-only model of daily life is a largely negative trend, and its drawbacks should be mitigated by keeping human contact at the heart of essential services rather than treating it as an optional extra.
