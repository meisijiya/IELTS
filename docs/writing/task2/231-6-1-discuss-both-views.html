Task 2 — TV watching children (discuss-both-views)

Some people think watching television every day is bad for children. Others think it is good for developing children as they grow up. Discuss both views and give your opinion. (2023.3.25)

立场: 折中（适度有益，过量有害）; 理由1: 优质节目能拓宽视野并培养想象力; 理由2: 每日过量观看挤占运动、睡眠与亲子互动

Whether daily television viewing helps or harms children's development remains a question that parents and teachers often answer differently. Some argue that any daily TV habit is harmful, while others believe that well-chosen programmes can make a positive contribution to growing up.

Those who value television point out that carefully designed programmes introduce children to a much wider world than their immediate neighbourhood. A nature documentary can turn a quiet child into a passionate birdwatcher, while a foreign-language cartoon can give a toddler their first exposure to a second language in a way that feels like play rather than work. Educational broadcasters also help families with limited income or schooling options to provide structured learning at home, which means daily viewing, when curated carefully, can be a genuine supplement to classroom instruction.

Those who oppose daily viewing counter that the average child now spends several hours in front of a screen, displacing the activities that healthy growth actually requires. Sleep suffers when screens stay on late, outdoor exercise falls sharply, and the unstructured time children need to develop creativity is quietly eroded. A clear example is the way teachers in many countries report that pupils who spend long evenings watching television struggle to concentrate during morning lessons, often arriving at school tired and distracted.

In my view, television itself is neither helpful nor harmful; what matters is the quality of what children watch and the place it leaves for sleep, play and family conversation.

In conclusion, daily television viewing can support children's development when programmes are carefully chosen and viewing time is kept within reasonable limits, but excessive and uncurated viewing does more harm than good.
