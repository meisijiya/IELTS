Task 2 — Students evaluating teachers

Some people think in order to continually improve the quality of education, high school students should be encouraged to evaluate and criticize their teachers. Others feel that this would result in a loss of respect and discipline in the classroom. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (2022.1.20)

Whether high school students should be encouraged to evaluate and criticize their teachers is a widely debated question. Some argue that student feedback keeps raising teaching standards, while others fear it erodes the respect and discipline a classroom needs. In my view, such evaluation can help, but only when it is carefully designed and used as a supplement, not the sole judge.

Supporters of student evaluation point out that pupils experience the classroom first-hand every day. Teachers may not notice how clearly they explain a difficult topic or how fairly they mark homework, whereas students see all of this directly. Anonymous surveys can therefore reveal real problems administrators would otherwise miss and push teachers to keep improving instead of falling into routine. Schools that collect such feedback often find that lessons become livelier and students feel respected.

Critics, however, counter that asking teenagers to judge their teachers can easily backfire. Students are still developing emotionally and may rate a teacher badly simply because the work is demanding or because they dislike a strict rule, not because the teaching is poor. If schools treat these evaluations too seriously, teachers may feel discouraged or lower standards to win popularity, which in the end weakens the authority and mutual respect that effective learning depends on.

In my opinion, both concerns are valid, so the answer lies in balance rather than in choosing one side. Evaluations should be anonymous, regular, and limited to practical matters such as clarity and pace of teaching, while a teacher's career should rest mainly with trained professionals who observe lessons directly. Used this way, student feedback improves teaching without threatening the respect and discipline schools must protect.
