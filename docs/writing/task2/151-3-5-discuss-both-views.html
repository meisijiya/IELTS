Task 2 — Long-lasting vs cheap products (discuss-both-views)

Some people think products should be made to last as long as possible, while others think making products cheap is more important. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

立场: 同意耐用优先，廉价作为补充; 理由1: 耐用产品降低资源消耗和环境负担; 理由2: 廉价让低收入家庭获得基本生活品

The question of whether manufacturers should prioritise durability or affordability in their products continues to provoke discussion. Some observers argue that long-lasting goods represent the responsible path forward, while others insist that cheap products better serve the daily needs of ordinary households across the income spectrum.

Those who favour durability highlight the environmental and economic benefits of well-made items. A washing machine or a pair of shoes that lasts a full decade cuts down on raw material extraction, factory emissions and landfill waste, while also saving the consumer repeated repair or replacement costs over many years. A clear example is the growing market for high-quality furniture, where buyers willingly pay more for craftsmanship because they expect to hand the pieces down to their children. In an era of climate anxiety, durability has become a quiet but powerful expression of environmental responsibility.

Advocates of cheap production, however, emphasise that low prices open modern goods to people on modest incomes. Without affordable electronics, clothing and basic household appliances, large sections of society would be excluded from the everyday comforts that wealthier citizens take entirely for granted. Were every manufacturer to focus exclusively on premium, long-lasting goods, the gap between rich and poor would widen inside every shop window, and social tension would quietly build.

In my view, durability should be the default ambition, while cheap ranges should be reserved for items that genuinely need to be disposable, such as medical supplies or packaging.

In conclusion, well-made and durable products offer the strongest long-term benefits for households and for the planet, but producers should still provide affordable options for those specific goods where frequent replacement is genuinely necessary.
