Task 2 — Using animals for human benefit (discuss-both-views)

Some people say it is acceptable to use animals for our benefit, others say it is wrong to exploit them. Discuss both points of view and give your opinion. (2024.12.28)

立场: 折中——有条件使用、必要时替代; 理由1: 动物对食物、药物、研发有不可替代的贡献，全面禁止会损害人类福祉; 理由2: 任何利用都应受到伦理约束，逐步以替代品取代，活体解剖、动物毛皮等非必要用途应被淘汰。

The question of whether humans may legitimately use animals for their own benefit, or whether any such exploitation is morally wrong, generates heated debate. Some defend animal use as natural and necessary, while others insist that sentient creatures deserve protection from being treated as mere resources.

Those who consider animal use acceptable point to its practical contributions to human wellbeing. Domestic livestock provide food, leather and wool that sustain entire economies, while laboratory animals have been central to the development of vaccines, surgical techniques and modern medicines. Supporters also observe that predator–prey relationships already exist in nature, suggesting that humans are simply continuing an established pattern rather than inventing a new one.

Opponents of exploitation maintain that moral progress demands a very different stance. Centuries of factory farming and cosmetic testing have caused widespread animal suffering, and modern science increasingly reveals how complex the inner lives of mammals, birds and even fish are. These critics argue that animals possess intrinsic value and a right to live free from unnecessary harm, making practices such as whaling for luxury meat or killing for fashion impossible to justify.

In my view, neither extreme is convincing. Society should continue to use animals where genuine human need is at stake, particularly in food production and life-saving research, but every such use must be governed by strict ethical standards, and rapidly improving alternatives should replace animal exploitation whenever it is scientifically feasible.

In conclusion, while animals can reasonably serve human needs under careful regulation, treating them purely as a commodity ignores their capacity to suffer. A path that combines pragmatic use with steady moral progress offers the most compelling way forward.
