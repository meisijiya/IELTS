Task 2 — Gendered job suitability (agree-disagree)

The different qualities of male and female mean they suit different jobs. Do you agree or disagree? (2022.11.5)

立场: 不同意职业按性别划分; 理由1: 个体差异大于性别差异; 理由2: 性别分工强化偏见并浪费人才

Some people argue that men and women possess fundamentally different qualities and therefore naturally fit different kinds of jobs. I firmly disagree with this view, because individual variation within each gender is far greater than any difference between them, and assigning careers by sex wastes talent.

The first reason is that the supposed differences between male and female abilities are far smaller and less consistent than the argument suggests. Psychological research shows that the overlap between the skills of men and women in any field is usually around ninety per cent, with only a handful of traits showing modest average differences. Job performance depends overwhelmingly on personal interest, not on whether a worker was born male or female. A clear example is nursing, a profession once assumed to suit only women but now thriving because of capable male nurses who bring the same compassion and technical skill to hospital wards.

The second reason is that dividing careers by gender locks talented individuals out of work that suits them and weakens the wider economy. When girls are steered away from engineering or boys away from teaching, employers lose access to excellent candidates and professions become unbalanced. Beyond economics, gendered job assignments reinforce the prejudice that one sex is naturally better at certain tasks, an attitude that fuels pay gaps and quiet discrimination in hiring. Were governments and schools to direct young people into occupations purely on the basis of their sex, they would be sacrificing fairness and national productivity for the comfort of a familiar stereotype.

In conclusion, men and women are far more similar in capability than older stereotypes suggest, and careers should be matched to personal talent rather than to assumptions about what each gender ought to do.
