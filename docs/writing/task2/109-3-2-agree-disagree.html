Task 2 — Everyone vegetarian (agree-disagree)

Everyone should be a vegetarian because they do not need to eat meat to have a healthy diet. Do you agree or disagree? (2025.5.10)

立场: 不同意全民素食; 理由1: 健康饮食可包含适量肉类; 理由2: 全民素食忽视文化多样与个人选择

Some people argue that everyone should adopt a vegetarian diet because human beings can remain perfectly healthy without eating meat. I disagree with this position, because a balanced diet that includes moderate amounts of meat has been shown to support good health, and forcing the entire population to give it up would disregard both nutritional science and long-standing cultural traditions.

The first reason is that meat, when eaten in moderation, supplies nutrients that are difficult to obtain in sufficient quantities from plants alone. Lean beef, fish and poultry provide high-quality protein, iron, vitamin B12 and omega-3 fatty acids that many people, particularly children and pregnant women, struggle to absorb from vegetable sources. National dietary surveys in countries such as Japan and Norway repeatedly show that populations who eat small portions of fish or lean meat enjoy longer life expectancy and lower rates of anaemia than those who attempt strict vegetarian regimens without careful supplementation.

The second reason is that compelling everyone to abandon meat would impose a single dietary rule on vastly different cultures, climates and family circumstances. Pastoral communities in Mongolia, Inuit villages in Greenland and barbecue-loving regions of Argentina have built their cuisines and economies around animal husbandry for centuries, and a sweeping ban would erode that heritage while doing little to address the real environmental problems caused by industrial meat production. Were governments to mandate vegetarianism, they would punish traditional herders for the excesses of factory farms, an outcome that no fair-minded policy can defend.

In conclusion, I still believe that no one should be forced to become a vegetarian, because balanced omnivorous diets can be perfectly healthy and because respecting cultural diversity and personal choice must remain a basic principle of any food policy.
