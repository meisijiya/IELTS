Task 2 — Working hard motivation (agree-disagree)

The only reason people work hard is to earn more money. There are no other reasons to do it. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2024.8.31)

立场: 不同意, 金钱之外另有重要动力; 理由1: 内在满足与同行认可是独立动力; 理由2: 家庭义务与公共责任驱动努力

Some commentators claim that earning more money is the only reason people choose to work hard, and that no other motive plays a significant role. While I accept that financial gain is often a strong incentive, I disagree with this absolute view, because other forces exert an equally powerful influence on people's willingness to make an effort.

One such force is the intrinsic satisfaction that comes from doing demanding work well. Many professionals take pride in solving hard problems, mastering a craft or earning recognition from peers, and these rewards exist independently of any salary they might receive. A clear example is the long hours that open-source software developers volunteer, or the time that championship athletes put into gruelling training schedules, even though neither activity generates direct income. Were money their only objective, such people would have abandoned these pursuits long ago and looked for steadier paid employment in other industries long since.

A second force is the desire to look after family members or to serve a broader community. Parents routinely endure long shifts so that their children can attend better schools, while doctors, firefighters and aid workers persist in gruelling roles to mitigate suffering they may never personally benefit from. These motivations are rooted in duty rather than personal reward, and they often sustain effort during long periods when financial incentives alone would have been insufficient to keep someone going through the inevitable difficulties of any career.

In conclusion, I still maintain that money is only one of several reasons to work hard, and that intrinsic fulfilment and a sense of duty are equally important in driving sustained effort.
