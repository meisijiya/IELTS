Task 2 — Tech and crime (discuss-both-views)

Some people think that development of technology helps to reduce crime, while others think that it encourages crime. Discuss both views and give your opinion. (2024.4.20)

立场: 技术是中性的,其利弊取决于使用方式; 理由1: 监控与数据分析显著提升警方预防和破案能力; 理由2: 网络与加密技术催生新型跨境犯罪且更隐蔽

Whether the development of technology helps to reduce crime or actually encourages it is a question that divides opinion. This essay discusses both views and then explains why I believe the answer depends largely on how the technology is applied.

Supporters of the technology-as-deterrent view highlight the powerful impact of surveillance and data analysis on modern policing. Closed-circuit cameras in city centres, automatic number-plate recognition on motorways, and DNA databases have all helped investigators solve serious crimes that might otherwise have remained unsolved. Online platforms now cooperate with law-enforcement agencies to remove terrorist propaganda and child-abuse material more quickly than ever before, suggesting that, used wisely, technology can be a formidable ally in keeping citizens safe.

Critics, however, point out that the same technological advances create powerful new tools for criminals. The internet has enabled large-scale fraud, identity theft, and the rapid spread of illegal pornography, offences that barely existed a generation ago. Encrypted messaging apps make it harder for police to gather evidence, while cryptocurrency allows offenders to move and launder money across borders almost beyond reach. Many victims suffer in silence because online crime is difficult both to detect and to prosecute in any single jurisdiction.

My own view is that technology is essentially neutral, and its effect on crime depends on which side adopts new methods faster. When police and governments keep pace with innovation, the preventive benefits tend to outweigh the new risks; when they lag behind, organised criminals exploit the gap with devastating efficiency.

In conclusion, technology neither eliminates nor inevitably increases crime, and societies that regulate it wisely while equipping their police with the latest tools will continue to enjoy the greatest net benefit.
