Task 2 — News waste of time (agree-disagree)

Some people think that the news has no connection to their lives, so reading newspapers and watching television news programmes is a waste of time. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2026.4.18)

立场: 不同意把新闻视为浪费时间; 理由1: 新闻帮助公民参与社会与理解政策影响; 理由2: 新闻提供判断生活决策所需的全局视野

Some people argue that, because news stories rarely touch their personal concerns, reading newspapers and watching television news programmes is essentially a waste of time. I firmly disagree with this view, because the news performs essential functions for both individual well-being and the health of democratic societies.

The first reason is that news coverage helps ordinary citizens understand and participate in the decisions that shape their daily lives. Reports on tax changes, healthcare reforms and local infrastructure projects may appear remote, yet they directly influence household budgets and the quality of public services that every family depends on. Citizens who keep up with current affairs are also far better placed to vote wisely in elections and to hold their representatives accountable, which in turn strengthens the transparency and accountability that healthy democracies require.

The second reason is that a wider view of the world supports sounder personal decisions. Knowing how a foreign currency is performing helps a small exporter set fair prices; understanding weather and shipping trends helps farmers and retailers plan their seasons; following public-health updates helps families adopt protective habits during outbreaks. A clear example is the way households that paid attention to early energy-price reporting were able to renegotiate contracts before costs peaked, while those who ignored the news faced sudden bill shocks. Without such background, individuals would be forced to make decisions in a fog of their own assumptions and miss opportunities that better-informed neighbours could exploit.

In conclusion, I still believe that keeping up with the news is far from a waste of time, because it equips citizens to participate in public life and makes everyday personal choices better informed.
