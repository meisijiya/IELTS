Task 2 — Stable career path (agree-disagree)

People who decide on a career path early in their lives and keep to it are more likely to have a satisfying working life than those who change jobs frequently. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2021.9.25)

立场: 不同意, 换工作反而更易找到满足感; 理由1: 换岗帮助个人找到与性格匹配的工作; 理由2: 跨行业经历提升韧性与意义感

It is often claimed that workers who commit to one career path from an early age enjoy more satisfying working lives than those who change jobs frequently. While stability certainly has its appeal, I disagree with this view, because job changes can in fact broaden a person's sense of fulfilment in ways that a single career cannot.

The first reason is that changing careers allows workers to align their work with their evolving personalities and talents. Few people finish school knowing exactly what suits them, and those who experiment with several roles are more likely to find a genuine match. A clear example is the growing number of professionals in their thirties who pivot from law or finance into teaching, design or public health, reporting higher day-to-day satisfaction than they felt in their original, higher-paying fields. Without the freedom to switch, such workers would have remained trapped in roles that conflicted with their temperament.

The second reason is that frequent job changes build psychological resilience and adaptability, qualities that deepen long-term satisfaction. Workers who have weathered several industries learn to manage uncertainty, collaborate with diverse colleagues and reinvent their skills, which makes each subsequent role feel more rewarding. By contrast, those who stay in a single track for decades may experience the same routines until curiosity fades and engagement deteriorates. Having moved between sectors themselves, versatile workers are usually better equipped to find meaning in whatever role they take on next.

In conclusion, I still believe that variety, not narrow stability, is more closely linked to a satisfying working life, because career changes allow individuals to discover genuine fit and to keep their professional curiosity alive.
