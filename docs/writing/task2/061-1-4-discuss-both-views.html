Task 2 — Educational qualifications and success (discuss-both-views)

Some people believe educational qualifications always bring success. Others think educational qualifications do not necessarily bring success. Discuss both views and give your own opinions. (2021.2.25)

立场: 学历并非必然带来成功,但仍是重要基础; 理由1: 学历提供技能与社会认可; 理由2: 实际能力、性格与机遇同样关键

There is a common belief that obtaining educational qualifications automatically leads to a successful career, while a competing view holds that such credentials are by no means a guarantee of success. Both arguments touch on important aspects of how professional life actually unfolds, and each deserves close examination.

Those who trust in the power of qualifications point out that formal certificates give graduates a recognised foundation of knowledge and a widely accepted signal to employers. A clear example is the legal and medical professions, where a degree and subsequent licence are not merely helpful but absolutely required for entry. Even in less regulated fields, recruitment officers routinely use qualifications as a quick filter, and candidates without them are often screened out before their actual talents can be demonstrated. For families from disadvantaged backgrounds, qualifications therefore function as a portable passport into better-paid work.

Critics, however, observe that many highly successful entrepreneurs, artists and innovators left school or university without distinction, and that numerous graduates with impressive transcripts struggle to advance once they enter the labour market. They argue that qualities such as creativity, resilience, emotional intelligence and the ability to sell ideas often matter more than any diploma. Furthermore, in fast-changing industries like technology, the specific knowledge that a degree conferred can quickly become outdated, leaving the certificate itself of limited practical value.

Having considered both perspectives, I believe that educational qualifications are a powerful starting point but not a sufficient condition for lasting success. Graduates who combine their credentials with practical skills, adaptability and a willingness to keep learning are, in my view, the ones most likely to turn paper qualifications into genuine achievement.
