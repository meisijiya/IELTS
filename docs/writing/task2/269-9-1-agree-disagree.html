Task 2 — School sports over pros (agree-disagree)

The government should spend money on children taking part in sports and art activities in schools, rather than sponsoring professional sports and art events for the public. To what extent do you agree or disagree? (2025.8.2)

立场: 同意; 理由1: 学校投入惠及更多儿童; 理由2: 早期投入缓解长期社会问题

It is often suggested that government money should fund children's participation in school sports and art activities rather than sponsorship of professional events aimed at the public. While I accept that both forms of spending have value, I largely agree with this view, because investment in young citizens produces longer-lasting social returns and reaches far more households.

The first reason is that school-based programmes build healthy habits and creative skills at an age when character is still forming. When children learn to swim, paint or play an instrument alongside their classmates, they acquire abilities that improve their concentration, teamwork and self-confidence for the rest of their lives. State funding in this area also widens access, because not every family can afford private lessons, and a child from a poor village deserves the same chance to develop as one from a wealthy urban district. In contrast, a professional sports match, though entertaining, reaches only a small audience and benefits few highly paid athletes, leaving the rest of the population as mere spectators.

The second reason is that an early introduction to sport and art helps to mitigate later social problems such as obesity, depression and juvenile crime. Compelling evidence from several European countries shows that pupils who attend regular art and physical-education classes are less likely to drop out of school and more likely to find steady employment later on. By contrast, professional events tend to be concentrated in big cities, leaving rural schools and small towns without any extra resources at all, which deepens the existing gap between regions.

In conclusion, I still believe that public funds should prioritise children over professional performers, because the long-term benefits for society and individual wellbeing far outweigh the short-term glamour of elite competitions.
