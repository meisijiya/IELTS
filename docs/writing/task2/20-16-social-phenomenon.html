Task 2 — Walking less (problem-solution)

Health experts claim that walking is the best exercise. However, people are walking less on a daily basis. What has made this happen and how to deal with it? (2021.3.27)

Although health experts agree that walking is one of the simplest and best forms of exercise, people in many countries are walking far less than they used to. This essay will first look at the reasons for the trend and then suggest some practical solutions.

There are two main causes. The first is the spread of private cars and ride-hailing apps. Short trips that people once made on foot, such as going to a nearby shop or a friend's house, are now often done by car or taxi, because driving feels quicker and more convenient. The second cause is the rise of sedentary leisure. Many adults spend their free time sitting in front of a screen, watching videos, playing games, or scrolling through social media, instead of going out for a walk. Modern office work also keeps people at desks for long hours, which leaves little energy or time for walking afterwards.

Several measures could help reverse this trend. Governments and city planners could design more pedestrian-friendly streets, with wider pavements, safer crossings, and green spaces that invite people to walk. Schools and employers could build short walking breaks into the daily routine, and public health campaigns could remind citizens that even a thirty-minute walk brings real health benefits. On a personal level, people could choose to walk for short journeys instead of driving, and use the stairs rather than the lift whenever possible.

In conclusion, the decline in walking is the result of modern convenience and sedentary habits, but with better urban design and small daily choices, this simple and healthy activity can be brought back into everyday life.
